## Item 1 Business — strategy (VERBATIM)

- Identity: "Core Scientific, Inc. ... designs, builds and operates large-scale, purpose-built data centers that support high-density colocation services and digital asset mining for both our own account and to a lesser extent, third-party customers. Our data centers are optimized for power-intensive, mission-critical computing workloads, with a focus on artificial intelligence ("AI") and other high-performance computing ("HPC") applications."
- Portfolio: "As of December 31, 2025, we owned or leased ten data centers across seven U.S. states, representing approximately 1.4 gigawatts ("GW") of gross utility power capacity, or approximately 920 megawatts ("MW") of total leasable customer power capacity."
- CoreWeave relationship: "in February 2024, entered into long-term contract with CoreWeave, Inc. ("CoreWeave") to deliver 16 MW of infrastructure at our Austin, Texas facility. In June 2024, Core Scientific announced that it had entered another contract with CoreWeave for 200 MW of leased customer power capacity. Through the exercise of several contractual options during 2024 and into early 2025, total leased customer power capacity under the relationship with CoreWeave increased to approximately 590 MW of leased power capacity."
- Total-conversion strategy (VERBATIM): "We intend to convert every megawatt in our portfolio to high-density colocation infrastructure over the next three years, while continuing to digital asset mine during conversion only to meet existing power commitments at facilities where HDC conversion is taking place or to honor a small number of digital asset mining hosting commitments."
- Revenue mix shift: "In 2025, we derived the majority of our revenue from earning digital assets for our own account but expect that a meaningful amount of our revenue will be derived from high-density colocation ("HDC") in 2026, as billable customer power capacity gets delivered to our end customer."
- CoreWeave merger (MD&A "Developments During 2025"): "On July 7, 2025, the Company entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger... with CoreWeave, Inc.... pursuant to which CoreWeave would acquire the Company in an all-stock transaction... On October 30, 2025, the Company terminated the Merger Agreement in accordance with its terms following Company stockholder rejection of the terms of the Merger Agreement at a special meeting of stockholders on that date."
- Customer concentration: "One customer, CoreWeave, currently accounts for 100% of our Colocation segment revenue." (Note 1: "CoreWeave, Inc. ("CoreWeave), an artificial intelligence-focused hyperscaler.")
- Hosted mining wind-down: "We do not expect to further expand our Digital Asset Hosted Mining operations in 2026 and future years and may not derive consequential digital asset hosting revenue in future periods."

## Sites & fleet (Item 1)

- Ten data centers: Alabama (1), Georgia (2), Kentucky (1), North Carolina (1), North Dakota (1), Oklahoma (1), Texas (3) — "totaling 920 MW of total leasable customer power capacity, or 1.4 GW on a gross utility power capacity basis."
- Gross utility power by site (MW): Paducah-Calvert City KY (TVA) 150; Marble NC (Murphy EPB) 35; Marble NC (Duke) 82; Dalton GA (Dalton Utilities) 195; Grand Forks ND (Nodak) 100; Dallas-Denton TX (Denton Municipal Electric) 394; Pecos TX (TNMP) 300; Muskogee OK (OG&E) 100; Austin TX (Austin Energy) 20; Auburn AL (Alabama Power) 50; "Total 1,426" MW gross.
- Fleet: "As of December 31, 2025, we deployed approximately 151,400 bitcoin miners, which number consists of approximately 135,500 self-miners and approximately 15,900 hosted miners, which represented 15.7 exahash per second ("EH/s") and 2.2 EH/s for self-miners and hosted miners, respectively. As of December 31, 2024... approximately 171,100 bitcoin miners... 19.1 EH/s and 1.0 EH/s..." — fleet shrinking by design as sites convert to HDC.
- 3 segments: "Colocation," "Digital Asset Self-Mining," "Digital Asset Hosted Mining."
- Block ASIC deal: "In 2024 we entered into an agreement with Block, Inc. a technology company developing ASICs, to acquire 3 nm ASICs customized for digital asset mining."

## Results of operations (Item 7)

- Revenue table (thousands) FY2025 vs FY2024: "Colocation revenue $65,424 | $24,378"; "Digital asset self-mining revenue $229,207 | $408,740"; "Digital asset hosted mining revenue from customers $24,388 | $77,554"; "Total revenue $319,019 | $510,672".
- MD&A summary: "During 2025, total revenue decreased to $319.0 million from $510.7 million, primarily due to lower digital asset self-mining revenue and digital asset hosted mining revenue as we shifted capital and infrastructure toward colocation, partially offset by higher colocation revenue from incremental billable customer power capacity. Operating loss increased to $245.6 million in 2025 from $142.1 million in 2024. Net loss was $288.6 million in 2025... Adjusted EBITDA decreased to $29.7 million in 2025 from $157.4 million in 2024." (Reconciliation table: "Net loss $(288,616)"; "Adjusted EBITDA $(29,659)".)
- Self-mining metrics table FY2025 vs FY2024: "Bitcoin mined 2,276 | 6,595 (-65%)"; "Average price of bitcoin $101,639 | $65,894 (+54%)"; "Self-mining hash rate 15.7 | 19.1 EH/s (-18%)". Decrease drivers: "reduction in our deployed mining fleet due primarily to our strategic shift to colocation... the Bitcoin network's halving... more challenging network conditions, including higher network difficulty."
- Cost of revenue FY2025: "Cost of colocation services $45,679; Cost of digital asset self-mining $218,868; Cost of digital asset hosted mining services $16,574; Total cost of revenue $281,121; Gross profit $37,898" (thousands).
- Power pass-through: "Power fees are passed through to customers without markup and are recognized as revenue on a gross basis... changes in power prices can cause fluctuations in colocation revenue that are not indicative of changes in our underlying colocation margins."
- Impairment: "Impairment of property, plant and equipment 11,359 | 122,869" — decrease "primarily driven by a lower volume of assets committed to demolition in connection with the conversion of data center facilities from digital asset mining to high-density colocation operations... We expect to incur future impairments of PP&E at the point at which the assets become committed to demolition, which is generally expected to occur at the time of colocation customer contract execution."
- Colocation organizational and site startup costs: "$48,249" FY2025 (15% of revenue).
- SG&A: "$159,224" FY2025 (50% of revenue).
- Merger costs: "Merger Agreement related costs 21,588" (in Adjusted EBITDA reconciliation).
