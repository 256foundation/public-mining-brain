## Key points (ch. 75)

- Input surface: the Braiins profitability calculator requires "almost two dozen different data fields" — "Deriving a precise number of expected mining revenue and profit requires more data inputs than most people realize." (p. 75)
- Timeframe: set the model "between 6 and 60 months"; "Bitcoin incentives long-term planning... Focusing on longer time periods is a more common strategy". (p. 76)
- Power units: "Miners usually measure their power consumption in watts (W) per hour (W/h) or kilowatt hours (kWh). One kW equals 1,000 W." (pp. 76–77)
- Electricity price: "electricity prices can vary significantly across different geographic regions... unless a miner secures a power purchase agreement with future power price predictability." (p. 77)
- Block subsidy: "This number changes after each halving event, which takes place once every four calendar years, approximately." (p. 78)
- Transaction fees: "Transaction fees for spends included in the block are also paid. Like price and hashrate, transaction fees paid per block vary significantly over time"; reference: "average fees per block for the past 2016 blocks (14 days)". (p. 79)
- Difficulty cadence: "Each year difficulty changes approximately 24 times (twice per month)... the average increase of mining difficulty over the past 5 years is 6% monthly, which equates to roughly 100% per year." (pp. 79–80)
- Difficulty units: "Difficulty is measured in arbitrary 'difficulty units,' meaning the number is relative." (p. 79)
- CapEx categories: "Buying ASICs and PSUs; Constructing, remodeling, or maintaining a mining facility; Cooling equipment (e.g., HVAC or immersion tanks); Compliance costs." (p. 81)
- OpEx (excluding electricity, which has its own field): "Salaries; Site Security; Insurance; Taxes and legal fees." (pp. 81–82)
- HODL ratio: "represents how much of the newly mined bitcoins a miner plans to hold... a miner that does not sell any bitcoins has a HODL ratio of 100%. A miner that sells most of their bitcoins could have a HODL ratio of 25%." (pp. 82–83)
- Discount rate: "an interest rate used to determine the present value of future cash flows... the calculator's backend calculates Net Present Value (NPV) and includes this value in the CSV file download." (p. 83)
- Depreciation: "appropriately depreciating hardware over time – especially as newer and more efficient models enter the market – is an essential calculation for any mining operation, regardless of size." (p. 82)
- Closing verbatim: "There's no fixed cost for mining bitcoin. The real cost for each miner depends on over a dozen variable data points." (p. 83)
