## Key points (ch. 25)

- Miners have exactly 3 roles: "1. Confirm transactions 2. Secure the blockchain 3. Participate in the fair distribution of new bitcoins." (p. 29)
- Nodes have 3 roles: "1. Validate transactions 2. Keep a historic record of transactions 3. Dictate and enforce the rules of the network." (p. 30)
- Supply progress: "Currently, about 18.6 million bitcoin have already been distributed to miners through the block subsidy, and this will continue until all 21 million bitcoins are distributed around the year 2140." (pp. 29–30)
- Post-2140: "miners will solely earn transaction fees for confirming transactions and securing the network." (p. 30)
- Power balance: "miners must play by the rules enforced by nodes in order to be rewarded with bitcoins. Nodes, on the other hand, are the true rulers of the network." (p. 30)
- Node cost: "running a bitcoin node is not very costly (it's typically in the $150-$400 range)"; a popular approach is to buy "$150-$200 worth of components and run a dedicated node on a raspberry pi." (pp. 30–31)
- Checks and balances: "No one miner or node can control the network. They each put checks and balances on each other to ensure no one cheats the system." (p. 31)
- Transaction flow: a transaction "is propagated through the network via gossip protocol"; nodes "ensure that the sender of a transaction is not spending the same BTC twice and didn't create it out of thin air"; miners "batch pending transactions into... blocks". (pp. 26–27)
- Revenue structure: "Every transaction includes a transaction fee and every block contains a subsidy of newly issued bitcoins, both of which are paid to whichever miner adds the given block of transactions to the blockchain." (p. 29)
- Risk profile: "miners can lose millions of dollars overnight due to extreme weather, floods, fires, and more" — mining "requires significant expertise and entails a lot of risk (unlike operating a node)". (p. 29)
- Because miners "must compete and spend resources to earn newly issued coins, bitcoin is more similar to gold and other commodities than to fiat currencies with unlimited supplies." (p. 29)
- Permissionlessness: "Anybody can become a node operator or miner without needing permission from anybody else." (p. 31)
