## Key points (ch. 8)

- Four economic variables: "There are four key economic variables to a hashrate-powered heating system upgrade: Your current heating operational costs, the upfront cost of upgrading to bitcoin-powered heat, the new heating system operational costs, and the bitcoin subsidy that the hashrate heating system will provide." (p. 128)
- Original OPEX formula: "OPEXoriginal = EnergyQuantity, Heat * Unit CostHeat Energy" — three inputs: "The quantity of energy you use (kWh, BTU, MCF, Gallon, etc.)", "The unit cost of that energy ($/kWh, $/BTU, $/MCF, $/Gallon, etc.)", and "The relationship between heat usage and external factors like weather or business operations." (pp. 128–129)
- Sizing rule of thumb: "A good rule of thumb is to use one miner per 1,000 sq/ft or 1,000 gallons as a general guideline, though factors like insulation, water temperature, and miner output temperature may affect this." (p. 129)
- Thermo-economic methods (footnote): "Heating Degree Days (HDD), Duty Cycle & Load Analysis, Thermal Modeling, and Energy Metering." (p. 129)
- Worked example — original heating: "On average, 4.34 million BTUs of heat are required throughout January to keep a building at 65°F, based on the historical average ambient temperature over the past 20 years" — "4.34 M BTU equates to roughly 51.6 gallons of propane, assuming a 92% heating efficiency (AFUE...)" — "OPEXOriginal = 51.6 gallons * $2.85/gallon = $147.06 (spent for heat on average in January)". (pp. 130–131)
- CAPEX formula: "CAPEX = CostMiners + CostAuxiliary Hardware + CostLabor" — "we want to size the system to use the minimum amount of bitcoin miners needed to sustain our heating needs." (p. 131)
- CAPEX example: "CAPEX = S19k Pro + Immersion Tank, Oil, Heat Exchanger + Labor... CAPEX = $1000 + $1000 + $1000 = $3000 (for the system upgrade)". (p. 132)
- Upgrade OPEX formula: "OPEXUpgrade = EnergyQuantity, Heat * Unit CostElectricity" — worked: "converting 4.34 M BTU to kilowatt-hours (kWh), we get 1271.4 kWh of energy... OPEXUpgrade = 1271.4 kWh * $0.13/kWh = $165.28 (estimated for average January)". (pp. 132–133)
- Head-to-head before subsidy: "the new electric heating system is slightly more expensive to operate ($165.28 vs $147.06 for an average January) than the original propane heating system. However, they're quite close – and we haven't even factored in the bitcoin mining rewards yet." (p. 134)
- Duty cycle (energy) formula: "Duty CycleAverage, Energy = EnergyMiner used for heat / Max EnergyMiner at full power" and "HashrateHeat, Average = (Σ MinersHashrate) * Duty CycleAverage, Energy". (p. 134)
- Miner sizing check: "1 miner running at full power of 2.76 kW could generate 2053.4 kWh of total heat energy over the month... 2.76 kW * 744 hours in January = 2053.4 kWh... 2053.4 kWh = 7 M BTU > 4.34 M BTU". (p. 134)
- Duty cycle worked: "Duty CycleAverage, Energy = 1271.4 kWh / 2053 kWh = 0.62 = 62%"; "The S19k Pro has a total hashrate of 120 TH/s at full power... the estimated average hashrate for heating is 74.4 TH/s... HashrateHeat, Average = 120 TH/s * 62% = 74.4 TH/s." (p. 135)
- Hashvalue vs hashprice: "1. Hashvalue: This measures revenue in bitcoin terms (BTC/TH/s/day) 2. Hashprice: This measures revenue in fiat terms ($/TH/s/day)". (p. 135)
- Hashprice inputs: "Bitcoin Block Subsidy (Decreases 50% after each halving) / Bitcoin Block Transaction Fees (Based on network traffic) / Bitcoin Network Difficulty (Competition from other miners) / Bitcoin Price (Current value in fiat terms)." (p. 136)
- Assumption: "we will use a moderately conservative bitcoin hashprice of 0.06 $/TH/s/day - taken a few months after the fourth halving, but before the bull market is in full swing." (p. 136)
- Subsidy formula and worked result: "BTCSubsidy, FIAT = HashrateHeat, Average * DaysHeating * Hashprice... BTCSubsidy, FIAT = 74.4 TH/s * 31 days * $0.06/TH/s/day = $138.38". (p. 136)
- January ledger (As-Of example): OPEX Original (Propane) $147.06; OPEX Upgrade (Electric) $165.28; BTC Subsidy, FIAT $138.38; "Heat, NET Cost... $26.90 — Average NET January Heating Cost after Bitcoin Subsidy"; "Savings... $120.16 (82%) — Average January Heating Cost Savings Compared to Propane System". (p. 137)
- Two ROI methods: "ROINET =CAPEX / Σ Year SavingsHeat... we focus on the net savings – how much you pocket after selling mined sats to help pay your heating bill"; "ROIGROSS =CAPEX / Σ Year BTCSubsidy, FIAT... you'd pay the regular heating bill and stack all the bitcoin mined as extra income." (pp. 137–138)
- Worked ROI: "$550.86 saved over the year if the sats were immediately put towards heating expenses... a total bitcoin revenue of $635.09, assuming a constant hashprice" — "ROINET =$3000 / $550.86/yr =5.4 years"; "ROIGROSS =$3000 / $635.09/yr =4.7 years". (pp. 138–139)
- Model conservatism: "this high-level, averaged approach is conservative, practical, and good enough for decision-making... an accessible and appropriate method for small-to-medium businesses and residential users, or for anyone looking for a quick first-pass analysis." (p. 139)
- Hashprice conservatism: "The model conservatively uses a near-historic low hash price, a few months after the halving event... the model excludes potential increases in transaction fees, which will likely grow as the block subsidy continues to halve"; "it's easier for bitcoin's price to rise faster than sufficient hashrate is added to suppress hashprice growth." (p. 140)
- Weather buffer: "if miners are predicted to run at 62% capacity in January, the remaining 38% capacity acts as a buffer for colder days"; "the model uses data from one of the coldest months over a 20-year period"; optional "margin-of-safety and increase heat energy estimates by 10-50%"; "keeping a propane system as backup ensures there's no risk of under-heating." (p. 141)
- New builds vs upgrades: "For an upgrade to a functional, newer system, ROI must be compelling... If the ROI stretches beyond a decade or the savings are minimal, the upfront cost likely isn't worth it. For new builds or replacing outdated or broken systems, the calculus changes... a hashrate heating system introduces mining revenue to offset heating expenses." (pp. 141–142)
- Longevity: halvings plus "rising network difficulty - averaging a 60% annual increase" shrink bitcoin-nominated rewards, but "As nominal bitcoin rewards decrease, their fiat value may rise"; "Hashrate heating systems also outlast traditional mining setups since heat value reduces reliance on miner efficiency... future upgrades of the 'miner heating element' can rejuvenate your system." (p. 142)
- Opportunity cost: "For hardcore bitcoiners, the opportunity cost of buying bitcoin directly is worth considering... potentially making direct purchases more profitable than investing in a heating system upgrade if timed appropriately." (p. 143)
- Beyond economics: "You might also be motivated by bitcoin itself, willing to invest extra to add hashing power, secure the network, and stack non-KYC sats"; "Environmental benefits are another draw. Hashrate heating systems are fully electric and can reduce local carbon emissions." (pp. 143–144)
- Section flow: UNDERSTANDING KEY ECONOMIC VARIABLES (Current Operational Expenses / Upgrade Capital Expenses / Upgrade Operational Expenses / Upgrade Bitcoin Subsidy) → CALCULATING ROI → Conservatism, Hashprice Variability, and Weather (Conservatism / Hashprice / External Variables and Weather Fluctuation) → NEW BUILDS OR REPLACING BROKEN SYSTEMS → MAKING THE UPGRADE LAST → OPPORTUNITY COST – MINE FOR HEAT OR BUY SATS INSTEAD? → MY ECONOMICS AREN'T GREAT, BUT I WANT TO DO IT ANYWAY (pp. 128–144)
