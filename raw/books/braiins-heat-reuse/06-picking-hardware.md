## Key points (ch. 6)

- ASIC anatomy: "Any modern ASIC miner has four main hardware components... the power supply unit (PSU), control board, hashboard, and cooling system." (p. 93)
- Heat balance law: "Every watt of power that a miner uses needs to be dissipated as heat." Example: "if a bitcoin miner draws 3000 watts of power but only dissipates 2000 watts as heat, it retains 1000 watts of energy. This retained heat energy will cause the miner to heat up, potentially leading to hardware damage." (p. 94)
- Three cooling methods: "Air cooling: Fans or airflow are used to pull heat away from the machine"; "Immersion cooling: The miner is fully submerged in a special oil that absorbs the heat"; "Hydro cooling: Water or coolant mix flows directly over the ASIC chips to remove heat." (p. 95)
- Established infrastructure: heat-transfer hardware is "already well-established, thanks to over a century of innovation in the heating industry." (p. 95)
- Power density: "Bitcoin miners are some of the most power-dense electrical devices on the planet." (p. 96)
- Air-cooled mechanics: cool air moves "over special metal fins called heat sinks, which are attached to the ASIC chips"; "Heat transfers conductively from the ASIC chips to the heat sinks, where cool air is blown over them to push the heat out of the device." (Footnotes: convection = "the transfer of heat through the movement of fluids (liquids or gases) over a solid surface"; conduction = "the transfer of heat through direct contact between solids.") (p. 96)
- Immersion mechanics: "a non-conductive, dielectric oil is used. This oil doesn't conduct electricity, so it won't short-circuit the device"; "You can take an air-cooled ASIC miner, remove its fans (since pumps now move the oil instead of fans moving air), and submerge it in the tank"; "cool immersion oil is pumped in from the bottom and flows up through the miner's heat sinks, absorbing heat via convection." (p. 97)
- Liquid advantage: "Liquids can hold much more heat energy than gasses like air. This is a better fit for higher-power miners, and is far more efficient when you need to move lots of heat over long distances or direct it to specific uses, like heating a pool or a hot water tank." (p. 98)
- Hydro mechanics: "Hydro-cooling involves pumping water directly onto the ASIC chips themselves. Because unpurified water contains minerals, it is electrically conductive. Submerging the entire miner into a tank of water would short-circuit the electrical components." The "water block" directs fluid "onto the ASIC chips... designed with seals to prevent leaks"; water "sometimes mixed with antifreeze coolant like ethylene glycol to prevent freezing." (pp. 98–99)
- No invention needed: "No new inventions are needed to unlock the potential of bitcoin heat reuse. The technology is already here, and the hardware that makes heat reuse possible is the same equipment used by bitcoin mining operations that currently discard the heat as waste!" (p. 100)
- Noise fix for air-cooled heaters: "A common modification is to replace these fans with larger-diameter ones. Larger fans can push the same amount of air at lower speeds, resulting in good airflow and reduced noise." (p. 100)
- Ducting interface: "Shroud adapters are key here. These parts act as connectors, linking the inlet and outlet of hashrate heaters to air ducts (since the miner's fans aren't the same size as standard ducting)." — "Inline fans, which are installed directly inside the HVAC ducting, can be added to help boost air pressure." (p. 101)
- Oil downsides: "the non-conductive oil has a few downsides: it's expensive, prone to leaks, and difficult to clean up... buying large quantities to transport heat over long distances isn't practical." (p. 102)
- Dual-loop isolation: "it's better to isolate the miner cooling loop from the heating liquid loop used for heat reuse. Using this dual-loop system also improves reliability and makes maintenance easier." The enabler: "a liquid-to-liquid heat exchanger... transfers heat from one liquid to another without mixing them." (pp. 102–103)
- Hydronics: "This type of system is well-established in an industry called hydronics. Hydronic systems are commonly used in residential, commercial, and industrial heating setups." (p. 103)
- Expansion tank safety: "liquids, unlike gasses, are incompressible... When heated, liquids expand, and in a closed-loop (air-tight) system like a hydronic loop, this expansion has no place to go... the rising pressure from the heated fluid could cause the system to explode" — the expansion tank "provides a pocket of space where the liquid can safely expand as it heats up"; "this isn't typically needed for the cooling oil side of an immersion system, since the immersion tanks are open to the air." (p. 103)
- Air control hardware: "duct splits, like Y-ducts and manifolds"; "Dampers are installed within the duct network and have adjustable blades that allow you to throttle airflow or completely shut it off"; "backdraft dampers... are essentially one-way valves for air." (p. 104)
- Liquid control hardware: "In hydronic heating systems, valves are the primary method of controlling the flow of liquid, functioning similarly to dampers in air systems"; "Mixing valves have the unique ability to blend hot and cold water to reach a specific output temperature... no software or electronics required"; "a hydronic manifold... split[s] liquid flow into separate paths"; check valves prevent backflow; "pressure relief valves are essential for protecting the system from overpressure." (p. 105)
- Monitoring hardware: "Switches are binary devices, often analog... Sensors, on the other hand, are typically digital and provide continuous, real-time data about variables like pressure, temperature, and flow rate"; "A flow meter, for example, might detect slowed flow rate - which could indicate a clog in the loop and trigger a pump shut-down to prevent damage." (pp. 106–107)
- Relays vs microcontrollers: "A relay is a simple electrical switch that controls mechanical devices like fans, pumps, or valves by turning them on or off. They can operate without any digital signals"; "In an analog logic circuit, this simple on/off mechanism can define decisions without a computer"; "Microcontrollers, like the Raspberry Pi, read data from digital sensors... a microcontroller could lower the fan speed incrementally or slowly adjust the flow of heated water through a valve." (pp. 107–108)
- User-facing control: "smart (wi-fi) thermostats work in tandem with microcontrollers"; "smart PDUs (Power Distribution Units), which provide full remote control over the power supplied to the hashrate heating devices themselves. This allows users to completely cut off power when heating isn't needed, reducing wasted energy and adding an extra layer of safety." (pp. 108–109)
- Air delivery/rejection: "vents, which channel warm air into specific rooms"; "air diffusers mix heated air with the surrounding environment"; "louvers can regulate airflow at the end of ducts"; "exhaust fans... forcefully pull heated air out of the end of the system." (p. 110)
- Liquid delivery/rejection: heat exchangers transfer "energy between the two liquids... This separation is crucial for safety and system separation"; "Hydronic tubing can also deliver heat directly, as seen in underfloor heating, baseboard systems, or even outdoor heated sidewalks"; rejection "to thermal sinks like streams, lakes, or oceans"; "Radiators... convert liquid heat to air heat (think heat sink, but extra large)"; "dry coolers (essentially large radiators with fans on them)." (pp. 110–111)
- Rejection only with excess: "in most hashrate heating systems - where mining operations are tied directly to heat demand - there should be no excess heat that needs to be rejected." (p. 111)
- Example system diagrams: air-based (intake/exhaust shrouds, inline fans, Y-duct manifold, duct damper, back draft blocker, vent with difuser, WiFi thermostat, WiFi PDU, microcontroller control panel) and liquid-based (immersion tank with ASIC/Hydro miner, cooling loop pump, liquid to liquid HX, water tank HX, electronic valves, check valves, hydronic manifold, house/garage radiators, heating loop pump, dry cooler). (pp. 111–113)
- Air's physics limits: "Air is less effective than liquids for transferring heat over large distances or with small temperature gradients... its low density and specific heat capacity (how much heat energy it holds) mean it carries less heat energy per volume or mass... However, its high diffusion (mixing) efficiency helps distribute heat evenly, making air-cooled miners ideal for warming rooms or large zones." (p. 114)
- Immersion strengths: "Immersion cooling submerges miners in dielectric oil, insulating hardware from dust and debris while leveraging the oil's high specific heat capacity and thermal mass to absorb and store heat. This helps stabilize temperatures, reducing wear and extending hardware lifespan. Thanks to full hardware submersion, immersion-cooling captures all heat from ASIC chips, power supplies, and control boards, making it ideal for applications where every watt of heat is valuable." (pp. 114–115)
- Immersion weaknesses: "Oil's high viscosity (thickness) makes it less efficient for pumping and transferring heat over long distances compared to water. Maintenance can also be cumbersome... immersion systems are notorious for eroding thermal paste between ASIC chips and heat sinks. Another major challenge for immersion hashrate heating is that most hydronic hardware - like pumps, valves, manifolds, and expansion tanks - is incompatible with dielectric oil." (p. 115)
- Hydro strengths: "Using water to directly target ASIC chips with hydro-cooling is more efficient at heat removal than immersion-cooling because there are no heat sinks in the way. Water's higher density, lower viscosity, and superior thermal conductivity allow it to transport more heat energy per unit volume and mass." — "the added complexity of tubing, seals, and water blocks add more potential points of failure... However, hydro cooling doesn't capture heat from power supplies and control boards, focusing instead on chip-level cooling." (p. 115)
- The verdict: "Immersion cooling systems are better suited for heat reuse setups that prioritize long-term reliability, temperature stability, full hardware encapsulation, and comprehensive heat capture. Hydro cooling, on the other hand, is better suited for heat reuse setups that prioritize more efficient heat transfer, higher heating fluid temperatures, more precise ASIC chip temperature control or direct compatibility with more hydronic hardware." (pp. 115–116)
- Component sizing discipline: "building a bitcoin-powered heating system isn't as simple as snapping together Legos... Each part needs to work in harmony to ensure your miners continuously generate both BTC and BTU without bottlenecks or inefficiencies." (p. 116)
- Unit conversion: "Most bitcoin miners measure power and energy consumption in kW and kWh respectively, while heating infrastructure measures energy in BTU and heating power in BTU/hr... Quick conversion: 1 kW of power consumption equals roughly 3,412 BTU/hr of heat output." (p. 116)
- Furnace-rating caveat: "A furnace may be rated for high BTU/hr, but is designed to run intermittently, commonly delivering only a fraction of the total heating power it's capable of." (p. 117)
- ASHRAE design conditions: "ASHRAE (American Society of Heating, Refrigerating, and Air-Conditioning Engineers) guidelines help by providing industry standards based on historical climate data... size components to handle your worst-case design conditions; the coldest day when miners are running at full power to meet heating needs, and the hottest day when heat will transfer the least efficiently." (p. 117)
- Heat exchanger sizing: "Heat exchanger size is determined by the flow rates and the temperature difference between the two loops. A larger temperature difference allows for more efficient heat transfer." (pp. 117–118)
- Pump sizing: "Pumps operate along a 'pump curve,' which shows the balance between flow rate and pressure head... An undersized pump won't move liquid fast enough for effective heat transfer, while an oversized pump can cause excessive pressure, waste energy, or damage components." (p. 118)
- Airflow sizing: "fan sizing ensures the airflow (measured in cubic feet per minute, or CFM) matches the ductwork and heat distribution needs." (pp. 118–119)
- Safety/maintenance: "Dust management is crucial for air-cooled systems, where intake filters keep debris from damaging components and exhaust filters ensure clean indoor air"; "For liquid-cooled systems, leak prevention is key. Pressure test and inspect pipes, fittings, and tanks after installation. For immersion setups, containment is critical to avoid spills, given the high cost and messiness of immersion oil." (p. 119)
- Expert reassurance: "all of these hardware tools have existed outside of bitcoin mining for decades. The only new variables added are the bitcoin miners. People know how this stuff works – so it's worth working with professionals." (p. 119)
