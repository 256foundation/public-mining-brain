## Key points (ch. 4)

- Space heaters as origin: "The hashrate heating movement began with bitcoin plebs retrofitting older mining hardware for space heating purposes. Today, companies have emerged that offer DIY solutions as well as fully integrated bitcoin space heater products." (p. 55)
- Second life for old ASICs: "some machines find a second life, repurposed as space heaters, since efficiency isn't as important when you also value the heat." (p. 56)
- Common DIY modifications: "adding WiFi, fine-tuning power, replacing loud fans, tuning temperature limits, modifying the power supply, creating an appealing enclosure, and more." (p. 56)
- CryptoCloaks: "kits and tutorials to help users modify Bitmain Antminer S9 miners into space heaters, complete with custom 3D-printed enclosures"; Altair Bitcoin Mining Solutions and D-Central Technologies offer retrofitted heaters. (p. 57)
- Pivotal Pleb Tech Loki Kit: "connects to the control board of certain Bitmain ASIC miners, allowing them to run with different power supplies on standard 120V outlets, transforming new-generation miners into practical home space heaters." (p. 57)
- Unbound Networks: "reuses industrial miner hashboards, pre-modifying the machines for easy home use and quiet operation." (p. 57)
- Heatbit: "hashrate space-heaters designed from the ground up for room heating and air purification... vertically integrated at the chip level with a custom hashboard and remote management app" — "the first hashrate heater that is UL safety certified." (p. 58)
- Dedicated appliances thesis: "this is the first step into the next phase of hashrate heating, where companies design dedicated heating appliances and infrastructure powered by bitcoin mining, free from the limitations and challenges described before with repurposed industrial mining equipment." (p. 58)
- Pools/hot tubs fit: "they often require heating for extended periods throughout the year. They don't get extremely hot, and typically have their own isolated plumbing and heating systems. Furthermore, many pools and hot tubs are already heated with resistive heat, stacking no sats for the same operating cost." (p. 59)
- Liquid conversion for pools: "converting air-cooled miners to liquid cooling through immersion tanks or using hydro miners ensures the hashrate heat can be captured and delivered to the pool or hot tub effectively." (Footnote: "Immersion involves submerging the hardware in a specialized non-conductive liquid to extract heat, while hydro-cooling circulates cool water through the miners, directly onto ASIC chips.") (pp. 59–60)
- Nakamoto Heating Solutions Hashtub — Michael Schmid (Schnitzel on X): "a fully integrated and smart hot tub heating system powered by hashrate... a Raspberry Pi microcomputer for seamless integration with Home Assistant"; "With only one miner, this system can bring a hot tub to operating temperature in just a few hours." (p. 60)
- Constellation Heating Star Heater (Canada): "designed specifically for heated pools and radiant flooring"; unlike burst heating it "operates with a constant, adjustable heat load... modulates the miner's power levels to better sustain the desired pool temperature." (p. 61)
- Integrated home heating routes: "This is typically achieved one of two ways: forced-air furnaces for air-based heat, or hydronic boiler systems that send liquid heat to radiators and other applications." (p. 62)
- Bob's House (Home Bitcoin Immersion Mining, HBIM YouTube): "using bitcoin mining to power nearly every heating application in his home, including domestic hot water for taps and showers, his furnace for forced-air heating, radiators in the garage for a cozy workshop, and even his clothes dryer" — "the best-documented and most thorough resource available" for home immersion heating. (p. 63)
- 256 Heat — Toine: custom air-cooled hashrate heating "for both home and business use, without the need for cooling oil or water"; trade-offs "such as noise levels, more limited heating capabilities, and lower mining power"; mitigations include "a smart Wi-Fi PDU for full remote power control... as well as quiet fans and filters" plus careful ducts and dampers. (p. 63)
- RY3T (Europe/Switzerland): "a bitcoin-powered boiler specifically designed for residential use" for hydronic systems — "cold water goes in, then bitcoin and hot water come out." (p. 64)
- Hestia Heating (Canada): "a forced-air furnace designed specifically to support certain industrial bitcoin miner models. Built with an optimized enclosure, airflow, fan speed, filtration, and power control panel." (p. 65)
- Business heat synergy: "breweries, distilleries, car washes, bathhouses, agricultural operations, and more. These use cases are ideal candidates for reducing operating costs while supporting the bitcoin network." (p. 66)
- Brewing/distillation fit: heat for "mashing, boiling, sparging, and pasteurization, most of which fall within the temperature range that bitcoin miners can easily generate" — high-duty cycles suited to "substantial cost savings and fast ROIs." (pp. 66–67)
- MintGreen (Canada): "hashrate heating solutions for both Shelter Point Distillery and Container Brewing, and have recently released a bitcoin-powered 'digital boiler' for business and industrial applications." (p. 67)
- Bitcoin Bloem (Netherlands): "a tulip farming company in the Netherlands, adopted hashrate heating by repurposing bitcoin miners to warm greenhouses" — reduced reliance on natural gas. (p. 68)
- TC Car, RV, and Truck Wash (Challis, Idaho): "integrated industrial bitcoin miners into its water heating system using immersion tanks from Fog Hashing", designed by Cade Peterson, "hashrate heating engineer and founder of Softwarm LLC" — Softwarm also heated "construction sites, concrete mixing facilities, the Clayton Silver Bar and even the US Post Office in Clayton, Idaho - population: 7." (p. 68)
- Bathhouse NYC: "has replaced its pool and hot tub heating with bitcoin-powered solutions" after previously relying on "electric resistive heating, which consumes the same amount of energy as bitcoin miners but generates no revenue." (pp. 68–69)
- Fog Hashing product range: "immersion oil tanks range from compact systems that can hold one or two industrial miners, suitable for home use, to expansive 24-miner tanks" — plus "industrial heat transfer solutions that are built for hydro-cooled bitcoin miners"; modular and linkable. (p. 70)
- HeatCore: "exclusively specializes in hydro-cooled hashrate heating solutions for commercial and industrial applications... modular and can be inter-connected to accommodate various heating demands." (pp. 70–71)
- District heating: "a centralized system where heat is generated at a plant and distributed via insulated pipes to multiple buildings"; "Hashrate heating systems can be scaled up (by adding more miners) to meet the high demand of district heating applications." (p. 71)
- MARA in Finland: "Mega-miner Marathon Digital Holdings launched a 2 megawatt hashrate heating pilot project in Finland. The project uses excess heat from bitcoin mining to warm a community of 11,000 residents." (pp. 71–72)
- Watchlist: "Community Pools, Fish Farms and Desalination Plants: Facilities requiring large volumes of low-grade heated water... are prime candidates for hashrate heating." (pp. 72–73)
- Thermodynamic cycles ahead: "the heat energy can be used in special industrial cycles to spin a turbine, power a generator, or even convert the heat energy into a cooling load for air conditioning!" — "organic rankine cycles (convert heat into electricity), absorption chillers (heat-powered air conditioning), or preheating for other industrial processes." (p. 73)
