## Key points (ch. 62, by Karim Helmy; originally written November 2020)

- Old-methodology flaw: metrics like "Miners Rolling Inventory" were "actually showing in 99% of cases... the inflows and outflows from mining pool wallets, not miners themselves." (p. 62)
- Better methodology: Coin Metrics measures "true miner transaction flows by looking at what happens to coins once they're 1-hop away from the original coinbase transaction." (p. 62)
- Macro trend: "their influence on the network is gradually declining, with net outflows becoming less volatile over time" as "several halvings have now reduced issuance dramatically". (p. 63)
- Accumulation signal: "miners accumulated over 380,000 BTC in the year leading up to the halving. This buildup was mostly confined to 1-hop addresses, so previous methodologies would've missed it." (p. 63)
- Why miners sell (verbatim): "miners sell to cover costs and take profits. Miners' expenses, including electricity and rent, are mostly fiat-denominated, but their revenues are earned in bitcoin." (p. 64)
- Squeeze model: "a 36-month cash flow analysis for a hypothetical Bitcoin mining operation with 1000 TH/s of hashrate, 30 J/TH efficiency, and paying an electricity fee of $0.03/kWh. With difficulty rising (+80%/year) faster than price (+20%/year)... the miner would need to be selling a larger portion of their BTC mined each month to cover costs." (p. 64)
- Bull-market exception: "during intense bull markets... network difficulty rises less quickly than price and miners are able to cover costs with a smaller portion of the coins they mine each month"; though some "may actually sell more coins at these times to realize the gains on coins mined and held through the bear market." (p. 65)
- Hedging adoption: "Surveys such as one conducted in the 3rd Global Cryptoasset Benchmarking Study by the University of Cambridge Centre for Alternative Finance showed that most miners don't employ advanced hedging strategies... even then, selling coins will continue to be how miners realize their profits." (p. 65)
- Supply hop accounting: the "0 hops (Pools)" bucket includes "approximately ~1 million coins mined by Satoshi and never moved"; "there are only about ~800k other BTC remaining in that category, most of which were also mined in the early days and many of which may be lost forever." (p. 67)
