## Key points (ch. 14)

- Hashprice definition (verbatim): "hashprice, which represents the $/TH/s/day that a miner earns, i.e. the daily amount of value that a miner will generate for every terahash/second of hashrate that they control." (p. 14)
- Hashvalue definition: "the BTC-denominated version of hashprice (BTC/TH/s/day) is in fact called hashvalue on Braiins Insights"; hashprice = hashvalue multiplied by BTC price. (pp. 14–15)
- Volatility asymmetry: "hashvalue is far less volatile... impacted only by changes in network difficulty and the block reward value... Meanwhile, hashprice is much more volatile." (p. 15)
- May 2020: "the steepest drop for both hashprice and hashvalue... a result of the 3rd halving that took place at block height 630,000 and cut bitcoin's block subsidy from 12.5 BTC per block to 6.25 BTC." (p. 15)
- China ban effect: "network difficulty dropped from then all-time highs of just over 25 trillion to a low under 14 trillion, sending hashvalue up by nearly 50% in the process." (p. 16)
- Same episode: "bitcoin price dropped from highs just over $60k to the low $30k range before the massive drop in hashrate took place... even as hashvalue practically doubled in a matter of weeks, hashprice merely recovered to where it had been before the latest 50% price drop." (pp. 16–17)
- Fee context: transaction fees per block "have been 1-2% of the total block reward for the majority of the time since July 2022." (p. 17)
- Difficulty growth: "the average annual change in network difficulty since the May 2020 halving is around 50%/year." (p. 18)
- Historical obsolescence: ASICs hit the market in 2013 with efficiency gains "so rapid and so large that most machines were made obsolete in well under 1 year"; "with the launch of the Antminer S9 in late 2016, things started to slow down a bit and hardware lifespans extended into multiple halving epochs." (p. 18)
- Growth in absolute terms: "an average of approximately 6 EH/s of hashrate came online per month in 2022, and an average of nearly 10 EH/s of hashrate has come online per month from the lows of July 2021 until the highs at the time of writing in early 2023." (p. 19)
- Power translation: for "the most popular hardware model online today, the Antminer S19j Pro... 6 EH/s/month of hashrate equals around 200 MW of new power capacity every month"; with network hashrate "exceeding 300 EH/s (as of February 2023)" the same monthly rate needs "around 2.4 GW of new power capacity for miners for the year" and yields "about a 25% increase in total network hashrate." (p. 19)
- Efficiency ceiling: "If we assume that all new hashrate will be from the most efficient hardware on the market today, Antminer S19 XP or equivalent 21.5 J/TH efficiency, it would still take about 3.5 GW of new power capacity just to increase network difficulty by 50% from here (40T -> 80T)." (p. 20)
- Planning baseline (verbatim): "50%/year seems like a good guess for difficulty, with lows around 20%/year and highs of 80%/year." Also expect "some hashrate drop off right after the halving unless price has recently done a 2x" (the "Halving Difficulty Drop" calculator input). (p. 20)
- Price-cycle method: BTC broke $20k ATH in December 2020, "3 years after it first set the high and 7 months after the halving"; the $69k ATH came in November 2021; from $23k with 19 months until November 2024, "we could conservatively guess 24 months to reach new all-time highs and put a 100%/year price increment. In this case, I'd consider 50%/year highly conservative and 150%/year as an attainable upper bound on bullishness." (p. 21)
- Bear-market planning: "if there has been a new all-time high BTC price set in the last ~6 months, it's probably advisable to plan for a 2-3 year painful bear market... plan for a repeat of 2022 with all-time low hashprice the next time we're in a bull market like 2021." (p. 22)
- Price–difficulty alignment: "if I model a bullish scenario with 150%/year average annual price growth, I should also assume difficulty will grow as fast as physically possible, which is maybe 80%/year"; a bearish price scenario "should mean slower difficulty growth" unless industry cost to mine is far below price. (p. 22)
