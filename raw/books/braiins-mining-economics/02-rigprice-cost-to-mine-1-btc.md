## Key points (ch. 23)

- Rigprice definition (verbatim): "rigprice: the $/TH capital expenditure for purchasing mining hardware. For example, a 140 TH/s Antminer S19 XP with a rigprice of $35/TH would have a total unit price of $4900 per machine (140 TH/s * $35/TH)." (p. 23)
- "Overpaying for hardware near the peaks of a bull market has sent many a mining company to the gallows over the years"; conversely "a well-timed bear market hardware purchase can outperform buying bitcoin directly in the short-term, even without accounting for the mining revenue." (p. 24)
- China's rigprice edge: Chinese miners "received the best rig pricing, lowest shipping costs, and they could plug machines in much sooner"; "Even if Chinese miners hadn't had access to such cheap power, their advantage on rig prices would have made them a mining powerhouse." (pp. 24–25)
- Sentiment metrics: "rig-to-hashprice is the hardware rigprice ($/TH) divided by hashprice ($/TH/s/day) and rig-to-hashvalue is the hardware rigprice (BTC/TH) divided by hashvalue (BTC/TH/s/day)"; "the lower the rig-to-hashprice or rig-to-hashvalue, the better the timing probably is to buy hardware." (pp. 25–26)
- Interpretation: "If hashprice and rigprices go up or down at exactly the same rate, it basically means that the expected CapEx break even period (assuming a constant hashprice after purchase) for that hardware is staying the same... What are miners willing to pay for hardware relative to the current revenue that hardware can produce?" (p. 26)
- Marginal cost definition: "The marginal cost to mine 1 BTC stat is computed very simply by dividing your monthly OpEx (all-in electricity costs) by monthly BTC mined (after revenue fees such as pool and dev fees)." (p. 27)
- Total cost definition: "Total cost to mine 1 BTC includes the marginal cost but adds to it the depreciation of assets"; "$200,000 on hardware that will fully depreciate in 5 years, then the total cost to mine will include an additional $3,333 per month ($200,000/60 months)". (p. 27)
- Worked example inputs: "$200,000... purchasing 40 of the 140 TH/s Antminer S19 XPs at $35/TH... an electricity price of $0.06/ kWh, starting BTC price of $23k, difficulty of 40T, a price increment of 80%/year, and a difficulty increment of 50%/year." (p. 27)
- Post-halving squeeze: "There is a 6-month period after the halving during which the BTC price is below the miner's total cost to mine 1 BTC, meaning that they would record a net loss after depreciation for those months while still bringing in solid profits on their marginal cost to mine 1 BTC." (p. 28)
- Bull-market hardware trap: buying "22 104 TH/s S19j Pros at $86/TH in December 2021" (when "hardware prices for new-generation models were exceeding $100/TH") with flat hashprice: "by Month 8 the BTC price has dropped below their total cost to mine 1 BTC"; "cumulative profit is only $92,000 in Year 1". (pp. 28–29)
- Real financing terms: "the 12-24 month loans with 8%+ interest rates that were more common at that time" explain "why so many miners have liquidated their treasuries, sold off assets, put a halt to scaling plans, and even filed for bankruptcy." (p. 29)
- Shut-off rule (verbatim): "Miners don't shut off when their total cost to mine 1 BTC is below the BTC price, only when their marginal cost to mine is below the BTC price." (p. 29)
- Survivability: network hashrate "continued climbing to above 300 EH/s at the time of writing even though price remains 70% down from all-time highs"; a miner who "paid $80/TH for some S19j Pros in late 2021... as long as their electricity price is below $0.08/kWh, they'll keep their machines brrrring along." (p. 29)
- Bear-market dynamics: "Those with strong balance sheets will acquire machines and infrastructure on the cheap from those who are hanging on to the edge of the cliff." (p. 30)
