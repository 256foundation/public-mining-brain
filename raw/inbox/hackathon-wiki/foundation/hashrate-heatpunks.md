## Overview

Hashrate Heatpunks is a community of builders working on hashrate heating: using the heat from Bitcoin miners to warm homes, water and businesses. Its core idea is that mining heat is a product, not a problem. It is a community project of the 256 Foundation, which hosts its infrastructure, stewards its donation fund, and runs its grants. Its main gathering is the annual Heatpunk Summit in Denver.

## Mission and beliefs

The mission, in the community's words:

> “Marry the bitcoin mining and heating sectors to accelerate the adoption of hashrate heating by building the standards, education, and community infrastructure needed to bring mining back to homes and businesses.”

The vision is a world where Bitcoin mining is synonymous with electric heating, with hashrate decentralized back into homes and businesses.

What Heatpunks say they believe:

- **Mining should be distributed.** Millions of small miners, not a handful of mega-facilities.
- **Heat is a product, not waste.** Every watt of mining produces useful heat.
- **Open source wins.** Standards, documentation and tools should be free to everyone.
- **The industry does not exist yet.** There are no safety certifications, building codes or training programs, so the community is creating them.

## The barriers it names

- No UL/CE safety certifications for hashrate heating appliances.
- Building codes do not recognize the technology.
- HVAC professionals have no training or resources.
- Limited real-world case studies and performance data.

Its answer has four parts: build community (forum, Telegram, annual summit), create free education, fund development through 256 Foundation grants, and connect the Bitcoin mining and HVAC/heating industries.

## Relationship to the 256 Foundation

The Heatpunks site calls the 256 Foundation its parent nonprofit. The foundation's community page says it hosts Heatpunks infrastructure and stewards the Hashrate Heatpunks fund. The community directs the work and the board approves every allocation.

Grants for hashrate heating work run through the foundation's grant program. There is one condition: the foundation funds open-source work only, so anything a grant produces must be released publicly. Donations are tax-deductible for US donors and are taken in USD and Bitcoin through Zaprite. The site says they fund open standards, free education, the grants program and the annual summit.

## Education resources

- **A book.** *Bitcoin Mining Heat Reuse*, by Tyler Stevens, a guide to building hashrate heating into homes, businesses and industrial applications.
- **Summit videos.** Recordings from Heatpunk Summit 2025 and Heatpunk Summit 2026, plus full summit archives.
- **More in development.** Tutorials, technical guides, installation documentation and safety standards. The site says this content does not exist yet because the community is the one creating it.

The community forum at forum.heatpunks.org carries build logs. Recent topics on the home page include a pizza bag warmer heated by a Bitaxe, a filament dryer, a DIY space heater, a "limp mode" idea for internet outages, and coolant questions for a hydro system.

## The Heatpunk Summit

| Year | What the summit page says |
|---|---|
| 2025 | The first gathering, also called Undermine: HPS 2025. Around 150 people who had never met. It led directly to heatpunks.org and the community forum. Listed with 10+ live demos. |
| 2026 | The year the movement formalized. Professionals from ASHRAE and the DOE attended. Heatpunks became a formal community project of the 256 Foundation, and summit host Tyler Stevens was named president of its board. Listed with 150+ attendees and 5 workshops. |
| 2027 | Theme: acceleration. The open-source mining stack is now public, so closed firmware no longer blocks builders. |

Both past summits were held in Denver.

### Heatpunk Summit 2027

- **When.** February 26–27, 2027. Programming runs all day Friday and Saturday, with evening activities both nights. An optional ski day is planned for the day before.
- **Where.** The Space, in the RiNo Art District, at 3700 N Franklin St, Denver, CO 80205.
- **Price.** $350 USD, payable in fiat or bitcoin. It covers meals, the after-party and all sessions. Travel, lodging and the ski day are not included.
- **How to get in.** Join the waitlist. Tickets are invite-confirmed after the organizers review each application. If demand is high, people who are actively building get priority.
- **Format.** A working event, not a conference. Live hardware and working builds are prioritized over slides.

The program has four strands: a "State of the Stack" session with real build data and the policy picture, hands-on sessions on building with the 256 Foundation stack, a demo floor for live hardware, and time in the room with mining developers, HVAC and building professionals, firmware hackers and policy people.

The foundation announced the waitlist on its own forum on 2026-07-22. It described the summit as a two-day, developer-friendly event and said the workshops would center on Mujina, Ember One, Libre Board and Hydrapool.
