## Overview

The wiki holds a partial archive of posts from the foundation's X account, @256FOUNDATION. It was collected on 2026-10-07 by keyword search and is split across eight files, plus one note on method. It is not an official X data download. It covers 2023-07-22 to 2026-10-05 with known holes. Every file says the same thing in its header: coverage is incomplete. Treat the archive as a sample of what the account said, good for dating announcements that it does contain, and not as proof that something was never posted.

## How it was collected

The method note describes the collection as an X keyword and thread fetch into the public brain's inbox.

- **Query.** X search with `from:<handle>`, plus date windows and `max_id` paging.
- **Limit.** 10 hits per query. This is the main cause of the gaps: a busy window returns only part of what was posted.
- **Text.** Original post text is preserved.
- **Engagement.** Likes, reposts, replies, quotes, bookmarks and views are recorded as of collection. They are a snapshot from 2026-10-07, not figures from when each post was new.

The method note covers more than this one account. It names @256FOUNDATION as the primary account, alongside @heatpunks, @osmu_global and several notable adjacent accounts. This article covers only the @256FOUNDATION files.

## What each post record holds

Each post sits under a date heading in ISO form. Below it are the post URL and ID, the engagement counts, any media link, and the post text. Where the post quotes another post, the record carries a "Quoted" line with the other account's handle and the start of its text.

Two things follow for anyone citing the archive:

- **Quoted text is someone else's.** A hashrate figure in a quoted @skot9000 post, or a grant total in a quoted @gladstein post, is that person's claim. The foundation shared it; it did not state it.
- **Some posts are replies without their context.** A post such as the 2025-04-24 reply about BZM2 chips answers a question the archive does not show.

Files run newest post first.

- The 2024 Q1 file says everything from the account's start through 16 Feb 2024 is missing. The 2023 and 2024 origin files now cover parts of it.

## Known gaps

Taking the files together, these stretches are still missing, by the files' own notes:

| Period | What is missing |
|---|---|
| Before 2023-07-22 | Everything from the account's start, which the 2023 file equates with POD256 episodes 1 to 25. |
| Late 2023 | November and early December 2023. |
| 19 Dec 2023 to 24 Jan 2024 | The run-up to the nonprofit announcement. |
| 3 to 9 Feb 2024 | The week after episode 034. Episode 036 did not return either. |
| Mid 2024 | Most of July 2024. |
| December 2024 | All of it. |
| Early 2025 | January, most of February, and 11 Feb to 20 Mar. A January 2025 search returned no hits. |
| Autumn 2025 | Most of September apart from the Ember One v5 release candidate, and early October between 8 and 15. |
| 2026 | Most of January, March, early April before the MARA vote week, May 1 to 18, July, and Sep 1 to 10. Some posts inside the February threads are also missing. |

Smaller holes exist inside covered periods:

- The root post of the 2025-04-05 grant cohort thread did not return. Its replies did.
- Podcast threads are often partial. The first post of a thread may be present without its follow-ups, or the reverse.
- Because of the 10-hit limit, a day with many posts may be only partly captured.

The holes around the turn of 2025 matter most. No captured post shows the first Telehash as it happened, so the archive shows block 881423 only through later posts. See [Origin Story](origin-story.md).

## How complete could it be

The method note calls the collection incomplete and says a complete dump needs the account's own X archive export. The 2026 file says the same: an X data download from the account would be complete. The note also records an earlier observation that Grok cannot pull a full 256 Foundation timeline.

## Reading the engagement numbers

Counts are comparable across posts only loosely, since older posts had longer to gather views. Within that limit, the files point to a few high marks:

- The 2025 H1 file names the Ember One v3 release post of 2025-04-30 as the highest-engagement foundation post in its slice.
- In the 2026 file, the 2026-08-17 post about [RY3T Nova](ry3t-nova.md) has the most views of any post in that file.
