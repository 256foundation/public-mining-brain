## Overview

POD256 treats privacy tools and the people who build them as part of the same fight as open mining. The clearest example is its coverage of Samourai Wallet. A Samourai co-founder was a guest in 2023, the show linked defense donations in 2024, and through 2025 and 2026 it followed the case from plea deal to sentencing to a pardon campaign, including a long interview with cofounder Keonne Rodriguez. The hosts' position is plain and repeated: code is not crime, and prosecuting developers chills all open-source work. This article follows that coverage and the related privacy and security topics in the show notes.

## The plea deal (episode 080)

The notes say the Samourai Wallet developers were charged with conspiracy to operate an unlicensed money transmitting business. They first faced a 25-year sentence. The plea deal reduced the maximum sentence to five years. They still carried $6,000,000 in fines, with a sentencing hearing due in November.

The hosts drew several lessons:

- A plea bargain can be a risk mitigation strategy for a defendant.
- Conspiracy charges carry a lower burden of proof for the government.
- The case could set precedent and chill development of privacy software.
- Developers should watch their digital footprint, and open-source collaboration may give some resilience against legal threats.

## Sentencing and the Keonne Rodriguez interview (episodes 094 and 096)

Episode 094 argues that the unlicensed money transmitter framing is dangerous given Samourai's non-custodial design, and calls for broad community action, including a pardon push and better anonymity for developers.

Episode 096 is a full interview with Keonne Rodriguez about his prosecution and five-year federal sentence for building noncustodial Bitcoin privacy software. Points from the notes:

- The government's theory of “unlicensed money transmission” shifted over time.
- Conspiracy charges were built on out-of-context tweets and slides.
- A noncustodial wallet was framed as a financial institution, even after FinCEN itself reportedly said it was not.
- Whirlpool's design involves no custody and uses blinded coordination. The notes separate mixers from CoinJoin.
- The plea cut his risk from 25 years to 5.
- Because Samourai was open source, its work lives on in community projects such as Ashigaru and RoninDojo.
- Broad prosecutorial language is said to threaten developers, node operators and even miners.

Episode 095, a week earlier, closes with a segment on the chilling effect of targeting software developers. Episode 110 later points to the community forks of Samourai's software as an example of open-source resilience.

## The pardon campaign (episodes 097 to 108)

- Episode 097 pushes for signatures on the pardon petition and explains privacy-friendly ways to sign.
- Episode 098 reports Keonne's facility designation and the continuing push for a presidential pardon.
- Episode 099 goes through the Change.org petition for Bill and Keonne: confusing verification flows, a low conversion rate from views to valid signatures, and the fact that pseudonyms and disposable emails are allowed. It warns that donations made on Change.org do not reach the families and says direct support should go to GiveSendGo. It also mentions a reported acknowledgment from President Trump.
- Episode 100 closes with "Code is not crime" and a link to the petition.
- Episode 108 says Keonne Rodriguez and William Hill are serving federal prison sentences. Lauren Rodriguez describes aggressive jurisdiction tactics by SDNY, a judge swap that curtailed the defense, and how Treasury guidance and DOJ memos did not protect the developers. Ways to help are listed: sign the petition, write letters to Keonne, and spread awareness.

## Related cases and forensics

- **Tornado Cash.** Episode 033 notes developer Roman Semenov's video on the precedent his case sets and his appeal for legal defense donations.
- **Roman Sterlingov.** Episode 040 links coverage arguing that bad blockchain forensics convicted the user of a Bitcoin mixer as its operator, and an OXT research piece on the pseudoscience behind the conviction. Episode 065 returns to the theme with chapters on junk science and chain analysis as courtroom evidence.
- **Regulation.** In episode 077 Stephen DeLorme says the Southern District of New York has been aggressive in prosecuting software developers, and discusses the Clarity Act. Episode 078 is titled around developers on trial.
- **GrapheneOS.** Episode 097 reports GrapheneOS stepping back from France under regulatory pressure, as part of a trend of governments targeting toolmakers.
