## Overview

Home mining is the oldest thread on POD256. From its fifth episode the show ran a weekly Home Miner of the Week segment, and many of the picks were people heating something with a miner. Over time the theme grew from showing off hobby builds into a serious program: hardware that lets miners run on ordinary home power, control software that treats a miner as a thermostat-driven heater, an annual Heatpunk Summit, and an argument that heat reuse is where small, distributed miners have an edge. This article gathers what the show notes say on home mining and heat reuse.

## Hardware that brings miners home

Running an industrial miner on household power was an early obstacle. The notes follow one line of fixes from Zack Bomsta's Pivotal Pleb Tech:

- Episode 028 says his Njord and Loki boards for Bitmain miners let people mine at home without upgrading the home's electrical system, while improving cooling and control.
- Episode 034 links an APW12 unlock guide in the Pivotal Pleb Tech file repo.
- Episode 041 covers a Loki board and the UnBound prototype heater.
- Episode 042 shows the APW12 120V mod in use.
- Episodes 099, 107 and 113 return to 120V power supply mods. Episode 107 credits Zach Bomsta and PivotalPlebTech for the hardware mods.

Small open miners are the other route. Episode 112 argues that open hardware like the Bitaxe matters far beyond its raw terahash.

## Guides and motives

Diverter, author of Mining for the Streets, was a guest in episodes 020 and 032. The notes describe the book as a simple guide for acquiring bitcoin without KYC/AML using ASIC mining. Episode 112 looks back and says home mining resurfaced around 2020, and credits that guide and another, Home Mining for Non-KYC Bitcoin, with galvanizing a wave of at-home tinkerers.

## Heat reuse before 2025

- Episode 003 links stories on growing tulips with mining heat and distilling water with mining.
- Episode 023 notes a spa in Brooklyn heating its pools with bitcoin mining, and angry commenters.
- Episode 035 includes an audience question from Tyler Stevens asking Wilson Mining whether they do anything with the waste heat.
- Episode 050 has Rev Hodl using miners as space heaters, food dehydrators and in maple syrup production on his homestead.
- Episode 061 lists heat reuse as a source of new business opportunities.
- Episode 066 has Schnitzel explaining that his own mining began with heating his home, and that control systems are the missing piece. The same episode explores absorption chilling, which uses heat to drive cooling.
- Episode 077 describes hackathon projects on cooling, including reusable zeolite clay.

## Hashrate heating as an engineering practice

Tyler joins the show in episode 081 with a stated passion for bringing Bitcoin mining back home through heating. From then on the notes get specific.

| Episode | What was covered |
|---|---|
| 084 | Integrating hash rate heating into homes and businesses; converting a fraction of the world's heating energy into hash rate. |
| 087 | Hot-tub hash heaters, floor warming and tobacco curing with miner heat. These uses need configurability that manufacturers do not provide. |
| 088 | Sovereign smart homes with at-home miners. A showcase build: a hydro-cooled array of Bitaxes with battery integration, PLC control and custom dashboards. |
| 089 | The idea of a fully passive, fanless space-heater miner. Firmware that targets room temperature through external thermostats or Home Assistant, and hashes on dummy work for heat when the network is down. |
| 091 | Immersion mining powering radiant floor heat, with control loops through Home Assistant, thermostats and dry coolers. The argument that tight software control beats expensive hardware fixes. |
| 092 | Canaan home miners integrated with Home Assistant through APIs and Node-RED, Zigbee sensors for room-aware control, solar-aware automations, and a planned miner control hub built on Raspberry Pi 5. |
| 093 | Certification for miners and heater integrations (FCC, UL, CE, RoHS) and its insurance implications. |
| 094 | A field report from installing an immersion-based heating system on subsidized power in Buena Vista, including an oil-leak mishap. The argument that recapturing heat favors small, distributed miners. |
| 098 | New Home Assistant integrations for Canaan Avalons and WhatsMiner. |
| 102, 103 | A sous vide heater driven by miner power management and sensor feedback. |
| 104 | Hot-tub immersion builds and sous vide steak with miner heat; the need for reusable open components. |
| 109 | A dashboard built on Home Assistant and a Venstar thermostat that tracks miner-delivered BTUs against natural gas. |
| 113 | Using LibreBoard as the bridge between standard 24V thermostats and miners; ramp control versus binary heat calls. |

Episode 109 gives the most concrete result. The dashboard is said to prove that a single 5kW miner can carry a 3,000+ sq ft home through shoulder season. The same episode discusses heat pumps versus combustion heat, why furnaces are oversized, and pairing hashrate heat with existing boilers or wood-fired hydronic systems.

## Heatpunk Summit

- Episode 092 mentions a Forbes feature on Heat Punk projects.
- Episode 093 previews HeatPunk Summit 2026 as a way to bring HVAC professionals and open-source miners together.
- Episode 106 debriefs the second annual Heatpunk Summit from the Hashtub in Denver. Builders from HVAC, hydronics and home mining attended. A professional hydronics engineer critiqued the hosts' boiler setup, and galvanic corrosion was one of the lessons. Workshops covered Home Assistant control, hydronics integration, an open-source mining OS, and regulatory and insurance topics.

Episode 106 also reports that Canaan is open to supporting the home-mining and heat reuse market, and discusses what a “willing partner” ASIC manufacturer could mean for decentralization.

## Why closed miners get in the way

Episode 103 puts the case from a heating business's side. Closed, proprietary miners and shifting hardware trends, such as hydro-only and three-phase machines with fewer 240V options, undermine innovation, safety certification and product planning. Episode 126 adds that miners should not be limited to the same closed shoebox form factor, and that open designs could unlock systems for heat reuse, off-grid energy and home mining.

## Solar and power matching

- Episode 090 mentions solar-aware mining control driven by Home Assistant.
- Episode 098 celebrates Gridless's open-source JuaKali direct-DC solar mining kit.
- Episode 117 points to Mujina's promise for responsive solar mining.
- Episode 120 describes a system that tunes miners to hit exact power targets in seconds, so that miner power draw can match excess solar generation.
- Episode 124 reports Riot's solar-responsive mining product built on Mujina.
