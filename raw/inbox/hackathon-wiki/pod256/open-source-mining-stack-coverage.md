## Overview

From 2024 onward POD256 doubles as the running log of the 256 Foundation's work to open-source the whole mining stack: the hash board (Ember One), the control board (Libre Board), the firmware (Mujina) and the pool (Hydra Pool). The show notes record each project from first mention to working demo, along with Telehash events, chip sourcing, Bitaxe news and funding. This article pulls those mentions into one timeline per project. It reports what the show notes say; for the projects themselves see the linked articles.

## From idea to foundation

- Episode 029 describes a telehash: a 24-hour event to raise funds, bitcoin and mining equipment for grassroots Bitcoin communities.
- Episode 034 is titled for a Home & Open Source Mining Initiative, and episode 035 gives the working name The 256 Foundation.
- Episode 051 states the mission of making Bitcoin mining free and open.
- Episode 056 ties the Bitaxe project to that mission and reports support from the Human Rights Foundation.
- Episode 067 uses the phrase that later recurs: the aim is dismantling the proprietary mining empire.

Episode 115 gives these figures for the Telehash #4 stress test: 30.8 zettahashes processed, an average of 1.32 EH/s, a peak of 2.495 EH/s, 2,231 workers and 59 unique users. Server CPU stayed low with more than 2,000 connections.

## Ember One (hash board)

- **057:** Introduced as a project to create a fully open-source, 100-watt hash board.
- **060:** Aims to standardize hash boards for easier manufacturing.
- **070:** The first version has been released, with a focus on modular mining hardware.
- **088, 089:** Version 5 design updates: a smarter voltage regulator with digital telemetry and over-temp safeguards, header breakouts for optional fan-control daughterboards, and dropping 24V input in favor of better performance up to 17V. The design is released on the 256 Foundation's GitHub.
- **094, 097:** Prototyping on the foundation's pick-and-place machine. The goal is developer kits before fully assembled plug-and-play units.
- **105:** Mujina developer Ryan found an IO voltage domain bug. A desk-side hardware fix got the board to about 2 TH/s. The stated target is 3.6 TH/s across 12 chips with proper cooling.
- **109:** The next revision targets the Intel BZM2 chip. The hosts describe Ember One as a reference platform for builders even if its efficiency lags the newest ASICs.
- **111:** Version 6.1 brings reliability upgrades.

See [Ember One](../hardware/ember-one.md).

## Intel chips and chip access

Episodes 060 and 062 raise Intel chips as an opportunity and a manufacturing challenge. Episode 081 mentions distributing Intel chips for further innovation. Episode 083 covers the arrival of the chips at the 256 Foundation and the work of reverse engineering them without documentation. Episode 093 reports lessons from the Bitaxe Bonanza, a new dev board called BIRDS, and hurdles with nine-bit serial. Episode 110 shows the Bitaxe Bonanza, built around Intel chips donated to the foundation and targeting about 1.2 TH/s, with a sidecar for Intel's 9-bit serial protocol.

> **Status: Disputed**
> The show notes name the Intel chip two ways. Episodes 083, 093 and 109 call it the BZM2. Episode 110 calls it the BMZ2. The notes do not explain the difference; one of them is probably a typing slip.

Other chip threads: episode 082 has Skot reverse engineering new Antminer S23 chips, and episode 095 says the foundation offers free Auradine chips for reverse engineering.

## Libre Board (control board)

- **066:** Schnitzel is developing the Libre Board for the 256 Foundation. His route into mining began with heating his home.
- **088:** Prototypes exist.
- **097:** Moving through v5 prototyping alongside Ember One.
- **109:** Version 3 is on deck, designed to orchestrate multiple boards, relays and sensors.
- **111:** Version 3 power design updates.
- **113:** Schnitzel's Doom-on-LibreBoard test. The hosts discuss LibreBoard as the bridge between standard 24V thermostats and miners.
- **127:** Further progress is mentioned.

The notes number the board's versions inconsistently: v5 prototyping in episode 097, then v3 on deck in episodes 109 and 111. The episode 097 wording covers Ember One and Libre Board together, so the v5 there may belong to Ember One only.

See [Libre Board](../hardware/libre-board.md).

## Hydra Pool (pool software)

- **068:** Jungly introduces the project. The notes stress payout mechanisms and decentralization.
- **070, 079, 082:** Public testing opens, continues, and listeners are asked to test a new server.
- **090:** The team moved from a CKPool fork to a fresh Rust stratum server. Goals include verifiable share accounting through API streams.
- **093:** Public Dockerized releases and coinbase payout flexibility.
- **097:** Described as open-source, non-custodial pool software running in the lab, with a Grafana dashboard and PPLNS accounting for up to 100 addresses per coinbase.
- **101:** d++ presents the HashDash visualizer and planned TeleDash dashboards. Payout models listed are solo, PPLNS and multi-address coinbase. The pool is stress-tested to 10,000 workers.
- **103:** The foundation runs a donation-only instance.
- **109:** Roadmap items include future Lightning and eCash payouts and a plugin architecture.
- **116:** GridPool is discussed as compatible with it.
- **126:** The hosted dashboard at dash.256f.org becomes more gamified, with metrics such as luck and loyalty.

See [Hydrapool](../hydrapool/hydrapool.md).

## Red-team work and outside adoption

Episodes 123 to 125 describe reverse engineering and red-team analysis of closed-source mining firmware, helped by AI tools. Episode 124 says these opaque systems create operational and even grid-level risks, and reports early findings from a WhatsMiner M64. On the other side, episode 106 reports Canaan's openness to the home-mining market, and episode 117 notes Braiins' new BraiinsForge initiative.
