## Overview

Bitaxe is an open-source standalone Bitcoin miner built around a single ASIC chip. Open Source Miners United (OSMU) is the community that grew up around it. Both predate the 256 Foundation and shaped it: Skot, who instigated Bitaxe, is now Secretary of the foundation's board and lead maintainer of Ember One.

## Bitaxe

A Bitaxe pairs one mining ASIC with an ESP32-S3 microcontroller that acts as the brain and connects to Wi-Fi. The OSMU wiki describes a hashrate between 250-500 gigahashes per second while using up to 15 watts.

The foundation credits Bitaxe with proving that a brand-new chip could be documented and run entirely on open source. It calls Bitaxe the single-chip step toward a fully open hash board, and [Ember One](../hardware/ember-one.md) the multi-chip step after it.

Bitaxe also shows up inside the foundation's own stack. Mujina uses the Bitaxe Gamma as its developer hash board, and the DOOMAXE demo used a Bitaxe as its hash board. See [Mujina Firmware](../mujina/mujina-firmware.md) and [Libre Board](../hardware/libre-board.md).

## Open Source Miners United

OSMU was founded in March 2023 and focuses on open-source Bitcoin mining hardware, firmware and software. Its stated position is that mining has become closed and proprietary over the last decade even though Bitcoin itself has been open source from the start. It rejects industry gatekeeping and secrecy and shares what members learn. The community gathers on the OSMU Discord.

## Link to the 256 Foundation

The foundation's mission page says Skot's work on Bitaxe sparked OSMU and helped influence both the formation of the 256 Foundation and the architecture of Ember One. The foundation also acts as fiscal sponsor for OSMU. See [256 Foundation: Mission and Organization](../foundation/mission-and-organization.md).
