## Overview

Skot announced each new Bitaxe generation on X. This article records four of those announcements: the Ultra, the Supra, the Gamma and the Gamma Hex. Each post names the chip, gives an early performance figure, and credits the people who helped. The figures are what Skot stated on the day, from early units. For the documented specs of each board, see [Bitaxe Model Lineup](bitaxe-models.md) and [Multi-Chip Bitaxe Designs](multi-chip-bitaxe-designs.md).

## Ultra

On 2023-08-21 Skot wrote that it was safe to say the bitaxeUltra was working. The post described it as open source everything and gave the credit to the Open Source Miners United crew for figuring it out.

## Supra

On 2024-02-07 Skot said the bitaxeSupra was working, again crediting the Open Source Miners United crew. A follow-up the same day added three points:

- The team had only just got it working, through a coordinated effort with @Public_Pool_BTC, @jpc0mps and @johnny9dev.
- It still needed more work before shipping.
- The BM1368 chips were so new that they were only available in sets of 324.

On 2024-02-09 Skot also thanked @checksum0 for the first hashboard pictures.

## Gamma

On 2024-08-19 Skot announced the bitaxeGamma as the newest member of the lineup. The post said everything about it is open source and permissionless. The archive calls this the highest-engagement Bitaxe post it found, with 1004 likes.

Two follow-ups in the same thread gave thanks:

- To @altair_tech for the early S21 Pro hashboard, @Public_Pool_BTC for the prototype build, @jpc0mps for tips, and the whole OSMU community.
- To OpenSats, for funding that lets Skot work on Bitaxe every day.

## Gamma Hex

On 2026-07-27 Skot announced the Bitaxe GammaHex. The post described six top-bin BM1370 ASICs, hydro cooling, and a four phase voltage regulator, and called it an overclocking machine. It thanked @Public_Pool_BTC and @SoloSatoshi.

The post quoted one from @SoloSatoshi, which said the Bitaxe Gamma Hex was officially unveiled at Mining Disrupt, called it the most powerful Bitaxe, and said it had been overclocked past `13TH/s`.

## Firmware support that followed

WantClue, who works on ESP-Miner, posted on 2026-09-25 that ESP-Miner 2.15.3 had landed and brought the Bitaxe Naja Duo and the Bitaxe Gamma Hex into the firmware family. On 2026-09-27 WantClue said the web flasher had been reworked so it is easier to pick the right device. See [AxeOS / ESP-Miner Firmware](axeos-esp-miner-firmware.md).

> **Status: Disputed**
> Naja Duo status. [Multi-Chip Bitaxe Designs](multi-chip-bitaxe-designs.md), from the board READMEs, calls the Naja boards untested prototypes that should not be built expecting them to work. WantClue's posts treat the Naja Duo as a supported device: ESP-Miner 2.15.3 added it (2026-09-25), and on 2026-09-18 WantClue called it the most capable open source miner so far. The READMEs carry no date in this wiki, so the X posts may simply be newer.

## Limits of the archive

These files are thread fetches from keyword searches. Only Skot's own posts in each thread are captured, not the replies. The Ultra and Supra file says it is missing the original Bitaxe launch thread and the later Hex and Naja launches. Like counts are as of collection.
