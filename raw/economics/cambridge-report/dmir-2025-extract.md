## Hardware trends

- Industry-wide efficiency (p.59): "Weighted efficiency, which accounts for the relative scale of operations (based on hashrate) of respondents, is approximated at 28.2 J/TH and serves as the benchmark for hardware efficiency in this report." Non-weighted: "an efficiency range between 18.0 J/TH and 71.4 J/TH, with half of the responses concentrated between 25.0 and 37 J/TH. The median efficiency, at 29.5 J/TH, is slightly lower than the average (30.6 J/TH)".
- YoY (p.9 / p.59): "As of June 2024, the industry-wide ASIC (SHA-256) hardware efficiency is estimated at 28.2 J/TH, marking a 24% YoY improvement, closely aligning with top-down projections from our theoretical CBECI model." CBECI model estimate for the same period: "25.3 J/TH".
- Efficiency trajectory (p.61): CBECI-model efficiency "at the beginning of the observed period (1 January 2021 to 31 December 2024) stood slightly above 60 J/TH, deteriorated towards 70 J/TH in early 2021, before gradually improving to 23.7 J/TH by the end of 2024."
- Next-gen projections (Figure 22, p.52, source TheMinerMag): expected average efficiencies "12.0 … 11.5 … 5.5 Joules per Terahash" for 2024 Q2 – 2025 Q4 window. (p.63): "the most efficient devices currently achieving 12 J/TH and forthcoming designs anticipated to reach sub-10 J/TH levels by 2025"; (p.91): "the most efficient mining device currently available (as of 31 December 2024) achieves an efficiency of 12 J/TH".
- Chip scaling (p.51): "The first ASICs were built using a 130 nm (nanometre) process, but by 2024, cutting-edge ASICs had shrunk to just 3 nm." Slowdown: "the pace of technological advancement… has begun to slow… manufacturers have demonstrated renewed progress since 2023".
- ASIC market structure (p.53): "Bitmain dominates the landscape, commanding an overwhelming 82.0% market share, followed by MicroBT (15.0%) and Canaan (2.1%). Collectively, these three manufacturers account for over 99% of the entire ASIC market." New entrants: "Bitdeer, Auradine, and Proto, are gaining traction with notable pre-orders".
- Firmware (p.53): "Stock firmware remains the most popular choice, used by 44.4% of miners… third-party firmware… with Vnish leading at 26.4%… custom firmware… 17.6%. Other third-party providers such as Braiins OS (5.6%), LuxOS (3.3%), and ePIC (2.7%)".
- Retirement & e-waste (p.56): "approximately 11.1% of network hashrate (as of 30 June 2024), equivalent to 61.8 EH/s, was expected to be retired between 30 June and 31 December 2024"; "86.9% of this decommissioned hardware is expected to be repurposed or recycled, with actual e-waste approximated at about 2.3 kilotonnes" (p.9); "13.1% of the expected hardware retirements could be considered non-recycled e-waste… approximately 2.3 kilotonnes of e-waste in 2024… significantly lower than e-waste estimates suggested in peer-reviewed literature" (p.56). Secondary use (p.55): "The majority (75.8%) indicated that a significant portion of retired devices is not permanently withdrawn from use." Recycling readiness (p.56): "Around 25.8% of participants reported actively exploring recycling strategies, while an additional 24.2% stated they have established recycling programmes in place… A small percentage (3.2%) admitted to lacking any plan".

## Electricity consumption

- Survey-based estimate (p.63): "the annualised electricity consumption of the Bitcoin network is estimated at 138.2 TWh as of 30 June 2024… representing a 17% YoY increase. To place this figure in a global context, this consumption level equates to approximately 0.54% of the world's total annual electricity consumption. The survey-based estimate also shows close alignment with the CBECI model (137.4 TWh) estimate."
- End-2024 update (p.63): "By the end of December 2024, hardware efficiency improved to 23.7 J/TH, yet electricity consumption rose to 183 TWh due to a substantial increase in network hashrate, from 558.7 EH/s (30 June 2024) to 795.7 EH/s (31 December 2024). Despite these increases, annual electricity consumption remained close to the pre-halving all-time peak of 180 TWh in March 2024."
- Hashrate vs consumption divergence (p.62): "the implied network hashrate rose by a substantial 455%, whilst annualised electricity consumption slightly more than doubled (+111%)" (2021–2024); summary quote: "Over the past five years, for each unit increase in electricity consumption, the Bitcoin network's hashrate (a key security indicator) increased by approximately 4 units."

## Electricity mix & sustainability

- Survey electricity mix (p.67): "the industry's electricity mix consists of 47.6% fossil fuels and 52.4% sustainable energy sources. Breaking down the sustainable energy sources further, renewables account for 42.6%, comprising 23.4% hydropower, 15.4% wind energy, 3.2% solar energy, and 0.5% other renewables. Additionally, nuclear energy constitutes 9.8% of the mix. Within fossil fuels, natural gas dominates, accounting for 38.2%, making it the single largest energy source, followed by coal at 8.9% and oil at 0.5%."
- Method comparison (p.68): "CBECI estimates the share of sustainable energy sources at 37.6%, significantly lower than the Survey (52.4%) and SBLB (47.9%) estimates"; "the CBECI model attributes 36.6% to coal, making it the predominant energy source. In contrast, SBLB identifies natural gas as the predominant source (37.0%), closely matching the Survey estimate (38.2%)." — CBECI's IP-based geography data is dated January 2022.
- Emission intensity (p.68): "the CBECI (506.7 gCO2e/kWh) is significantly higher than both SBLB (338.9 gCO2e/kWh) and the Survey-based estimate (288.2 gCO2e/kWh)". GHG totals: "Survey (39.8 MtCO2e)… approximately 42.8% and SBLB (46.8 MtCO2e) roughly 32.8% lower than CBECI (69.6 MtCO2e)".
- Context (p.9 / p.69): "approximately 39.8 MtCO2e attributable GHG emissions… representing about 0.08% of global annual GHG emissions, although a more nuanced analysis suggests a potential range of 32.9 to 37.6 MtCO2e"; "similar to the annual emissions of Slovakia (39.8 MtCO₂e) and roughly half the estimated environmental impact of the global tobacco industry (84 MtCO₂e)" (p.69).
- Off-grid power (p.65): "26% of surveyed digital mining firms reported utilising off-grid energy sources, while the remaining 74% did not. In terms of total power consumption, however, off-grid energy accounts for only 8.1%, or 1.23 GW".
- Demand flexibility (p.9): "miners reported a total load curtailment of 888 GWh for the calendar year 2023"; climate mitigation (p.76): "70.8% of respondents are actively pursuing some form of climate mitigation strategy, while 29.2% are not".
- Flaring note (p.69): "the average methane destruction efficiency of flares in key U.S. basins is approximately 91.1%, with some flares operating as low as 60% efficiency" (vs commonly assumed 98%).

## Mining economics — revenue

- Revenue drivers & halving block (p.81): "ViaBTC, the mining pool that mined the halving block (block height 840,000), earned a remarkable 74.051 BTC… primarily driven by a surge in transaction fees, which reached 37.626 BTC, and an additional 33.3 BTC from the sale of an 'epic sat'".
- Fee mix (p.83): "regular transactions dominate fee revenue, accounting for approximately 79% of total fee revenue (December 2024)". Runes anomaly (p.85): "Runes transactions accounted for 41% of total transaction fees in April 2024, despite constituting only 15% of total transactions during that period."
- Hashprice definition (p.88): "It is the revenue, measured in U.S. dollars or native units, per unit of computational power per day" (BTC/PH/day and USD/PH/day, 2020–2024, source Coin Metrics).
- Long-run squeeze (p.89): "During the period under review, bitcoin price (+1209%) consistently remained either close to or above network difficulty (+734%). Yet, despite the considerably larger increase in bitcoin price, hashprice experienced a significant decline (-60%). This drop can be attributed to two halving events during the period (May 2020 and April 2024), which reduced the block subsidy from 12.5 bitcoin to 3.125 bitcoin – a 75% reduction."
- Revenue per kWh (p.90): "previous-generation devices (30 J/TH) currently generate about $0.08/kWh, whereas newer models achieve roughly between $0.11 – $0.15/kWh" (1 July – 31 Dec 2024; 15 J/TH ≈ $0.15–0.20 band).
- Epoch hashprice swings (p.103): "During the second epoch, hashprice soared to a peak of $3,877 before plunging to a trough of $78 per PH/day, representing a dramatic 98% decline. In the third epoch… falling from a high of $422 to a low of $55 per PH/day (an 87% decline). Similarly, in the current epoch… dropping from $90 per PH/day at its peak to $38 at its lowest point – marking a 58% decline – before rebounding to $54 by the end of 2024."
- Hashcost trend (p.104): "hashcost falling from $128 at the beginning of the second epoch to $16 at the beginning of the fourth epoch – a reduction of 87.5% over approximately eight years."

## Mining economics — costs & profitability (the cost-curve core)

- Electricity rates (p.86): "the median direct electricity rate reported by miners is $45 per megawatt-hour"; all-in (Figure 44, p.87, N=35/32): direct median $45.0/MWh, all-in median $55.5/MWh, "The difference between the median all-in electricity rate and the median direct electricity rate… representing other expenses, in USD per megawatt-hour" = $10.5/MWh. Figure 44(b) expense split: "81.1%" electricity cost vs "18.9%" other expenses "in COGS and SG&A". Box-plot values (USD/MWh): direct 20.0 / 38.5 / 45.0 / 55.0(?) / 70.0; all-in includes 50.8, 55.5, 65.0 (figure text layer).
- Cost to mint one BTC (p.86): "Figure 45(a) illustrates the range of implied electricity costs for producing a single bitcoin, which spans from $17,417 to $56,606, with a median of $39,189. This finding lends credence to our theoretical CBECI bitcoin production cost estimate of $39,009 (as of 30 June 2024)… Focusing on all-in production costs, Figure 45(b) reveals an extended range of $21,771 to $60,960, with a median of $48,333."
- Figure 45 detail (p.87, USD): Direct quartiles "33 528 | 39 189 | 47 897", whiskers "17 417 … 56 606", "CBECI 39 009"; All-in quartiles "44 240 | 48 333 | 56 606", whiskers "21 771 … 60 960".
- Electricity share of opex (p.9): "Miners report a median electricity cost of $45/MWh and an all-in cost of $55.5/MWh, with electricity constituting more than 80% of their cash-based operational expenses."
- Hashcost definition (p.91): "hashcost represents the cost per unit of computational power, typically measured in U.S. dollars… if hashcost exceeds hashprice, miners are operating at a loss."
- Production-cost vs price history (p.91): "a prolonged period of depressed margins from summer 2022 to around spring 2023, and more recently, a compression following the fourth halving event in April 2024. However, closer to the end of 2024, the spread significantly widened and the difference between BTC price and production cost marked a new all-time high in absolute terms."
- Hash margin definition (p.92): "Hash Margin = Hashprice – Hashcost" (also expressible as %).
- Break-even matrix (p.92, Figure 50(a), at constant hashprice; rows = electricity rate, cols = efficiency 15–45 J/TH):
  - $20/MWh: 87% 82% 78% 73% 69% 65% 60% (USD margins $47 $45 $42 $40 $37 $35 $33)
  - $40/MWh: 73% 65% 56% 47% 38% 29% 20% ($40 $35 $30 $25 $21 $16 $11)
  - $60/MWh: 60% 47% 34% 20% 7% -6% -19% ($33 $25 $18 $11 $4 -$3 -$11)
  - $80/MWh: 47% 29% 12% -6% -24% -41% -59% ($25 $16 $6 -$3 -$13 -$23 -$32)
  - $100/MWh: 34% 12% -11% -33% -55% -77% -99% ($18 $6 -$6 -$18 -$30 -$42 -$54)
- Interpretation (p.92): "at an electricity rate of $80/MWh, miners with an efficiency of 25 J/TH are slightly above break-even, while those with 30 J/TH are already experiencing a loss. Conversely, at a lower electricity cost of $40/MWh, miners with an efficiency as low as 45 J/TH still remain profitable".
- 30 J/TH worked example (p.92–93): "using an efficiency level of 30 J/TH, which was considered top-of-the-line in 2020… reaching a peak of 91% during the most favourable conditions and falling below 10% in less advantageous times… throughout the five-year period that included two halving events, the hash margin remained above the break-even point, even rebounding to 35% by the end of 2024." / "Even with an assumed electricity rate of $50/MWh, profitability was maintained throughout the observed period."
- Implication for hardware lifespan (p.93): "at a lower electricity cost of $40/MWh… such units, despite their age, would still hold a competitive advantage over current next-generation devices operated at electricity rates of $80/MWh or higher."

## Miner sentiment & growth barriers

- Concerns (p.9–10): "long-term energy price increases (57%), unfavourable governmental action at local or federal level (47%), and adverse BTC price developments (40%)".
- Risk management (p.10): "miners named business diversification (64%), power hedging (60%), and geographical diversification (55%) as key risk management strategies. Hashprice hedging and cryptoasset collateralisation are perceived as less effective."
- Growth barriers (p.98 / p.10): "a lack of deployment capacity… 46.8% of respondents ranking it as at least a high constraint… 47% of miners seeing the former and 45% the latter [logistical challenges] as at least a high constraint – although access to debt (40%) and equity (36%) financing also pose a challenge."
- Forecasting (p.100): actual year-end 2024 hashrate "796 EH/s"; "44.7% accurately anticipated the hashrate would fall between 700 and 800 EH/s"; scenario medians — restrained "688 EH/s", baseline "750 EH/s", accelerated "824 EH/s" ("the accelerated scenario more accurately reflected the year-end outcome").

## Trends: AI/HPC convergence

- Revenue comparison (p.112): "CaaS workloads can generate revenues of $1,600 to $4,000 per MWh, while colocation services (IaaS) bring in $120 to $400 per MWh – both considerably higher than the $80 to $151 per MWh currently achievable with Bitcoin mining".
- CAPEX (p.112): "Estimates from J.P. Morgan Research suggest CAPEX for these models ranges from $9 to $40 million per MW, a 8-33x difference compared to Bitcoin mining's $1.2 million per MW".
- Data-centre demand (p.112): "global power demand for data centres expected to increase by 116% from 2024 to 2030" (citing Goldman Sachs projection of ~1,100 TWh by 2030, 14.5% CAGR, Figure 59, p.111).
- Strategic framing (p.10): "The legacy miner revenue model, heavily reliant on the block subsidy, faces increasing pressure from recurring halving events. Diversification into high-growth sectors like HPC serving computationally intense AI workloads, leveraging existing infrastructure, is emerging as a key adaptive strategy."
- Case study (p.113): "Core Scientific will deliver approximately 500 MW of digi[tal infrastructure…]" (HPC to AI hyperscaler CoreWeave).
