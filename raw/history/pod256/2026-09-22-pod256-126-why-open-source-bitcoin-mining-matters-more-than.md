## Show notes

In this episode, we kicked things off with a fresh Bitcoin mining network update, covering hash rate, difficulty, pool distribution, and some interesting movement from Ocean. Even though Eco accidentally called this episode #125, it is actually #126. Moving on, we zoomed in on our hosted HydraPool dashboard at dash.256f.org, where recent updates have made the experience more gamified and sparked a broader conversation about better miner metrics, including luck, loyalty, difficulty, and clearer ways to represent huge hash totals. We also got into a fun but revealing discussion about the long-term future of mining rewards, fee pressure, and whether today’s network usage is enough to support Bitcoin miners as subsidies continue to shrink.

We then spent most of the episode unpacking the bigger picture behind open source Bitcoin mining. We reflected on our recent fundraiser in San Francisco with the 256 Foundation and how helpful it was to present, in person, why open source mining matters and how our core projects fit together: hash board, control board, firmware, and pool infrastructure. We talked through the pain of today’s proprietary mining stack, why miners should not be limited to the same closed “shoebox” form factor, and how community research, public documentation, and shared tooling can unlock new mining systems for heat reuse, off-grid energy, home mining, and other specialized use cases. Along the way, we also touched on AI’s growing usefulness for hardware development, the importance of publishing field knowledge, and some of the security concerns emerging from current mining and IoT device ecosystems.
