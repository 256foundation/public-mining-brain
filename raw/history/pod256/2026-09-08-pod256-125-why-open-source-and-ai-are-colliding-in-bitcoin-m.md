## Show notes

After a short hiatus, we’re back and diving into how our workflow is changing with BuzzRelay, an open-source collaboration tool built on Nostr that lets AI agents participate as native members of the chat. We walked through how we’re using a dedicated podcast agent to prep each episode with Bitcoin, mining, and open-source mining updates, and how that same setup now helps us track activity on our HashDash pool—everything from user growth and hashrate peaks to leaderboard changes and standout submissions. We also unpacked the latest chaos around the Liquid sidechain exploit, the blurry line between “white hat” and outright theft, and what incidents like this actually say about Bitcoin security versus the role miners play in securing the network.

We also spent a lot of time on what feels like the next major unlock for open-source mining: AI-assisted hardware, firmware, and PCB design. We talked through the rapid progress happening with KiCad and newer AI models, how these tools are making serious electronics design far more accessible, and why open reference designs matter so much if mining is going to benefit from this shift. From our ongoing 256 Foundation red-team work on proprietary miner firmware, to Mujina development, reverse engineering power behavior, and the broader challenge of building a healthy open-source ecosystem instead of fragmented forks, this episode was a wide-ranging look at how quickly the tooling is improving—and why that makes open infrastructure more important than ever.
