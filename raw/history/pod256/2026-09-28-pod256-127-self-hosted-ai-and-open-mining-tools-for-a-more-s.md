## Show notes

In this episode, we recapped Tyler’s trip to the Midwest Bitcoin Summit in Columbus and dug into a theme that kept surfacing throughout the week: how patents and closed systems can slow adoption, while open-source development unlocks entirely new markets. We connected historical examples like automated flour mills, steam engines, and aluminum production to the current state of Bitcoin mining, where a small number of hardware vendors still hold too much of the stack behind closed doors. We talked through why open hardware and software matter so much for Bitcoin’s resilience, how centralization creates both censorship and supply-chain risk, and why we believe the economic incentives will ultimately favor open designs, shared reference implementations, and broader participation across the mining ecosystem.

We also spent time on the rapid rise of self-hosted AI and how it is already becoming a force multiplier for open-source builders. We shared experience setting up a private local LLM on a new Mac Mini, and we explored how AI agents can help with hardware design, software development, pull request review, and even activist tools built at a recent AI Hack for Freedom event. From LibreBoard progress and Adit boards to new Bitaxe enclosures, Proto development, and WhatsMiner-based community builds, this conversation tied together the bigger picture: open tools, sovereign infrastructure, and the growing set of building blocks that can make Bitcoin mining more accessible, decentralized, and unstoppable.
