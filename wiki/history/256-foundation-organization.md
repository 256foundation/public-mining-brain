## Overview

The 256 Foundation is a US 501(c)(3) public charity, based in Nashville, Tennessee, that funds open-source bitcoin mining. Its thesis is that a miner is made of four building blocks: hashboard, control board, firmware and pool. Each of them is closed or concentrated today, so the Foundation pays engineers to publish open versions of all four. The results are Ember One (hashboard), Libre Board (control board), Mujina (firmware) and Hydrapool (pool) ([256F: Mission](../../raw/history/256f-org/256foundation-org-mission.md); [256F: FAQ](../../raw/history/256f-org/256foundation-org-faq.md)). The Foundation was formed in February 2024 by Bitkite and Econoalchemist, co-hosts of the POD256 podcast. Its first working capital came from block 881423, solo-mined during its first Telehash hashrate fundraiser. It has since added grants from OpenSats, the Human Rights Foundation (HRF) and the MARA Foundation, plus a set of programs beyond the core grants. It calls itself "the Linux Foundation of Bitcoin Mining" ([256F: Our Work](../../raw/history/256f-org/256foundation-org-our-work.md)).

## Founding and legal timeline

The Foundation grew out of POD256, a podcast that started in December 2022 ([POD256 #001](../../raw/history/pod256/2022-12-03-pod256-001-hashrate-down-14-from-ath-hashprice-at-atl-territ.md)) and whose early episodes were hosted by Econoalchemist and Rod (Bitkite) ([POD256 #004](../../raw/history/pod256/2023-01-21-pod256-004-recap-nashville-energy-mining-summit-2023-state-o.md)). On episode 034 (2024-02-02) the two introduced "a non-profit initiative to support home & open source bitcoin mining innovation" and asked listeners to help name it ([X archive: 2024 origin](../../raw/history/256f-research/2026-10-07-x-256foundation-2024-origin.md)). The earliest 256foundation GitHub repo was created 2024-02-08 ([256F GitHub org snapshot](../../raw/history/256f-research/2026-10-07-github-256foundation-org-snapshot.md)).

## Mission and its evolution

| Period | Stated mission | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 (Jun 2024 capture) | "A Free Open Source Bitcoin Mining, Energy, & Education Initiative": education on intelligent energy use and variable load controllers, grants, and a mining lab, with a pledge that every project would be FOSS | [archived home, Jun 2024](../../raw/history/256f-org/archive-20240606-256foundation-home.md) |
| 2025 (Jan 2025 capture) | "Dismantle the proprietary mining empire to make Bitcoin and freedom tech accessible to anyone". Software is defined by GNU's free-software definition and hardware by the OSD. Supporters could sponsor Bitcoin Park memberships for developers | [archived home, Jan 2025](../../raw/history/256f-org/archive-20250130-256foundation-ref-mining-build.md) |
| 2026 (current site) | To decentralize bitcoin mining by building, funding and stewarding open-source alternatives to every closed layer of the stack, so it "cannot be owned, switched off, or permissioned by anyone" | [256F: Mission](../../raw/history/256f-org/256foundation-org-mission.md) |

The thesis on the current site is "Bitcoin mining will be open-source, or Bitcoin remains permissioned." ([256F: Our Work](../../raw/history/256f-org/256foundation-org-our-work.md); [Presidio fundraiser post](../../raw/history/256f-org/256foundation-org-newsroom-presidio-bitcoin-fundraiser.md)). The FAQ names three centralization vectors to justify it. The first is hardware and firmware controlled by one Chinese company with ~90% market dominance. The second is ~90% of global hashrate controlled by four pool operators and their proxies. The third is ~40% of mining rewards going to one custodian ([256F: FAQ](../../raw/history/256f-org/256foundation-org-faq.md)). The Presidio post argues that a company is the wrong vehicle for commoditizing mining because "a company has an edge to protect", while a nonprofit has nothing to protect. It compares the goal to the Hall-Héroult process, which cut aluminum from $4.86 a pound to 78 cents in five years, and to Linux, TCP and HTTP ([Presidio fundraiser post](../../raw/history/256f-org/256foundation-org-newsroom-presidio-bitcoin-fundraiser.md)).

The donation stance has stayed the same in substance. The 2025 site said "money from all, influence from none", and the current site says "Money from anyone, influence from no one", with no special treatment for any funder ([archived home, Jan 2025](../../raw/history/256f-org/archive-20250130-256foundation-ref-mining-build.md); [256F: Mission](../../raw/history/256f-org/256foundation-org-mission.md)). The Foundation says it starts projects but does not own or sell them. Every core project and grant is released under a recognized open-source licence, "no exceptions" ([256F: Mission](../../raw/history/256f-org/256foundation-org-mission.md)).

## Funding policy and how grants work

- **Mechanics.** An applicant submits a scoped proposal. The board reviews it against written criteria. The Foundation and grantee then finalize scope, term and milestones, and the board approves the grant. A written grant agreement keeps the work open source, and "IP never transfers to the Foundation". Grantees are paid monthly and build in public against milestones ([256F: Grants](../../raw/history/256f-org/256foundation-org-grants.md)).
- **Eligibility.** Eligible work includes OSHWA-compliant hardware, OSI-compliant firmware and software, pool software, education and documentation, and tools. The Foundation does not fund closed-source work, unrelated projects, duplicates without meaningful differentiation, or work that restricts others' use ([256F: Grants](../../raw/history/256f-org/256foundation-org-grants.md)).
- **Confidentiality.** The exact dollar or sat amount awarded to each grantee is kept confidential for security reasons. Grants are paid in bitcoin or fiat, as the recipient prefers. Annual tax disclosures are available for public inspection ([256F: FAQ](../../raw/history/256f-org/256foundation-org-faq.md)).
- **Pseudonymous grantees.** Taxpayer information is still required. The Foundation suggests forming a Wyoming LLC so the grantee can share entity information instead of personal information ([256F: FAQ](../../raw/history/256f-org/256foundation-org-faq.md)).
- **Pay level.** Core contributors are funded as full-time salaries at market rate for their skill sets. A transparency report was promised for Fall 2026 ([256F Telegram digest](../../raw/history/2026-10-08-256f-telegram-signal.md)).
- **Treasury.** POD256 #110 described an all-bitcoin treasury experiment that pays developers in sats pegged to cost basis ([Assembling Freedom, POD256 #110](../../raw/history/256f-newsletter/2026-04-05-pod256-episode-110-newsletter-april-fools-real-progress-open.md)).
- **Continuity.** The Foundation says core contributors need multi-year funding. In its account, continuous donations are what keep a funded term on schedule instead of stopping and restarting ([Libre Board funding post](../../raw/history/256f-org/256foundation-org-newsroom-libre-board-funding.md)).

The 2025 FAQ described a different disbursement model. General Fund donations went out "typically in 6, 9, or 12 month cycles per project", and the FAQ promised that 100% of General Fund donations went to grantees, with "no cut taken for administrative costs nor for board members" ([FAQ, archived Sep 2025](../../raw/history/256f-org/archive-20250913-256foundation-faq-html.md)).

> **Status: Outdated** (2026-08-04)
> The "100% passthrough" claim was retired sitewide on 2026-08-04 (website PR #5) and replaced with "Direct Funding" language. The FAQ question about the share of donations reaching contributors was reframed because it "was built around a figure we no longer publish". The separate claim that no board member is compensated was kept as still accurate ([website PR #5](../../raw/history/256f-org/2026-08-04-github-website-pr-5-copy-retire-the-100-passthrough-claim-si.md); [256F: FAQ](../../raw/history/256f-org/256foundation-org-faq.md)).

The funding-ethics question came up early. When Foundry USA became the first major public supporter (2024-08-09), community members argued over taking "centralized" money. Econoalchemist answered that Foundry "isn't suggesting or requesting anything from us" ([256F Telegram digest](../../raw/history/2026-10-08-256f-telegram-signal.md)).

## Telehash fundraisers

Telehash is the Foundation's occasional in-person, livestreamed fundraiser. The community points hashrate at the Foundation's pool in solo mode, and any block reward goes to the Foundation ([256F: Telehash](../../raw/history/256f-org/256foundation-org-telehash.md)). POD256 first announced the idea on 2023-09-23 as a 24-hour event planned for Bitcoin Park in January 2024 ([X archive: 2023](../../raw/history/256f-research/2026-10-07-x-256foundation-2023.md)). To take part, set the pool to `stratum+tcp://pool.256foundation.org:3333` and use `username.workername`. If the username is a website, X handle or Nostr npub, the leaderboard shows its avatar ([256F: Telehash](../../raw/history/256f-org/256foundation-org-telehash.md)).

> **Status: Disputed**: which pool found block 881423, and what it launched
> The Telehash page says participants pointed hashrate at "our Hydrapool instance" and that the block raised the money "that launched the organization" ([256F: Telehash](../../raw/history/256f-org/256foundation-org-telehash.md)). Several contemporaneous sources disagree. On 2025-03-25 the Foundation posted that it was "running a @FutureBit Apollo for our node and stratum server" ([X archive: 2025 H1](../../raw/history/256f-research/2026-10-07-x-256foundation-2025-h1.md)). On 2025-01-27 the donation endpoint was pointed at the Foundation's self-hosted Apollo ([256F Telegram digest](../../raw/history/2026-10-08-256f-telegram-signal.md)). The May 2025 newsletter says the first Hydra Pool was built "specifically for the upcoming Telehash #2" ([newsletter, May 2025](../../raw/history/256f-newsletter/2025-05-05-bitcoin-mining-will-not-be-decentralized-until-it-is-open-so.md)). The January 2026 newsletter refers to "#1 with the Apollo solo pool" ([Assembling Freedom #13](../../raw/history/256f-newsletter/2026-01-14-assembling-freedom-13.md)). The organization itself had existed since February 2024 ([256F: FAQ](../../raw/history/256f-org/256foundation-org-faq.md)). Best assessment: block 881423 was mined through a FutureBit Apollo solo stratum server, and Hydrapool did not exist yet. The block seeded the four core projects, not the organization. The FAQ's wording ("seeded the four core projects") is the accurate one.

> **Status: Disputed**: Telehash #3 sous-vide temperature
> The Telehash page says Mujina held the probe "at exactly 101°F" ([256F: Telehash](../../raw/history/256f-org/256foundation-org-telehash.md)). Assembling Freedom #14 says water temperatures hit "131°F for medium-rare" ([Assembling Freedom #14](../../raw/history/256f-newsletter/2026-01-30-assembling-freedom-14.md)). Best assessment: unresolved. 131°F matches the stated medium-rare goal. The newsletter is an AI-assisted podcast summary, and the website is the Foundation's own record.

> **Status: Disputed**: Telehash #4 peak hashrate
> The forum metrics post gives a peak of 2.49 EH/s and an average of 1.32 EH/s ([Telehash #4 metrics](../../raw/history/256f-org/2026-05-21-forum-telehash-4-performance-metrics.md)). A Foundation X post says "Over 2.5 EH/s" ([X archive: 2026](../../raw/history/256f-research/2026-10-07-x-256foundation-2026.md)), and a mid-event Telegram reading was 1.2 EH/s ([256F Telegram digest](../../raw/history/2026-10-08-256f-telegram-signal.md)). Best assessment: the forum's computed metrics are authoritative. "Over 2.5" is rounding, and 1.2 EH/s was a point-in-time reading close to the event average.
