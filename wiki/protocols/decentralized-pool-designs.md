## Overview

The design space beyond the hosted-pool model: template selection (DATUM), trustless payout schemes that avoid custodians, decentralized share accounting (GridPool, P2Poolv2, Dean's sketch), and the small protocol details — pool signatures, coinbase payout limits, midstate handling — that turn out to matter. Much of this is live 256F-adjacent engineering: Hydrapool shares core libs with P2Poolv2, and GridPool runs on an experimental Hydrapool fork. For the wire protocols these designs ride on (Stratum V1 quirks, SV2 channels, Noise auth, translator interop), see [Stratum V1 & V2 in Practice](stratum-in-practice.md).

Editorial position from the 256 Foundation newsletter: miner-built templates (DATUM, SV2) are a good step, but while a centralized pool still counts shares and coordinates payouts it can exclude non-conforming shares or payout addresses, so "centralized pools will never decentralize Bitcoin mining" ([Assembling Freedom, 2025-06](../../raw/history/256f-newsletter/2025-06-19-you-know-i-m-something-of-a-decentralized-pool-myself.md)).

The POD256 pod framed the same gap in April 2026: heater miners distributed around the world are "pointing them at centralized pools", so "we still haven't won"; template selection "helps", but "we do not have a complete solution that really decentralizes pool mining" ([POD256 e112](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-04-15-e112.md)).

## Template selection & DATUM

- DATUM (Luke Dashjr's template system) opened up template-creation options outside the big pools; observed with surprise that it wasn't built on Stratum v2 [#1308 · 2024-09-29 · Tyler Stevens] [#1310 · 2024-09-29 · econoalchemist]
- A mined block can carry a 'Mined by POD256'-style identifier via the template [#1311 · 2024-09-29 · Elijah Sanders]
- mempool.space policy: a pool must solve one mainnet block before mempool.space will display its scriptsig; The Space's pool block appeared via Ocean/DATUM because Mechanic contributed Ocean-specific code to mempool — call to generalize for Hydrapool and standardize [#3728 · 2025-11-29 · Ryan] [#3730 · 2025-11-29 · Ryan] [#3732 · 2025-11-29 · Ryan] [#3733 · 2025-11-29 · Ryan] [#3734 · 2025-11-29 · Ryan] [#3741 · 2025-11-29 · Skot Bitaxe]
- DATUM on StartOS (August 2026): per Dylan, you have to "configure block notify to specifically notify the Datum service" — possible on Bitcoin Knots but not on Core under StartOS on the release he tested; a StartOS overhaul since may have changed that ([POD256 e124](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-08-17-e124.md))
- Mechanics per the UnderMine 2025 DATUM setup tutorial (Bitcoin Mechanic, per the program page): DATUM is, per the talk, "a stratum v1 server", with SV2 support only once "SV2 gets more widely supported" ([DATUM tutorial](../../raw/history/heatpunks-summit/20251111-undermine-2025-datum-setup-tutorial-with-bitcoin-mechanic.md) [00:10:02-00:10:33]); template construction is the node's job — DATUM asks the node, "saying can I have a template to the node", "that's via RPC and the node responds with here's your template", then generates work for local miners from it [00:27:34]. On StartOS the install "will configure not to do things that datam requires mainly is uh this block notify thing" (not = Knots, per caption) [00:04:46] — earlier than Dylan's August 2026 experience above, so the automation looks release-dependent. New-block handoff runs over block notify, which the speaker called the right tool and ZMQ "broken": "the longer that takes, the more you're wasting electricity for no reason" — without it, miners can burn "like 30 seconds" on stale work [00:05:18-00:05:50].
- What template selection actually rests on, per the same talk: "when you're constructing block templates, everything that's in the block is informed by what the underlying node has for its mele policy. And contrary to popular belief, this is not just completely wide open" (mele = mempool, per caption) — "nodes are far more restrictive than just consensus" [00:00:34]. Defaults cited: data carrier size "set to 83 bytes" in Core and "it's set by default to 42 bytes" in Knots (per caption, with the speaker's spoken correction mid-sentence), which is why "some samurai stuff didn't make it into our templates" when Ocean launched [00:00:34-00:01:05]; an attendee tied the BRC-20 fight to "43 bytes allowed BRC20" [00:03:12].
- The proxy argument for gateways: a pool pushing work updates to "like 10,000 ant miners" over one bad connection (Starlink sites in Ethiopia/Paraguay) is the problem; with a local DATUM the pool sends one work update, the gateway "generates the work locally" and "It just connects over your land to all the miners", cutting latency to "tens of milliseconds rather than hundreds" [00:07:58-00:10:02] [00:41:16]. Same mechanism explains the empty-block speed-up — work without a merkle tree arrives faster, then the full template follows; the three empty blocks Ocean found in a row had "the statistical likelihood of that was almost zero" [00:08:29-00:09:30].
- Division of labor when mining upstream: the gateway "reaches out to Ocean and asks for a Coinbase split", and "every share you send back upstream to Ocean will qualify for reward splits from everyone else on Ocean" — "all Ocean is doing is it's just a a coordinator"; "That's all a pool ever should do and that's all Ocean actually does" [00:42:22-00:42:54]. Adoption per the same talk: "like 90% of the mining on ocean is datam now" [00:42:54].
- The coinbase-output feature is the decentralization payload: "what DATM does is it allows um Coinbase with multiple uh outputs in it rather than just the pool paying themselves which is an important aspect of decentralizing this stuff" [00:31:50-00:32:20]. Coinbase sizing is fingerprint-driven per miner: old Antminer firmware and LuxOS get "16 outputs", new Antminer firmware gets "70 or 80 miners getting paid out in the block", Bitaxes "can handle enormous coin bases" [00:14:14-00:19:24]; Bitmain had the 16-output fix internally and released it about a week after the public tweet — "they were selling defective shovels to people competing with them to dig" [00:15:15].
- The SV2 critique from the same stage: "Stratum v2 is the kitchen sync protocol and it's doing a lot of things and um, template construction on the minor side was an afterthought" [00:29:12]; the job negotiator "mimics what Datam does, but it doesn't support uh Coinbase payouts where you split and pay all the miners in the actual block itself" [00:30:14]; "the champions of Stratam v2 are not moving in the direction of miners controlling blocks. In fact, they're doing the opposite"; "The pool that's kind of taken SV2 under its wing, as far as I can tell, doesn't even make its own blocks as a pool. It uses Bitmain to do that" [00:30:14-00:30:44]. DATUM deliberately stays on unencrypted SV1 miner-side ("your local area network is secure"), which is also why no Bitmain DATUM firmware was ever needed; DATUM-to-Ocean traffic is encrypted [00:30:44-00:31:50]. He still grants "SV2 is strictly better apart from one exception", but "Bitmain has zero interest in doing it as far as I can tell" and he is not "waiting for hell to freeze over" for it [00:31:17-00:31:50].
- It is an open protocol, not an Ocean product: "In theory, Foundry can come along and support DATM tomorrow"; "Anyone can support DATM. It's an open protocol" [00:32:20-00:32:51]. Ocean nonetheless runs DATUM gateways on all its own servers and charges more ("we will charge you more fees") to those who use Ocean's centralized templates instead — the "50% discount" is the adoption carrot [00:27:02, 00:32:51].
- Lineage, per the audience Q&A: Ocean is "basically a revival of Eligious" (auto-caption spelling) — "one of the first pools", which "ran for six years with all the servers sitting in Jason's bedroom" carrying "20% of the network hash rate"; much of today's miner firmware traces to code Luke wrote years ago [00:28:40-00:29:12].
- Template quality is not just fee-sorting: construction is "a computer science problem. It's called the knapsack problem", which is why "sometimes Oceans templates with spam filtration pay more than core templates" — "It's people intuitively think that would never happen, but it does all the time" [00:50:02-00:50:33].

## The custody problem in PPLNS

- Problem statement: PPLNS pools must custody funds when payouts don't fit in the coinbase; options debated — instant Lightning payouts, atomic swaps of shares for BTC, federated ecash custody [#1560 · 2025-01-31 · jungly 🐕💸] [#1561 · 2025-01-31 · jungly 🐕💸]
- Federated-mint idea: biggest hashers become federation members; combine with Super Testnet's bitpac contracts so a multisig treasury of top contributors governs payouts [#1562 · 2025-01-31 · Karl] [#1564 · 2025-01-31 · Karl]

## Bitmain I2C & level-shifting archaeology

- S17 hashboards have footprints for level-shifting opamps but they were never populated — 'Seems like Bitmain was concerned'; S19jPros have no level shifters, which makes sense because the IO voltage is much higher than the domain voltage [#2395 · 2025-06-08 · Skot Bitaxe] [#2396 · 2025-06-08 · Skot Bitaxe]
- Most new ASICs have on-chip buffers making external level shifters unnecessary, but Bitmain ASICs still need them — their much higher core voltage (1.2V) [#2432 · 2025-06-10 · Jstefanop] [#2450 · 2025-06-10 · Skot Bitaxe]
- T17 schematic archaeology: the I2C diode temperature sensor is controlled by the ASIC (same on the L7); the S21's BM1368 put the whole measurement circuit internal on-chip, then the BM1370 dropped it [#2566 · 2025-06-30 · Skot Bitaxe] [#2567 · 2025-06-30 · Mæstro Juergen] [#2571 · 2025-06-30 · Skot Bitaxe]
- The S21 Pro has level-shifted I2C lines running the whole hashboard, with special power tabs carrying a little bridge so I2C clk/data run underneath [#2575 · 2025-06-30 · Skot Bitaxe]
- Domain-crossing theories: the 0xAA55 preamble may be charge-pumping the AC-coupled signal; if ASIC IO is in the microamp range, plain dividers with 33 Ω resistors could do the level shifting [#2581 · 2025-06-30 · NebulaMiner] [#2590 · 2025-06-30 · Jstefanop]
- Relevant ASIC registers: 0x58 'IO Drive Strength' and 0x68 'UART Relay' (though neither seems able to change voltage) [#2587 · 2025-06-30 · Skot Bitaxe] [#2588 · 2025-06-30 · Skot Bitaxe]
- The S17 shows op-amp spots but the T17 has none — 'like Bitmain wasn't sure if it was going to work or not' [#2591 · 2025-06-30 · Mæstro Juergen] [#2592 · 2025-06-30 · Skot Bitaxe]

## GridPool (AgentP) — trustless share consensus (June 2026)

- GridPool added an SV1 endpoint for a 2% fee 'to encourage decentralization', running on an experimental fork of Hydrapool: stratum+tcp://stratum.main.gridpool.net:3333 [#4986 · 2026-06-19 · AgentP]; earlier admission: 'not offering Stratum V1 turns a lot of people away at the door' [#4955 · 2026-06-18 · AgentP]
- No sharechain to attack: 'Not possible on Gridpool, there is no sharechain to 51% attack' — it would give no advantage and disadvantage no one [#4991 · 2026-06-19 · AgentP] [#4992 · 2026-06-19 · AgentP]
- Consensus = the "heaviest Winners List" rule: a Winners List of 15 highest-difficulty sharers per template; nodes gossip, trustlessly verify other lists' share proofs (every PoW share with its Slot-0 attribution and headers), total the difficulty, and switch to the strongest list — analogous to Bitcoin's heaviest-chain rule [#5002 · 2026-06-25 · AgentP]
- Compact Share Relay: shares reduced to reconstruction info (Slot-0 address, nonce, time, version) <1200 bytes → single UDP packet, FIBRE-style; mitigates pool splits from last-second shares; untested as of June 2026 [#5003 · 2026-06-25 · AgentP]
- Censorship-resistance argument: a censoring node works on a lower-difficulty list, so even 99%-hashrate censorship should lose economically (author flags it needs more careful gaming) [#5004 · 2026-06-25 · AgentP]
- Pool-hopping rebuttal: the block finder keeps Slot-0 + all transaction fees on their own templates, so a lucky miner's rational move is to point more power at GridPool, not leave [#5000 · 2026-06-25 · AgentP]

- Launch & architecture (forum, 2026-06-16): landing page at gridpool.net; miners either send hash to the demo node with their own DATUM client or run their own GridPool node ([GridPool landing thread](../../raw/pools/hydrapool/2026-06-16-forum-new-gridpool-landing-page-is-up.md))
- Protocol Version 2: Jason Hughes argued no large miner would give up 50% of the reward; in response GridPool's Version 2 syncs the active payout list with the best-shares list more often, so new miners get paid sooner ([GridPool landing thread](../../raw/pools/hydrapool/2026-06-16-forum-new-gridpool-landing-page-is-up.md))
- Evidence gap: another developer said there was no supporting data for the network-modelling, bandwidth and latency claims; AgentP accepted it and put network performance testing high on his list ([GridPool landing thread](../../raw/pools/hydrapool/2026-06-16-forum-new-gridpool-landing-page-is-up.md))
- 1-click packages (2026-09-15): Umbrel and StartOS installs (StartOS v0.2.2-beta.7, Umbrel v0.2.2-beta.9) — paste a payout address and join the network; pitched as no pool operator, no custodial wallet, no sharechain, no central share accountant, templates kept hidden; **SV2 only for the beta**; the 256 Foundation Red Team (Schnitzel) found key vulnerabilities fixed before release ([GridPool packages](../../raw/pools/hydrapool/2026-09-15-forum-gridpool-1-click-packages-are-released.md))

POD256 deep dive (May-June 2026): Iko read the site's own summary on the pod ([POD256 e115](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-05-27-e115.md)), then Agent P gave a full interview ([POD256 e116](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-10-e116.md)). Mechanics from those episodes:

- Winners list & slot zero: nodes maintain a "winners list" of 299 shared payout slots; "Slot zero always belongs to the block finder, so a full template has 300 total payout slots" ([POD256 e115](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-05-27-e115.md)). Miners build their own templates from that coinbase, insert their own payout address in slot zero for one share, and submit high-difficulty shares; the on-deck list holds the best shares vying for the next round, and "when they see a real Bitcoin block added to the chain tip that pays out the winners list" the on-deck list becomes the next winners list ([POD256 e116](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-10-e116.md)).
- Trust without encryption: the share message is a "totally unencrypted HTTP JSON message"; that's safe because a tampered coinbase invalidates the nonce, so altered shares fail the difficulty check and get trashed ([POD256 e116](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-10-e116.md)).
- Fairness by construction: payouts are equal slots ranked by share difficulty, not share counts; at half the pool's hashrate "statistically you're going to win 50% of the payout slots", and a big miner's slots collapse into one address when the coinbase is constructed ([POD256 e116](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-10-e116.md)).
- No sharechain: the difficulty-sorted design "does not require a share chain", so latency is "No bigger of a problem than in Bitcoin" — the core simplification versus P2Pool's stream of shares ([POD256 e116](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-10-e116.md)).
- Small-miner cadence: at a "300 P2H" pool a 1 PH miner lands a slot about monthly; double the pool to "600 P2H" and blocks come every two weeks but slots are twice as contested, so a small miner still waits "300 times shorter than if you were pure solo" ([POD256 e116](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-10-e116.md)).
- Withholding resistance & capacity: slot-zero self-crediting doubles as "resistance to block withholding attacks"; up to 300 addresses can be paid at once — "Two ninety nine and then the zero slot" ([POD256 e116](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-10-e116.md)).
- Fees: "my hope is there's no fees on this. 0% fees" — the target user is the mid-sized pool stuck in the dead zone "when you're not getting a block more than every week or so", while mega miners stay on managed pools like Foundry for "compliance and SOC two" reasons ([POD256 e116](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-10-e116.md)).
- Why 300 slots: 150 was considered ("if you go less than 300, we could have done a 150") but finer payouts would fill blocks with coinbase outputs, and "a lot of minor firmware is broken and cannot handle Coinbase transactions that are too large" — "It's Antminers that can't handle these large coin base", which he calls incentive to fix firmware rather than shrink the list ([POD256 e116](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-10-e116.md)).
- Bootstrap: "just like Bitcoin, there's there's hard coded addresses"; he wants committed runners for "stable, well connected nodes", has a Docker script for self-hosting, and is testing real round cycling on Bitcoin testnet4 ([POD256 e116](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-10-e116.md)).
- Frontend: gridpool.net just mirrors Agent P's own node — "His worker name is gridpool.net" ([POD256 e115](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-05-27-e115.md)) — and "Anyone can run their own node" ([POD256 e116](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-10-e116.md)).
- Hydrapool integration: he forked Hydrapool (and P2Pool v2, for the custom payout list) so a Hydrapool node submits its shares to your GridPool node and gets the winners list back to construct the coinbase; the PR is in and jungly is "supportive of it" but "wants to go a slightly different route" ([POD256 e116](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-10-e116.md)).
- Trivial clients: because the protocol is "just a couple of HTTP JSON messages", he vibe-coded a Bitaxe (ESP-Miner) client that builds templates via RPC to his node and speaks HTTP JSON to his GridPool node with no stratum layer at all; next integration targets: CKPool, Public Pool, Stratum V2, and Atlas Pool (itself a CKPool fork) ([POD256 e116](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-10-e116.md)).
- Live pending winner (May 2026): had a block landed then, the 256 Foundation donation address was in line to collect "0.46 BTC" ([POD256 e115](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-05-27-e115.md)).

## Why pools stay centralized (April 2026)

- An audience question at BitBlock Boom put the pool side of the problem on the pod: a heater miner is physically decentralized, there's "the physical decentralization", but "that's not really the big problem" — "If everyone just points to their FPPS pool, what's the answer?" ([POD256 e112](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-04-15-e112.md))
- Eco's April 2026 stocktake: template-selection pools "help", but "we do not have a complete solution that really decentralizes pool mining" — and despite what pool marketing claims, "we do not have the complete solution in my mind"; P2Poolv2 and Hydrapool are named as the in-development attempts ([POD256 e112](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-04-15-e112.md))
- Centralization is partly baked into the concept: "what even the term pool means is we're pooling all our resources together under one roof" — pools centralize naturally for payout-smoothing reasons ([POD256 e112](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-04-15-e112.md))
- Tyler's stake in the ground: if heater miners win and people run miners at home for the heat, "we still haven't won if they're all pointing them at centralized pools" — a GE-scale appliance maker could "become the next bitmain" the same way ([POD256 e112](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-04-15-e112.md))
- The Hydrapool answer in two steps: one-click deploys (Ansible/AWS, Start9 and Umbrel packaging on the roadmap) make pools "very difficult to stop" for a government wanting to crack down; then multiple Hydrapool instances coordinate "through something like P2Pool V2", which he called "about as good as it's gonna get for the foreseeable future" ([POD256 e112](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-04-15-e112.md))
- The bootstrapping constraint behind all of it: even PPLNS shared pools need "exahash level miners pointed at it, otherwise your users aren't gonna get paid out with any kind of frequency"; one workaround being explored for unbootstrapped pools is vnprc's "futures markets for the share" ([POD256 e112](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-04-15-e112.md))
- Money followed the problem: "local hosts just raised some money for P2Pool development" — outside the 256 Foundation — funding "a full time contributor alongside Jungly" on P2Poolv2 ([POD256 e112](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-04-15-e112.md))
- June addendum after the Agent P episode: "there's only like 15 pools listed on that pie chart" on mempool.space, so the pool definition itself is up for grabs — "Nothing's stopping us from just redefining pool to be a protocol solution, not a company solution"; the centralization risks span pools, hardware, firmware and mining rewards flowing to "a handful of custodians" ([POD256 e117](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-17-e117.md))
- The HPS 2026 payout panel added Ocean's own bootstrap offer: "you can use ocean as a parent pool for a lot of bootstrapping purposes for smaller pools", with the pool's proper role framed as "a dumb transparent coordination layer" — "It's not meant to be a thing that pays you bitcoins that don't exist. It's not meant to be a thing that acts as a bank account. And these are what pools have become these days" ([HPS 2026 Talk 14](../../raw/history/heatpunks-summit/2026/20260305-hps-2026-your-heat-hash-rules-pool-payout-sovereignty-talk-14.md) [00:03:50-00:05:29])

## The 2026 pool landscape (April-August 2026)

## Ecash share pools (Hashpool / Axepool)

- Hashpool issues an ehash ecash token for every submitted share, uses ecash epochs to age shares in FIFO order, expires them with a rotating key set, and publishes verification proofs per epoch and per solved block; payouts resemble PPLNS ([Assembling Freedom, 2025-05](../../raw/history/256f-newsletter/2025-05-05-bitcoin-mining-will-not-be-decentralized-until-it-is-open-so.md))
- Hashpool and Axepool run ecash shares over a modified Stratum V2, Axepool being a proxy layer for small miners such as Bitaxes; both funded in OpenSats' Twelfth Wave (2025-07-08) ([grants research](../../raw/history/256f-research/2026-10-07-research-proto-braidpool-canaan-grants.md))
- Hydrapool lists ecash (echash) payout models among its long-term goals ([hydrapool.org](../../raw/pools/hydrapool/hydrapool-org-home.md)); see also the custody discussion above
- Live pitch at HPS 2026 (Talk 14): Evan of VN PRC (per his on-stage self-introduction) presented hashpool.dev — a "cippher punk mining pool" (per caption) treating shares as ecash so that "You can self-custody your own hash rate shares", against existing stacks where a share is "a record in the mining pool database" and "essentially, they custody your hash rate"; he is "funded by OpenST and HRF" (OpenSats and HRF, per caption) ([HPS 2026 Talk 14](../../raw/history/heatpunks-summit/2026/20260305-hps-2026-your-heat-hash-rules-pool-payout-sovereignty-talk-14.md) [00:00:33-00:02:48])
- The layered-stack idea from the same talk: small community pools direct hashrate to an upstream pool that finds the blocks — "you need to find Bitcoin blocks in order to get payouts", "a threshold, a barrier to entry" — so layering scales pools down while pools "erect a privacy barrier", since the upstream pool no longer sees each miner's IP, hashrate and payout addresses [00:01:42-00:02:14]
- Verification roadmap: "part of the hashpool road map is fully verifiable mining pool operations" — a "Merkel sum tree" (per caption) over all shares lets miners check their e-hash against issued rewards; the gap is that "No firmware does this" (miners don't store the shares they sent), hence the Hash Scope counting proxy; the panel's call to action: "ask all your pools where is the a public API to verify my shares" — if "20 or 30%" of miners verify, cheating gets caught [00:23:41-00:25:38]
- Payout R&D direction: Lightning felt like "hammering in a nail with a wrench" for one-directional pool payouts; the next phase is ARC — VTXOs that accumulate until the miner broadcasts them on-chain and exits "without permission from anyone"; "It's an ideal tool for mining pool payouts"; "maybe we'll get a hydropool ocean" [00:12:51-00:14:38]
