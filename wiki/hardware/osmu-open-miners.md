The Bitaxe is the best-known open home miner, but the OSMU wiki documents a wider family of open hardware and firmware that grew up beside it. Three lineages matter: the **NerdMiner** line (an ESP32 mining solo on software alone, grown into display hybrids around Bitaxe-class silicon), the **PiAxe/QAxe** line (a Raspberry Pi HAT and its quad-chip spin-off, controlled by external software), and the **BitForge Nano** (a standalone dual-BM1370 unit with its own firmware). The community keeps a canonical registry of all of them, the bitaxeorg/foss-miner-list, which is also where the board-number convention used across this family is written down ([foss-miner-list](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-foss-miner-list.md)). For the core Bitaxe line itself, see [Bitaxe Models & Open-Source Miner Family](bitaxe-models.md).

## The NerdMiner line

The Nerdminer is the entry point: a free and open source project whose stated goal is to let you "try to reach a bitcoin block with a small piece of hardware", learn about mining, and have a nice object on the desk. It descends from Valerio Vaccaro's HAN Miner and is an implementation of the Stratum protocol on the ESP32 microcontroller, mining solo by default against Public Pool. It began on ESP32-S3 and now supports other boards, configures over WiFiManager to SPIFFS, and is genuinely multicore: both ESP32 cores hash while other threads handle stratum and Wi-Fi, updating work on every stratum job notification to avoid stale shares. Three built-in screens ship by default — NerdMiner (own mining data), ClockMiner (a clock that mines), and GlobalStats (network stats) ([OSMU wiki: NerdMiner](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-nerdminer-about.md)).

The line then grows by attaching real hashing silicon:

- **NerdNOS** is an add-on board for a Nerdminer that lifts its hashrate to 150~200 GH/s using a BM1397 (the Bitaxe Max chip), deliberately underclocked to draw about 8W. Setup runs through the miner's own access point: connect to "NerdMinerAP" (password `MineYourCoins`), enter your Bitcoin address in the WiFiManager page, and monitor the address on the pool. Button 1 switches displays (hold while powering on to edit entries, 5 seconds to factory-reset), button 2 toggles the display without stopping mining, and the board wants a USB port rated for at least 2A ([OSMU wiki: NerdNOS](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-nerdnos-about.md)). WantClue confirmed on the forum that the NerdNOS also exposes its own API — general information at `ip/api/status` and configuration at `ip/api/config` — which is how the Hashlink app reads it ([NerdNOS & Hashkink thread](../../raw/history/osmu-forum/2026-06-04-forum-nerdnos-hashkink.md)).
- **NerdAxe** pairs the Nerdminer display with Bitaxe Ultra hardware: a modified Bitaxe Ultra board (BM1366) plus the Lilygo T-Display S3, talking to the ASIC over GPIO pins and reaching 500GH/s — the Ultra-class number. It runs its own ESP-Miner fork, ESP-Miner-NerdAxe ([OSMU wiki: NerdAxe](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-nerdaxe-about.md)).
- **NerdQAxe+** scales the same display-plus-logic idea to four ASIC chips on a modified QAxe+ board, up to 2.5TH/s, still with the Lilygo T-Display S3 and shufps' modified AxeOS ([OSMU wiki: NerdQAxe+](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-nerdqaxeplus-about.md)).
- **NerdQAxe++** moves to four BM1370 chips (the Antminer S21 series part) at up to 4.8 TH/s on roughly 60 watts — among the most efficient home miners — in a silent, compact package ([OSMU wiki: NerdQAxe++](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-nerdqaxeplusplus-about.md)).

One spec conflict is live in the sources: the OSMU wiki's NerdQAxe+ page says four BM1366 chips, while foss-miner-list lists the NerdQAxe+ as four BM1368 ([OSMU wiki: NerdQAxe+](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-nerdqaxeplus-about.md); [foss-miner-list](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-foss-miner-list.md)).

## PiAxe and QAxe

The PiAxe takes a different shape: a Raspberry Pi HAT carrying one BM1366, controlled over the Pi's GPIO pins, powered by 12V, and running the Python-based piaxe-miner software on the Pi. Its hashrate matches a Bitaxe Ultra at around 500GH/s. The board work emphasizes hand assembly — smallest parts are 0805 size — with a TVS diode and fuses added and a revised buck regulator; an LM75-compatible temperature sensor is on board ([OSMU wiki: PiAxe](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-piaxe-about.md)). OSMU hosts step-by-step assembly and building guides for it ([OSMU wiki: PiAxe assembly](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-piaxe-assembly.md); [building](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-piaxe-building.md)).

The QAxe extends the PiAxe to four BM1366 chips on an STM32-controlled board, with three revisions: rev1 tested at about 1.8TH/s average, rev2 reaching the same after minor fixes, and rev3 fixing misplaced 330µF caps and adding a boot switch. It is a PiAxe/Bitaxe hybrid design ([OSMU wiki: QAxe](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-qaxe-about.md)). Two build details are worth knowing before assembling one:

- You can run a QAxe with a single ASIC installed and work up to all four; cover the pads of unpopulated ASIC positions before heatsinking and testing ([OSMU wiki: QAxe assembly](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-qaxe-assembly.md)).
- Flashing changed for the better: the STM32L072CB variant has an integrated DFU bootloader entered by holding the BOOT button during reset, after which dfu-utils flashes the firmware. The older CMSIS-DAP path (Picoprobe on a Raspberry Pi Pico, or shufps' three-part low-cost DAP board) is explicitly deprecated as "quite a hassle" for one-time flashers; rev3 boards were supposed to make DFU entry universal ([OSMU wiki: QAxe installation](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-qaxe-installation.md)).

Both PiAxe and QAxe depend on a separate computer running piaxe-miner — neither is standalone ([OSMU wiki: QAxe](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-qaxe-about.md)).

## BitForge Nano

The BitForge Nano is the standalone entrant: a dual-BM1370 miner developed by WantClue with manufacturer DTV Electronics (board designed in Germany, produced by DTV), with the case, custom heatsink and airflow CFD contributed by "I Am GPIO" of The Solo Mining Co. An ESP32-S3-WROOM-1 manages mining, cooling and connectivity; the unit is Wi-Fi only, with no Ethernet. It runs ForgeOS, an open-source firmware with a browser dashboard for pool configuration, monitoring and tuning. Three performance profiles span roughly 2.0 TH/s (eco) to about 2.6 TH/s (performance) at approximately 40W — around 15 J/TH — cooled by two 40×20mm 12V PWM fans at measured noise around 30 dB ([OSMU wiki: BitForge](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-bitforge-bitforge.md)). The case files are public, too: dvb-projekt showed a 3D-printed Nocuta Edition case in original Nocuta PLA (caramel-coloured, printed via 3DPS24 from the BitForge nano .stl files) — the name plays on Noctua, but as Guepi noted it is not a Noctua edition ([BitForge nano Nocuta Edition thread](../../raw/history/osmu-forum/2026-06-03-forum-bitforge-nano-nocuta-edition.md)).

## Firmware forks and the web flasher

The family maintains its own firmware forks rather than patching ESP-Miner itself, and the fork registry in foss-miner-list is the map: ESP-Miner (AxeOS) for Bitaxes, ESP-Miner-NerdAxe for the NerdAxe single-chip variants, shufps' ESP-Miner-NerdQAxePlus for NerdQAxe+/++ and Octaxe-class boards, ESP-Miner-TCH for the TinyChipHub 70x boards, and ForgeOS for the BitForge Nano ([foss-miner-list](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-foss-miner-list.md)). Since 2026-09-28 the family also has WarpAxe, the first firmware written from scratch in Rust rather than forked from ESP-Miner (below).

The official Bitaxe web flasher has a complicated relationship with these forks. In January 2025 shufps' PR #16 "Nerdaxified" the flasher — replacing its Bitaxe entries with Nerd*axe variants ([web-flasher #16](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2025-01-27-github-bitaxe-web-flasher-pr-16-nerdaxified.md)) — but coverage is not uniform: a NerdQAxe++ owner found the flasher reported a successful update while the firmware stayed on 1.0.33, and the resolution was that the NerdQAxe is "a different repo" the Bitaxe flasher does not serve ([web-flasher #27](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2025-11-19-github-bitaxe-web-flasher-issue-27-nerdqaxe-not-updateable.md)). Practical rule: flash Nerd-family boards from their own firmware repos unless the flasher explicitly lists the variant.

The newest entry does not fork ESP-Miner at all: **WarpAxe**, by forum member dvb-projekt, is a firmware for the Bitaxe Gamma written from scratch in Rust (GPLv3). It replaces AxeOS on the device and speaks the same HTTP API, so tuners, dashboards and fleet tools that talk to AxeOS keep working. Its stated differences: signed online updates (the miner fetches a new release and checks its signature before installing it), one image for all supported boards with the board detected on first start and Wi-Fi/pool settings carried over from AxeOS, Stratum V1 with optional TLS plus Stratum V2 with a fallback pool, a web UI in 8 languages with a Swarm page that shows every miner on your LAN (AxeOS, NerdOS, ForgeOS or WarpAxe), and chip, chain and board drivers exercised in CI against a simulated chain and I²C bus. The author is explicit that AxeOS is still ahead on board coverage and maturity, plus features WarpAxe does not have yet: scoreboard, themes, the BAP accessory port and Wi-Fi setup over Bluetooth ([WarpAxe Gamma thread](../../raw/history/osmu-forum/2026-09-28-forum-warpaxe-open-source-rust-firmware-for-the-bitaxe-gamma-early-feedback-welcome.md)). Status is early: v0.1.6 at introduction on 2026-09-28, mining 24/7 on his Gamma 602 at about 1.33 TH/s and 17.7 J/TH, installed over USB from the browser, with a one-flash return to AxeOS via the official Bitaxe flasher.

By v0.1.24 (last release post 2026-10-09) it had grown well past the Gamma, and the thread doubles as a running hardware-findings log:

- **Boards.** With v0.1.7 it also mines on the NerdQAxe++ Rev 6.1 (24/7 since 25 September, on Stratum V2) and the NerdOctaxe Rev 3.1 (6-phase TPS53667) — at 700 MHz / 1210 mV about 11.2 TH/s at 213 W (19 J/TH), on par with NerdOS; on first start it takes Wi-Fi, pools, clock/voltage, fan settings, temperature limits and display orientation from NerdOS ([WarpAxe NerdQAxe++/NerdOctaxe thread](../../raw/history/osmu-forum/2026-09-28-forum-warpaxe-open-source-rust-firmware-now-runs-on-the-nerdqaxe-and-nerdoctaxe.md)). v0.1.9 paced Nerd-board work like NerdOS (every 500 ms) to close an ~8 % gap to 11.59 vs 11.71 TH/s at the pool on the NerdOctaxe, and runs the NerdAxe Gaia and the BitForge Nano — the Nano at 2.19 TH/s on its Standard preset vs 2.10 on ForgeOS. v0.1.10 added profiles for the Gamma Duo (650), Gamma Turbo (801), Naja Duo (1201) and Gamma Hex (1300), none yet run on hardware at that point, with the warning that v0.1.9 had taken a Duo for a plain Gamma because board version 650 lies inside the Gamma's 600 range — Duo owners need v0.1.10 or later. v0.1.20 runs the same image on a LilyGO T-Display-S3 (NerdMiner hardware), hashing on the ESP32-S3 itself at about 290 kH/s, and v0.1.22 added the NerdNOS (BM1397): at 300 MHz the pool counted about 190 GH/s with WarpAxe against 180 on its stock firmware.
- **Chip findings.** v0.1.13 documented that the BM1373 really has eight hash-domain counters — four at 0x88–0x8B as on the BM1370 and four more at 0xAC–0xAF, each counting an eighth of the 0x8C total; AxeOS reads the first four and doubles them, WarpAxe reads all eight. v0.1.15 found the TPS546 keeps its output on through an ESP reset — three minutes in download mode on a BitForge Nano measured 82 °C chip / 95 °C regulator — so WarpAxe cuts core voltage before every restart, and its web flasher saves a full flash backup before installing and can restore it. v0.1.16 bounds the regulator: the TPS546 only regulates accurately (and only warns on undervoltage) between 0.25 and 0.75 V at its reference — a clean output of 0.25–0.75 V divided by VOUT_SCALE_LOOP, i.e. 1–3 V on the 0.25 most boards use — so WarpAxe never commands a core voltage below that and publishes the floor as `regulatorMinMv`.
- **Init divergence.** v0.1.24 starts the BM1370 on Nerd boards (NerdQAxe++, NerdOctaxe, NerdQX) with NerdOS's chip init instead of ESP-Miner's: ESP-Miner changed a core-clock trim in August (register 0x0C to 0x10), which left one hash domain at about 3 % of its rate on his NerdOctaxe at 700 MHz until a power cycle; with the NerdOS value it came up clean 6 times out of 6. Bitaxes keep ESP-Miner's init.

## foss-miner-list as the family registry

The bitaxeorg/foss-miner-list README is the closest thing the ecosystem has to a canonical index, and its hardware table shows the family extends beyond any single org: NerdAxe Ultra (1x BM1366) and NerdAxe Gamma (1x BM1370) under BitMaker-hub; the NerdQAxe line under BitMaker-hub and shufps; a community NerdQAxe++ rev.7 under benjamin-wilson; NerdOCTAXE-Plus (8x BM1368) and NerdOCTAXE-Gamma (8x BM1370) under Patsch91; the TinyChipHub Supra Hex and Gamma Hex forks; and the WantClue BitForge Nano ([foss-miner-list](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-foss-miner-list.md)).

The family registry only indexes firmware forks, but the hardware itself gets forked too, and the forum is where those one-off variants surface. TDX33029 has open-sourced a modified 6-ASIC version of the Bitaxe: having a large stock of STM32 chips on hand, he opted to use Ethernet to control multiple Bitaxe units rather than having them operate independently via ESP32s — a single PC can control up to 220 Bitaxe units, monitoring temperature, real-time hashrate, submitted shares and rejection rates, with remote power control of individual units. The PC takes over the tasks previously handled by the ESP32s, managing pool connectivity and job distribution, so all you need is a network switch and the necessary Ethernet cables. He rewrote the firmware for the different chips and functionality, modified the power supply scheme and removed certain components to further reduce costs, and reports both the hardware and the software as tested and fully operational, with all files open-source in the TDX33029/BTCMinerControl repository (an English README is provided) ([modified 6-ASIC Bitaxe thread](../../raw/history/osmu-forum/2026-09-26-forum-a-modified-6-asic-version-of-bitaxe-open-sources.md)).

## Pools and tools from the OSMU forum

The forum is also where the ecosystem's pool and tooling projects get announced, and several are built explicitly around OSMU hardware:

- **dvb-WarpPool** (dvb-projekt, the WarpAxe author) is an open-source, self-hosted solo Bitcoin pool written from scratch in Rust. Its signature feature is hardware-aware VarDiff: the pool reads the miner's user agent and starts every device in the right difficulty league — eight classes, down to a NerdMiner V2 getting sub-1 difficulty from the first share — and difficulty survives reconnects and pool restarts. It speaks Stratum V1 (plain and TLS) and Stratum V2; on the public pool.warppool.org instance ports 3333, 3334 and 3336 serve V1, V1-over-TLS and Sv2. Since v1.16.0 it can also pull block templates directly over Bitcoin Core 31's IPC interface, which measured about 5–12 ms per template versus roughly 90–100 ms over the classic GBT+ZMQ route. The license is AGPLv3 (v1.6.0; originally MIT/Apache-2.0). The project's operations history is a case study in self-sovereignty: on 2026-07-09 GitHub permanently suspended the account after its abuse review classified the project's CI usage as "incentivized activities / general computing", and the complete history — including all 50 release tags — moved 1:1 to the project's own Forgejo instance at git.warppool.org ([dvb-WarpPool thread](../../raw/history/osmu-forum/2026-06-10-forum-dvb-warppool-open-source-solo-mining-pool-written-in-rust-umbrel-macos-windows-linux.md)). Its companion dvb-WarpTune auto-clock sidecar, which holds AxeOS miners at a temperature target by stepping frequency and voltage along the board's own ladder, ran a week-long live test on a Gamma (2026-09-11 → 09-18, 991 samples) with zero interventions at 1306.8 GH/s average against a 1305.6 GH/s baseline, 22.8 W, 17.5 J/TH; its voltage calibration took a BitForge Nano's Max step from 52.1 W to 43.7 W (−16 %) with the pool's hashrate unchanged.
- **Pogolo** (0xf0xx0) is a db-less solo mining pool designed to serve your LAN swarm, loosely based on public-pool and written in Go, configured through a well-documented TOML file. v1.1.4 (2026-07-07) added a ZMQ hashblock listener for rapid block-change updates; v1.1.6 (2026-09-14) brought native Stratum V2 support. Ongoing Sv2 work includes share batching into windows (by default, 15 seconds) before the ack is sent, a backend sync wait before the template loop starts, and a static authority key generated with `pogolo --generate-authority-key`. The author considers TLS out of scope for the LAN and recommends a reverse proxy instead, pointing anyone running a pool on the public web toward m45core ([Pogolo thread](../../raw/history/osmu-forum/2026-05-30-forum-pogolo-local-golang-pool.md)).
- **BTC PoW Lab Pool** (Carlos Monzon, Power CM Software) is a Bitcoin pool with a Hybrid Solo model: a found block reward is allocated as 85% to the finder, 10% to Community Reward and 5% to BTC PoW Lab, with community eligibility based on accepted work within a rolling seven-day window (the finder is excluded from the Community Reward for their own block). On openness the author is direct: "The production pool code is not currently published as an open-source release." What is public is the reward rules, telemetry and verification data, plus a separate browser-local Share Work Audit tool — which he stresses is not the pool engine ([BTC PoW Lab Pool thread](../../raw/history/osmu-forum/2026-09-06-forum-btc-pow-lab-pool-hybrid-solo-accounting-and-accepted-work-telemetry.md)).
- **Bitaxe Monitor** (magicmikemirror) is a privacy-first local dashboard for Bitaxe and AxeOS miners. It polls the local AxeOS API and stores metrics locally, so hashrate, temperatures, power, voltage, shares, best difficulty and events remain available independently of the miner itself; it runs in Docker and is packaged for ZimaOS, with no cloud service required ([Bitaxe Monitor thread](../../raw/history/osmu-forum/2026-09-16-forum-bitaxe-monitor-local-monitoring-and-history-for-axeos.md)).
