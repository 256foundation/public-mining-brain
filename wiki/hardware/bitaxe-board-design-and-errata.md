## Overview

A Bitaxe is a small open-source board that wraps one or more Bitmain (or Intel) mining ASICs with everything a full Antminer control board and PSU would otherwise provide: a core-voltage regulator, an ESP32-S3 controller, a temperature/fan controller, level shifters for the ASIC's low-voltage serial bus, and a power input. Almost every hard lesson in three years of bitaxeorg hardware issues falls into four buckets: **power** (the core regulator — first a TPS40305 with a current DAC, now a PMBus-programmable TI TPS546D24 — and the extremely narrow 5V input window), **thermal sensing** (reading an undocumented on-die diode through an EMC2101 and tuning it until readings make sense), **board errata** (wrong BOM part numbers, footprints, missing pull-ups/pull-downs, strapping resistors), and **manufacturability** (heatsink mounting, PCB warping, pad sizes, parts availability). This page is the builder's reference for those topics; for symptom-driven fixes see [Bitaxe Troubleshooting](../getting-started/bitaxe-troubleshooting.md), and for the model lineup see [Bitaxe Models](bitaxe-models.md). Hardware repo issues are not a support desk: buyers should contact their seller and DIY builders the OSMU Discord ([bitaxeGamma #29](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2025-03-14-github-bitaxegamma-issue-29-bitaxe-gamma-not-hashing.md)).

## Power architecture

### Regulator generations

| Generation | Boards | Core regulator & telemetry | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prototype | Original bitaxe (2x BM1387) | 5V input regulated down to 1.8V and 0.8V rails; ASICs in series from a VIN of about 1.6V | Experimental; no serial response at the 2022 README snapshot ([BM1387 prototype README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2022-05-26-bitaxe-bm1387-prototype-readme.md)) |
| TPS40305 era | Max (BM1397), Ultra up to 205, Supra 40x | TI TPS40305 buck from 5V; Maxim DS4432U+ current DAC sets core voltage (0.04V to 2.4V); TI INA260 power meter; EMC2101 temp + fan | ([bitaxeMax README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-bitaxemax.md)) |
| TPS546 era | Gamma 600+ (from Supra 402 hardware), Ultra 207, Hex 302+, GT, Gamma Hex | TPS546 with integrated switches; INA260 and DS4432U+ removed — the regulator itself reports voltage, current and temperature over PMBus | gamma-600 was first ([hardware release records](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/bitaxe-hardware-release-records.md)); ultra-207 moved the Ultra over ([bitaxeUltra releases](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeultra-releases.md)) |
| Multi-phase | GT (dual TPS546), Gamma Hex 1300 (four-phase TPS546D24S) | Phases share load for 12V multi-ASIC boards | ([bitaxeGT README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-bitaxegt.md)); ([Gamma Hex README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-bitaxegammahex.md)) |

Consequence for firmware: voltage-regulator temperature can only be read on TPS546 boards (Ultra 207+, Supra 402+, Hex 302+), so AxeOS simply hides the VR gauge when it reads 0 ([ESP-Miner #237](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2024-06-20-github-esp-miner-issue-237-hide-the-axeos-voltage-regulator.md)). The TPS546 measures only its own output power, so fan, display and ESP32 draw are not in the AxeOS power number ([ESP-Miner #701](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2025-02-12-github-esp-miner-issue-701-tps546-power-measurements-are-inc.md)), and AxeOS reads at the DC jack, not the wall — one user measured a Gamma at 14 W in AxeOS vs 24 W at a smart plug, attributed to brick inefficiency ([ESP-Miner #908](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2025-05-09-github-esp-miner-issue-908-actual-bitaxe-consumption-is-unde.md)).

### Series voltage domains (multi-ASIC boards)

Bitmain hashboards power ASICs in series: an S17 puts about 20V across roughly 13 BM1397s so each sees about 1.5V ([ESP-Miner #3](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2023-01-21-github-esp-miner-issue-3-adc-is-using-deprecated-driver-need.md)). The ultraHex copies this: six BM1366s in two domains of three, and since the BM1366 wants 1.2V core, three in series need a 3.6V regulator output — originally achieved by changing R10 from 1.54k to 2k in the TPS40305 feedback network, later by a redesigned regulator with a nominal 3.6V output ([ultraHex #11](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2023-11-25-github-ultrahex-issue-11-change-nominal-tps40305-output-to-3.md)). Design consequences:

- **Measuring a stacked voltage.** Up to 4.5V is too high for the ESP32 ADC, so a divider was added; 69.8K / 182K were chosen because those values were already on the BOM, giving 2.6V at VDD = 3.6V and 3.25V at VDD = 4.5V ([ultraHex #10](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2023-11-25-github-ultrahex-issue-10-core-voltage-is-too-high-for-esp32.md)). v304 added a divider on each domain so firmware can check balance ([ultraHex #36](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-05-15-github-ultrahex-issue-36-add-voltage-sensing-to-each-domain.md)), with further dividers because the top domain's sense line can exceed the ADC range ([ultraHex #42](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-06-06-github-ultrahex-issue-42-adc-inputs-for-domain-voltage-sense.md)).
- **Crossing domains.** The TLV3544 level shifter was originally powered between VDD and -VDD (typically 2.4V), below its 2.5V minimum; the fix powers it from 5V and GND ([ultraHex #30](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-04-22-github-ultrahex-issue-30-level-shifter-opamp-vs-is-outside-r.md)).
- **Why not skip the regulator?** 10 BM1366s at 1.2V each straight off 12V was rejected: ASICs are expensive, and real 12V supplies drift by 5% or so, which upsets per-domain voltage and kills the fine core-voltage control needed for tuning ([ultraHex #27](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-04-01-github-ultrahex-issue-27-document-efficiency-compared-to-oth.md)).
- **Where it's going.** The BitaxeGT plans to switch from parallel multi-phase to series-powered ASICs on a single-phase TPS546D24 ([bitaxeGT #15](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2025-09-22-github-bitaxegt-issue-15-switch-to-series-powered-asics.md)); BIRDS already runs four Intel BZM2s in series from a TPS546D24S tuned for 2.8V output @ 20A ([bitaxeBIRDS README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-bitaxebirds.md)).

### TPS546D24 configuration over PMBus

- **Strapping vs registers.** skot wants all TPS546D24 settings written to regulator RAM over PMBus rather than relying on pin-strapping resistors, and warned that the regulator's flash NVM has only about 1000 write cycles, so ON_OFF_CONFIG/STORE_USER_ALL must not be written blindly at every boot ([ESP-Miner #1394](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2025-11-25-github-esp-miner-issue-1394-write-all-tps546d24-settings-to.md)) ([ESP-Miner #1392](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2025-11-25-github-esp-miner-issue-1392-write-tps546-on-off-config-in-nv.md)). On the GT, the strapping resistors (R7, R9-R13) and EN/UVLO parts (R4, R5, C17) can be DNP: the regulator then starts disabled at PMBus address 0x24, firmware sets undervoltage shutdown via VIN_OFF (0x5A) and must clear the CP bit in ON_OFF_CONFIG (0x02). The same change failed on a Gamma 601 with communication errors, so it remains open ([bitaxeGT #11](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2025-09-01-github-bitaxegt-issue-11-can-we-dnp-all-the-tps546-strapping.md)).
- **Configuring without main power.** The TPS546 can run on 3.3V without regulating, useful for register setup; a plan to feed USB 3.3V to AVIN was dropped once USB 5V was removed ([ultraHex #17](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-02-04-github-ultrahex-issue-17-provide-3-3v-to-tps546-for-configur.md)).
- **Single-phase wiring.** The datasheet says ground BCX; skot later concluded BCX can float in single-phase use but SYNC should be grounded. The Gamma leaves both floating with no observed problems (open) ([bitaxeGamma #47](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2025-09-02-github-bitaxegamma-issue-47-tps546d24x-bcx-sync-pins-should.md)). AGND and GND are net-tied at a single point per the datasheet; a PR to tie them directly was disputed by skot ([ultraHex PR #50](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2026-08-26-github-ultrahex-pr-50-issues-fixed-and-adjust-some-details.md)).
- **Pull-ups.** The ESP32's internal pull-ups are extremely weak and PMBus calls for stronger ones; v304 Hex added discrete I2C pull-ups ([ultraHex #39](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-05-20-github-ultrahex-issue-39-add-pullup-resistors-to-the-i2c-sig.md)), and the Gamma has an open erratum to add 4.7k pull-ups on SDA, SCL and the TPS546 alert pin ([bitaxeGamma #25](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2025-03-04-github-bitaxegamma-issue-25-add-pullup-resistors-on-i2c-smbu.md)).
- **Filtering and layout.** On the Hex, 180 uF electrolytics were swapped for 2x 100 uF ceramics with caps moved out from under the heatsink in v304 ([ultraHex #29](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-04-22-github-ultrahex-issue-29-optimize-voltage-regulator-input-an.md)) — and v304 then turned out to lack Vcore output bulk capacitance; the README workaround is to solder a couple of 180 uF aluminium polymer capacitors, one each side, across the existing ceramic output caps ([ultraHex README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-ultrahex.md)). The "start of winding" end of inductors L1/L2 should sit nearer the switch node for EMI ([ultraHex #34](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-05-06-github-ultrahex-issue-34-change-the-silkscreen-for-l1-and-l2.md)); the GT extended top copper under the TPS546 ([bitaxeGT #2](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2025-01-09-github-bitaxegt-issue-2-extend-top-copper-under-tps546.md)).

### TPS546 faults

- **Fault set.** The TPS546 supports warn/fault thresholds on VIN over/under-voltage, VOUT over/under-voltage, overcurrent and overtemperature; skot chose to enter fault mode on VOUT_OV, VOUT_UV, IOUT_OC, VIN_UV and TEMP. A TI silicon bug prevents setting `VIN_UV_WARN_LIMIT` anywhere between around 2.5V to 8V, so it is useless on 5V boards and set to 0V ([ESP-Miner #757](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2025-03-10-github-esp-miner-issue-757-handle-tps546-warnings-and-faults.md)).
- **The 0 GH/s at ~5 W state.** From December 2024, Gammas dropped to 0 GH/s at about 5 W with ASIC voltage 0, recoverable only by a full power cycle. skot attributed it to power-supply faults with only partially implemented fault handling; PR #780 (v2.6.0) shows a fault banner and stops mining, and a warm restart clears it once the cause is gone ([ESP-Miner #588](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2024-12-18-github-esp-miner-issue-588-tps546-based-bitaxe-0-gh-s-hash-r.md)).
- **Opaque shutdowns.** After a heatsink-contact fault (regulator audibly buzzing, then off), STATUS_WORD stayed 0x0840 (PGOOD + OFF), so AxeOS can only say "power supply fault" (open) ([ESP-Miner #1322](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2025-11-04-github-esp-miner-issue-1322-tps546-fault-related-shutdown-re.md)). One duplicate-share investigation ended in a TPS546 output overcurrent shutdown (status 0x4850) at an IOUT_OC_FAULT_LIMIT of 30.00 A ([ESP-Miner #1820](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2026-07-19-github-esp-miner-issue-1820-asic-results-duplicated-200x-eac.md)).
- **A vs S silicon.** ESP-Miner v2.3.0 made the TPS546D24S a drop-in for the TPS546D24A ([ESP-Miner releases](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/github-esp-miner-releases.md)). In multi-phase setups, though, the A variant shuts down with SYNC_FAULT after ~20 minutes while identical S builds run fault-free for weeks; no register setting fixes it ([bitaxeGT #17](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2025-09-29-github-bitaxegt-issue-17-sync-fault-with-tps546d24a.md)), so skot asked firmware to support only the TPS546D24S on 800-series boards ([ESP-Miner #1255](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2025-09-29-github-esp-miner-issue-1255-remove-support-for-disqualified.md)). Conversely, a boot race ("i2c handle not initialized") from 2.8.0 appeared specific to the S variant on 601/602 Gammas ([ESP-Miner #1291](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2025-10-25-github-esp-miner-issue-1291-i2c-master-transmit-receive-1206.md)). A replacement TPS546D24A on a Gamma 601 failed with "Device ID mismatch" and firmware treats VCORE init failure as critical (open) ([ESP-Miner #1886](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2026-08-16-github-esp-miner-issue-1886-tps546d-regulator-not-detected-d.md)).
- **The regulator turns itself on.** Even with ASIC reset held low, a Gamma's ASIC draws about 2 W because the TPS546 enables in hardware; firmware should disable it until needed ([bitaxe-raw #10](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2026-03-19-github-bitaxe-raw-issue-10-fan-default-behavior-seems-interm.md)). The reset button resets only the ESP32, not the regulator — hard power-cycle for a clean run ([bitaxe-raw #9](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2026-03-03-github-bitaxe-raw-issue-9-having-trouble-flashing-and-runnin.md)).

### Power control pins (TPS40305-era Ultra)

Since board 202 the Ultra can switch the core regulator off through PWR_EN on GPIO10 (for over-temperature or a missing barrel jack); from board 204, PLUG_SENSE on GPIO12 goes high when the barrel jack is inserted ([ESP-Miner #75](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2024-01-08-github-esp-miner-issue-75-shutdown-core-voltage-regulator.md)) ([ESP-Miner #76](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2024-01-08-github-esp-miner-issue-76-detect-barrel-jack-insertion.md)). On 205, a barrel-plug parts shortage led to leaving the USB 5V diode unpopulated, disconnecting USB from 5V entirely ([ESP-Miner #148](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2024-03-21-github-esp-miner-issue-148-is-power-management-has-power-en.md)). The Hex hit the reverse problem — USB 5V back-feeding VIN and spinning the 12V fans — and also removed USB power ([ultraHex #18](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-02-12-github-ultrahex-issue-18-need-diode-on-5v-regulator-output.md)).

## Input voltage, UVLO and protection

### The 5V window

Single-ASIC Bitaxes are **5V DC only**; other voltages on the same connector will damage them ([bitaxeGamma README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-bitaxegamma.md)). The thresholds that matter, from several layers:

| Threshold | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| OSMU safe input range | 4.8-5.3v | ([OSMU FAQ](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-tips-faq.md)) |
| Maximum input | 5.5V — skot suggests staying at or below 5V when chasing repeated faults | ([ESP-Miner #588](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2024-12-18-github-esp-miner-issue-588-tps546-based-bitaxe-0-gh-s-hash-r.md)) |
| Gamma firmware TPS546 VIN on / off | 4.8 / 4.5 | ([ESP-Miner #1188](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2025-08-13-github-esp-miner-issue-1188-power-fault-detected-miner-too-s.md)) |
| Gamma hardware EN/UVLO divider | 4.36V (TPS546D24A ENuvlo threshold 1.05V) | ([bitaxeGamma #31](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2025-03-17-github-bitaxegamma-issue-31-uvlo-voltage-divider-it-set-wron.md)) |
| AxeOS low-voltage warning | (nominalVoltage + 0.5) * 0.87 → below 4.785 for 5V devices, 10.875V for 12V devices | ([ESP-Miner #1682](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2026-05-03-github-esp-miner-issue-1682-feature-fix-hardware-aware-low-v.md)) |

skot's summary: 5V Bitaxes have a very narrow VIN range, and high-resistance connectors or barrel plugs alone can cause "Power Fault Detected"; maintainers declined to widen it ([ESP-Miner #1188](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2025-08-13-github-esp-miner-issue-1188-power-fault-detected-miner-too-s.md)). The GT's UVLO divider was updated to match the WEBENCH design ([bitaxeGT #1](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2025-01-07-github-bitaxegt-issue-1-uvlo-voltage-divider-resistors-don-t.md)).

### PSU requirements and field failures

- **Gamma:** must deliver in excess of 4A (20W) without sagging below 5V, which often means a supply rated for 25-30W; the Meanwell GST60A05-P1J at 30 Watts is the README's recommendation. The barrel jack is 5.5x2.1mm center-positive (5.5x2.5mm plugs have been known to work) ([bitaxeGamma README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-bitaxegamma.md)). **Max:** 5V, a supply capable of over 15W ([bitaxeMax README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-bitaxemax.md)). **ultraHex:** 12V, around 50W @12V, supply capable of 100W ([ultraHex README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-ultrahex.md)). READMEs for the GT and Gamma Hex still lack stated stock power draw (open requests) ([bitaxeGT #22](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2026-08-03-github-bitaxegt-issue-22-add-power-supply-recommendations-to.md)).
- **Undersized bricks:** a 5v 1A supply left a unit connected to the pool but not mining — at least 4a is needed ([ESP-Miner #43](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2023-10-16-github-esp-miner-issue-43-not-mining.md)); random reboots and low-voltage warnings were cured by a supply that never drops below 5000mv, ideally 5100-5300mv ([ESP-Miner #97](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2024-01-28-github-esp-miner-issue-97-system-reboots.md)).
- **ATX rails and "6A" bricks:** a Gamma would not run from several ATX PSUs via Molex/SATA despite measuring 5.1V, while the stock 5V 6.0A 30W brick worked; skot: any supply holding a steady 5.0V at the regulator at the required current works ([ESP-Miner #1188](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2025-08-13-github-esp-miner-issue-1188-power-fault-detected-miner-too-s.md)). Some 5v 6amp supplies trip power faults under v2.11.x where an LRS-350-5 does not ([ESP-Miner #1352](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2025-11-15-github-esp-miner-issue-1352-2-11-0-self-test-issue.md)).
- **Loose plug:** a unit that kept dropping offline ran stable at about 2200 mA once the power cable was pushed fully in ([ESP-Miner #128](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2024-03-10-github-esp-miner-issue-128-v2-1-1-gui-improvements.md)).
- **Wider VIN** has been declined for the Hex (24 Volt solar: "No plans for extended voltage range at this time") ([ultraHex #3](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2023-10-21-github-ultrahex-issue-3-extender-voltage-range.md)); an open Gamma PR proposes a TPS82130SIL module to accept 5V-16V with the fan kept on 5V ([bitaxeGamma PR #57](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2026-02-22-github-bitaxegamma-pr-57-add-tps82130-for-wide-vin-support.md)).

### Input protection and fusing

> **Status: Disputed** — TVS diode and input fuse
> *For:* BitMaker-hub proposed an SMAJ5.0A TVS against plug/unplug transients; etkaar warned a TVS must come with a fuse because a dead TVS fails short ([bitaxeGamma #11](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-10-24-github-bitaxegamma-issue-11-add-power-input-protection.md)), and proposed a 5V input fuse because failing MLCCs get very hot and PLA cases can burn, noting NerdQAxe uses an SMD fuse ([bitaxeGamma #17](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-11-15-github-bitaxegamma-issue-17-thinking-about-placing-a-fuse-on.md)). The community ECO-603 proposal bundles TVS + fuse into a re-layout ([bitaxeGamma PR #61](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2026-09-05-github-bitaxegamma-pr-61-eco-603-601-602-heritage-pmbus-usb.md)).
> *Against:* skot found the SMAJ5.0A unsuitable for a 4.5V - 5.5V input — its 5.0V stand-off conducts and heats at 5.0 - 5.5V and its 9.2V clamp is too high when they need to clamp at 6-7V ([bitaxeGamma #11](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-10-24-github-bitaxegamma-issue-11-add-power-input-protection.md)); a Littelfuse 217 drops 130 mV at 4 A, which is bad at only 5V, and a traditional fuse is poor UX for overclockers — a resettable eFuse would be considered instead, and the TPS546 already shuts down on overcurrent ([bitaxeGamma #17](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-11-15-github-bitaxegamma-issue-17-thinking-about-placing-a-fuse-on.md)). The Hex eFuse request was likewise closed because "the TPS core voltage regulator already has shutdown capability for overcurrent and overvoltage", with an inline wire fuse suggested for those who want one ([ultraHex #23](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-02-27-github-ultrahex-issue-23-add-efuse.md)).
> *Best assessment (2026-10-09):* official boards ship without a TVS or fuse; the regulator's own OC/OV shutdown is the designed protection. The unresolved risk is upstream of the regulator (a shorted input cap), which the TPS546 cannot see — an inline fuse on the supply lead is the low-friction option for owners worried about it.

## Thermal sensing and fan control

### Reading the die diode (EMC2101 / EMC2103)

| Board | Temp source | Fan controller |
|---|---|---|
| Max (BM1397) | BM1397 die diode via EMC2101 | EMC2101 ([bitaxeMax README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-bitaxemax.md)) |
| Ultra ≤205 (BM1366) | BM1366 lacks die-temperature support, so the EMC2101 sits very close to the ASIC and uses its internal sensor ([bitaxeUltra README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-bitaxeultra.md)) | EMC2101 |
| Ultra 207 | Adds an external MMBT3904 diode (Q1) near the ASIC; the EMC2101 is farther away so its internal sensor reads cooler ([ESP-Miner #1228](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2025-09-10-github-esp-miner-issue-1228-add-support-for-bitaxeultra-207.md)) | EMC2101 |
| Gamma 60x (BM1370) | Die diode via EMC2101 | EMC2101; the two fan headers are tied together like a splitter ([ESP-Miner #476](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2024-11-13-github-esp-miner-issue-476-secondary-cooling-fan.md)) |
| ultraHex | TMP1075 inlet/outlet PCB sensors | EMC2302 dual-fan ([ultraHex README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-ultrahex.md)) |
| GT 80x, Gamma Hex | Dual-diode EMC2103 | EMC2103 ([bitaxeGT README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-bitaxegt.md)) |
| BIRDS (BZM2) | On-chip digital temperature sensor; EMC2101 dropped | — ([bitaxeBIRDS README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-bitaxebirds.md)) |

- **Each diode needs its own reader.** Temp_P/Temp_N is just a diode inside the chip, which is why an S9 reports only a few chip temperatures ([ESP-Miner #2](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2023-01-18-github-esp-miner-issue-2-not-an-issue-just-a-suggestion-on-e.md)).
- **Gamma tuning saga.** Some Gamma 600 builds overheated within minutes at stock 525MHz / 1150mV; the root cause was wrong BM1370 die readings (both too high and too low), closed as fixed "with better temp diode tuning on the EMC2101" ([bitaxeGamma #12](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-10-28-github-bitaxegamma-issue-12-some-bitaxegamma-builds-are-over.md)) ([bitaxeGamma #15](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-11-13-github-bitaxegamma-issue-15-some-asic-die-temperature-readin.md)). After PR #484 (v2.4.0), users saw readings about 10 degrees Celsius higher and fans about 2000 RPM faster ([ESP-Miner #512](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2024-11-23-github-esp-miner-issue-512-chip-temperature-2-3-0-2-4-0.md)); skot declined averaging bad readings instead of fixing them ([ESP-Miner #475](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2024-11-11-github-esp-miner-issue-475-add-the-average-temperature-next.md)). The GT's EMC2103 needed its own settings — slope correct, -10C offset ([ESP-Miner #881](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2025-05-02-github-esp-miner-issue-881-need-to-tune-temperature-sensor-f.md)).
- **Series resistance.** R22/R23 sit in line with the Gamma's temp diode; per the EMC2101 datasheet series resistance errs by +0.7°C per ohm and up to 100Ω is auto-corrected, so whether any is needed is an open question ([bitaxeGamma #13](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-11-03-github-bitaxegamma-issue-13-determine-proper-asic-temp-diode.md)); the ECO-603 proposal changes the temp-diode resistors to 10R ([bitaxeGamma PR #61](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2026-09-05-github-bitaxegamma-pr-61-eco-603-601-602-heritage-pmbus-usb.md)). Readings swinging 20ºC or more between samples remain open ([bitaxeGamma #16](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-11-13-github-bitaxegamma-issue-16-fluctuating-asic-die-temperature.md)).
- **Fault codes are not temperatures.** An open-circuit EMC2101 diode returns 0x3F8, which decodes to exactly 127.0 °C, and a garbage TPS546 word was cached as a valid reading — both triggered overheat downgrades that persist to NVS ([ESP-Miner #2000](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2026-09-25-github-esp-miner-issue-2000-overheat-protection-acts-on-sens.md)). A "temp diode fault" on a GT 801 is likely hardware, since the diode is inside the ASIC ([bitaxeGT #21](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2026-06-08-github-bitaxegt-issue-21-error-code-issue-temp-diode-fault.md)).

### Overheat thresholds

> **Status: Disputed** — where to trip
> *Firmware:* 75 °C for the ASIC and 105 °C for the TPS546; maintainers refuse to let users disable it because it prevents fried chips and fires ([ESP-Miner #1099](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2025-06-30-github-esp-miner-issue-1099-enhancement-request-overheat-con.md)). skot called the 75 figure a guess, since there are no datasheets ([ESP-Miner #599](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2024-12-26-github-esp-miner-issue-599-device-overheat-warning-automatic.md)).
> *Raise it:* a 2024 proposal sought 75C to 85C; benjamin-wilson asked for evidence to exceed Bitmain's limit ([ESP-Miner #316](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2024-09-02-github-esp-miner-issue-316-raise-overheat-limit.md)); others cited Bitmain S9 figures (Chip max. 135) and VNISH targets ([ESP-Miner #599](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2024-12-26-github-esp-miner-issue-599-device-overheat-warning-automatic.md)).
> *Stricter guidance:* the OSMU FAQ's safe ranges are ASIC below 70°C and voltage regulator below 90°C ([OSMU FAQ](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-tips-faq.md)).
> *Best assessment (2026-10-09):* treat the OSMU ranges as the operating target and the firmware trips as the safety net; no BM13xx datasheet backs a higher number, and S9-era figures don't transfer to 5nm-class silicon.

### Fan hardware and control

- **Fan choice.** Single-ASIC boards need a 4-pin 5V PWM fan (40mm, 20mm thick); a 12V fan spins too slowly and overheats the Bitaxe ([bitaxeGamma README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-bitaxegamma.md)) — confirmed when a fan that wouldn't spin at low speed turned out to be 12v ([ESP-Miner #1171](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2025-07-26-github-esp-miner-issue-1171-fan-boost-on-start.md)). The Hex instead moved fans to 12V because 5V isn't ideal for bigger fans ([ultraHex #9](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2023-11-25-github-ultrahex-issue-9-fan-should-be-12v.md)) and added a 5V pull-up on PWM because some fans need it ([ultraHex #15](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2023-12-23-github-ultrahex-issue-15-fan-pwm-may-need-a-5v-pullup.md)). Stock 3-wire no-name fans have the sense wire tied to ground, so 0 RPM is correct ([ESP-Miner #66](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2023-12-12-github-esp-miner-issue-66-fan-speed-0-rpm-with-version-2-0-4.md)).
- **Fan-on at power-up is not guaranteed.** The EMC2101 should go full-on at startup via the R24 5.6k pull-up, but only the EMC2101-R variant does this, and BOMs calling for -R get plain EMC2101 substituted (one of three PlebSource 601s had -R); the variant is readable as a product ID over I2C ([bitaxe-raw #10](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2026-03-19-github-bitaxe-raw-issue-10-fan-default-behavior-seems-interm.md)). Firmware has had the same hole: in 2.1.7 the fan stopped at power-up if WiFi was unreachable ([ESP-Miner #208](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2024-06-08-github-esp-miner-issue-208-overheating-protection.md)).
- **Polarity.** Ultra hardware needed an inverted-PWM mode ([ESP-Miner #21](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2023-08-31-github-esp-miner-issue-21-change-the-polarity-of-the-fan-spe.md)); the setting inverts duty cycle, not airflow, and a wrongly-set invert stops the fan at 100% ([ESP-Miner #459](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2024-11-05-github-esp-miner-issue-459-fan-stops-after-overheat-protecti.md)). Removing the setting broke the GT's EMC2103 fan ([ESP-Miner #865](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2025-04-23-github-esp-miner-issue-865-bitaxegt-fan-is-not-working.md)).
- **Scaling and tach quirks.** EMC2101_set_fan_speed() scales against 63, but the real maximum is 46, so commanded speeds are about ~1.37x too high and anything at or above 73% saturates (open) ([ESP-Miner #1927](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2026-08-29-github-esp-miner-issue-1927-emc2101-fan-duty-cycle-is-scaled.md)). An EMC2103 with no tach pulses reads 960 RPM and still passes the self-test target of 500 ([ESP-Miner #1970](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2026-09-12-github-esp-miner-issue-1970-fan-rpm-display-will-not-move-fr.md)). A fan stuck at 7000rpm was a failed PWM line ([ESP-Miner #1375](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2025-11-20-github-esp-miner-issue-1375-fan-speed-stuck-at-max-7000rpm.md)).
- See [ASIC Thermals and Heat Reuse](asic-thermals-and-heat-reuse.md#small-board-bitaxe-thermal-lessons) for leakage vs fan-power measurements.

### USB-C CC pull-down detail

Upstream USB-C (especially Thunderbolt) ports need to see Rd pull-downs on CC1/CC2 to detect a device; USB-A ports detect via D+/D- pull-ups, which is why the bug hid for so long. skot confirmed adding 5.1k pull-downs to a Gamma 601 made it mount on MacBook Thunderbolt ports, and said the bug was introduced on 204 hardware. The spec puts Rd at 5.1 kΩ ±10% (4.59 kΩ to 5.61 kΩ), so a 1% 5.6k resistor can land out of range ([bitaxeGamma #37](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2025-04-28-github-bitaxegamma-issue-37-bitaxe-is-not-detected-when-conn.md)).

## Mechanical and DFM

- **Heatsink pressure and warping.** More mounting holes stop the PCB bending and raise contact pressure — the Hex v303 added a center column of 3 holes ([ultraHex #20](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-02-26-github-ultrahex-issue-20-more-heatsink-mounting-holes.md)); the GT added a 5th hole between the ASICs ([bitaxeGT #12](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2025-09-01-github-bitaxegt-issue-12-mounting-hole.md)) and skot advises building GTs on thicker PCBs, "prolly 2mm" ([bitaxeGT #16](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2025-09-23-github-bitaxegt-issue-16-build-with-thicker-pcbs.md)). On the Gamma, replacing the spring clamps with M3*8 bolts and nuts let a user run 595MHz @ 1150mV, beyond the stock cooler's 525MHz limit ([ESP-Miner #369](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2024-09-30-github-esp-miner-issue-369-lower-the-default-hash-frequency.md)). Gamma hole positions changed slightly between 601 and 602 ([bitaxeGamma #32](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2025-03-19-github-bitaxegamma-issue-32-pcb-mounting-holes-are-a-bit-off.md)).
- **Odd hole patterns.** The Hex heatsink pattern of 60x56mm matches no known processor, making off-the-shelf coolers hard to find ([ultraHex #47](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-09-20-github-ultrahex-issue-47-heatsink-mounting-holes.md)); drill heatsink holes only deep enough to tap ([ultraHex #5](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2023-11-01-github-ultrahex-issue-5-heatsink-hole-spacing.md)). Hex cooling needs at least one 80x80mm 12V 4-pin fan, a 90mm heatsink with tapped M3 holes and a 130mm enclosure forcing air through it ([ultraHex README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-ultrahex.md)).
- **Connectors.** Hex power moved from screw terminals to a PCB-mount XT30 in v304 ([ultraHex #38](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-05-18-github-ultrahex-issue-38-change-power-input-to-xt30.md)); the USB-C receptacle was moved for overmold clearance, and an upright receptacle rejected for durability ([ultraHex #2](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2023-10-19-github-ultrahex-issue-2-usb-c-port-needs-clearance-for-plug.md)); gamma-601 added a 4-pin SMD JST fan connector ([bitaxeGamma releases](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxegamma-releases.md)). Unplugging the OLED while powered can break it ([ESP-Miner #567](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2024-12-10-github-esp-miner-issue-567-add-configuration-option-setting.md)); skot's long-term plan is a bare OLED mounted directly on the board ([bitaxeGT #8](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2025-05-15-github-bitaxegt-issue-8-display-falls-out.md)).
- **Hand-solderability.** Bigger packages are preferred where there's no reason for tiny ones — TMP1075DGKR on the Hex ([ultraHex #35](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-05-13-github-ultrahex-issue-35-change-temperature-sensors-to-a-lar.md)), level-shifter diodes and IO LDOs on BIRDS ([bitaxeBIRDS #6](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2026-02-09-github-bitaxebirds-issue-6-use-bigger-packages-for-level-shi.md)).
- **BOM hygiene and parts whack-a-mole.** Swap DigiKey tape-and-reel numbers (10,000+ MOQ) for cut-tape ([ultraHex #26](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-03-19-github-ultrahex-issue-26-replace-digikey-tape-reel-part-numb.md)); the Gamma moved to barrel jack 54-00164 when PJ-036AH-SMT-TR went out of stock, with L1 alternates IHLP5050FDERR30M01 and PSPMAA1007-R30M-ANP-DC ([bitaxeGamma #9](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-10-12-github-bitaxegamma-issue-9-dfm-increase-pad-size-of-barrel-p.md)). Several "wrong part number" reports were Excel mangling the CSV on import ([bitaxeGamma PR #3](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-09-16-github-bitaxegamma-pr-3-fix-100pf-capacitor-product-number-o.md)).
- **Wrong ESP32 module.** Only the ESP32-S3-WROOM-1 N16R8 (16MB flash, 8MB Octal PSRAM) is supported ([ESP-Miner README](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/github-bitaxeorg-esp-miner.md)); boards built with no-PSRAM M0N16 modules boot-loop on newer firmware ([ESP-Miner #826](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2025-04-06-github-esp-miner-issue-826-make-firmware-releases-for-other.md)).

## PCB fabrication and hand assembly

- **Ordering.** Bitaxe PCBs are 4-layer, 6mil trace/space, 0.3mm holes; 1oz outer / 0.5oz inner copper works; order top and bottom paste stencils. Every part except the ASIC is on DigiKey ([bitaxeGamma README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-bitaxegamma.md)). Pre-release Hex boards need 2 oz. copper inside and out ([ultraHex README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-ultrahex.md)).
- **Files.** Fabs need gerbers plus drill files; assemblers also need a BOM and centroid file, generated in KiCad via Fabrication Outputs ([Gamma building guide](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxegamma-building.md)). Required layers: F/In1/In2/B copper, F/B paste (stencils only), F/B silkscreen, F/B mask, Edge.Cuts ([OSMU wiki: building PCBs](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-tips-building-pcbs.md)). CPL files aren't shipped because they're assembler-specific, and the latest design always lives in KiCad ([ultraHex #45](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-08-26-github-ultrahex-issue-45-cpl-files-please.md)) ([ultraHex #28](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-04-06-github-ultrahex-issue-28-bitaxehex-303-schematic-pdf-missing.md)).
- **Toaster-oven reflow** (skot's method): Sn63/Pb37 no-clean paste kept cold; soak at 140-150C for 2 minutes; reflow holding 200 to 220C for 30 seconds; cool with the door open; inspect for bridges and dry pads, especially on QFN/QFP. Use a thermocouple rated to ~300C just above the PCB; double-sided boards reflow fine because surface tension holds the bottom parts ([Gamma assembly guide](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxegamma-assembly.md)) ([OSMU wiki: assembly](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-tips-assembly.md)).
- **Bring-up debugging.** The OSMU wiki publishes expected Ultra test-point voltages (e.g. TP4 1.8, TP14 0.8, TP17-TP19 3.3) ([OSMU wiki: Bitaxe 200](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-bitaxe-200.md)); on a single-chip board, probe Vcore across C13 ([ESP-Miner #3](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2023-01-21-github-esp-miner-issue-3-adc-is-using-deprecated-driver-need.md)). A crack across an ASIC die means it overheated (probably during soldering) and is destroyed ([OSMU Lab: BM1366](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-osmu-lab-bm1366.md)). Hex boards seeing only 5 of 6 ASICs were attributed to soldering, BOM placement or the TCH firmware fork, not the design ([ultraHex #49](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2025-06-25-github-ultrahex-issue-49-bm1366-mode-versions.md)). Building is an advanced SMD job; non-builders should buy assembled ([bitaxeGamma README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-bitaxegamma.md)).

## Bitaxe Accessory Port (BAP)

The BAP sits on the top of the board and exposes four serial ports plus a 5V power pin and GND ([OSMU wiki: BAP](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-bitaxe-bap.md)); the Hex replaced its old display connector with BAP for cross-compatibility ([ultraHex #21](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2024-02-26-github-ultrahex-issue-21-add-bap-to-hex.md)), and the GT and Gamma Hex carry a 12V BAP ([bitaxeGT README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-bitaxegt.md)) ([Gamma Hex README](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxeorg-bitaxegammahex.md)). The protocol is NMEA-like — `$BAP,COMMAND,PARAMETER,VALUE*CHECKSUM` with an XOR checksum — supporting REQ/RES/SUB/UNSUB/SET and more, with subscriptions timing out after 5 minutes ([ESP-Miner BAP README](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/github-esp-miner-main-bap-bap-readme.md)). SET can change Wi-Fi, frequency, voltage and fan settings without authentication; maintainers kept that on purpose because it needs physical access ([ESP-Miner #1947](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2026-09-04-github-esp-miner-issue-1947-bap-set-accepts-configuration-wr.md)). The first add-on, BitHalo, is an LED board that pulses on each share ([OSMU wiki: BitHalo](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/osmu-wiki-bitaxe-bithalo.md)).

## Contradictions & Open Questions

- **Which rev 603?** bitaxeGamma published an official gamma-603 release on 2026-02-17 with no release notes ([bitaxeGamma releases](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/github-bitaxegamma-releases.md)), while a separate, unmerged community "ECO-603" proposal (September 2026) bundles the open Gamma errata — SYNC/BCX to ground, PMBus pull-ups, temp-diode resistors, TVS + fuse, USB-C CC and ESD, BOM fixes — into its own rev 603 re-layout ([bitaxeGamma PR #61](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2026-09-05-github-bitaxegamma-pr-61-eco-603-601-602-heritage-pmbus-usb.md)) ([bitaxeGamma PR #62](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/2026-09-07-github-bitaxegamma-pr-62-eco-603-pcb-rev-603-manufacturing-i.md)). The captures do not say which errata the official 603 already fixes.
- **Gamma USB-C fix scope.** ESP-Miner docs mention a USB-C problem on Gamma models before 602 ([firmware release records](../../raw/hardware/bitaxe-osmu/bitaxe-firmware-release-records.md)), yet ECO-603 still lists the CC fix as outstanding. Check your board for R pull-downs on CC1/CC2 rather than trusting the revision number.
- **BCX/SYNC grounding**, **strapping-less TPS546 on the Gamma**, and **whether any temp-diode series resistance is needed** remain open (see sections above).
