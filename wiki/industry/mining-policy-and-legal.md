## Overview

The legal and policy thread that runs through POD256's 2025–2026 episodes. The hosts' recurring thesis is that law and regulation — not physics — set several of open-source mining's hardest constraints: the Samourai Wallet prosecution tested whether writing noncustodial Bitcoin software can be treated as money transmission, a theory the hosts and guests argue reaches miners themselves; US security agencies scrutinized Chinese-built mining hardware (the "operation Red Sunset" inquiry, customs holds, FCC license policy) in a market where Bitmain holds roughly 80% share; open-hardware projects fought trademark squatters at the USPTO, tariff surprises at customs, and certification regimes at the product stage; and net-metering rules quietly decide whether home solar plus mining pencils out. Almost everything on this page is host commentary and guest recollection on the podcast, not legal advice — recorded conversations, cited as such.

## The Samourai Wallet case

### Charges, plea and sentence

- Samourai Wallet developers Keonne Rodriguez and William (Bill) were indicted on 04/24/2024 on two counts: conspiracy to commit money laundering (maximum twenty years) and conspiracy to operate an unlicensed money service business (maximum five years) ([POD256 e096](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-26-e096.md); [POD256 e093](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-05-e093.md)).
- By August 2025 the developers had taken a plea: the money-laundering conspiracy charge (twenty-year maximum) was dropped in exchange for a guilty plea to conspiracy to operate an unlicensed money transmitting business (five-year maximum). Prosecutors' superseding indictment had already dropped the "nineteen sixty b one b" object (not registering with FinCEN as a money transmitter) after FinCEN guidance on noncustodial wallets, leaving the "nineteen sixty b one c" object (knowingly transmitting criminal proceeds) ([POD256 e080](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-08-01-e080.md)).
- Eco's read at plea time: a guilty jury verdict risked setting case-law precedent that noncustodial wallet software is money transmission; the plea avoided precedent but let the government effectively punish open-source noncustodial wallet developers, which he expected to recur ([POD256 e080](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-08-01-e080.md)).
- At sentencing (November 2025), prosecutors sought the five-year maximum plus $250,000 in fines; the probation department's pre-sentence report recommended forty two months and a $20,000 fine; Judge Cote imposed the maximum — sixty months and a $250,000 fine — on Keoni, who had also forfeited $6,300,000 (a combined figure for him and Bill, not each) after being on electronic monitoring for 560-plus days on a million dollar bail package, time that does not count toward the sentence. The first developer was sentenced November 6 and the second November 7; no appeal was allowed under the plea ([POD256 e096](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-26-e096.md); [POD256 e093](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-05-e093.md)). By March 2026 Keonne was serving a five year prison sentence and Bill four years ([POD256 e108](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-03-18-e108.md)).
- Plea arithmetic per Rodriguez: the government threatened a 237,000,000 forfeiture judgment absent a deal; with the deal, forfeiture was set at $6,300,000 — the fees Samourai earned over roughly a decade (about 6,000,000 dollars against the alleged volume, described as primarily Sybil-resistance fees for Whirlpool coordinator service). He said an appeal would have taken five to eight years and cost 10 to $12,000,000 with a twenty-five-year downside ([POD256 e096](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-26-e096.md)).
- Procedural history the hosts found notable: the case was reassigned mid-stream from Judge Berman to Judge Cote, who denied without argument or written order the motion to dismiss, Bill's motion to sever, and amicus briefs; the defense cited the 1940 Falcone sugar-to-bootlegger precedent and a 1990s grow-light manufacturer appeal (mere knowledge that buyers may commit crimes is not enough to join a conspiracy); prosecutors filed a third superseding indictment after the motion to dismiss and about a week before the response was due ([POD256 e096](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-26-e096.md)). Lauren Rodriguez added that the replacement judge — a former chief of SDNY's criminal division — denied the dismissal motion, amicus briefs and Brady issue in a hearing lasting five or ten minutes ([POD256 e108](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-03-18-e108.md)).

### FinCEN said no license was needed

- FinCEN guidance from 2013, reiterated in 2019, says money transmission requires custody and control of funds, and the 2019 guidance said "anonymity providers" without control are not transmitters ([POD256 e096](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-26-e096.md)). Per Rodriguez, discovery showed prosecutors had asked FinCEN before the indictment whether Samourai needed a license, and FinCEN answered they were not money transmitters because they took no custody; the indictment came roughly six months later, and the defense raised it as a Brady violation ([POD256 e096](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-26-e096.md)). Eco's summary at sentencing: FinCEN agreed no FinCEN license was needed — so that charge was dropped — but prosecutors kept an unlicensed-money-transmitting theory based on knowingly transferring criminal proceeds ([POD256 e093](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-05-e093.md)).
- How Whirlpool actually worked, per Rodriguez: users never relinquish custody; coins move between addresses the user controls; a blinded coordinator only passes messages it cannot read until at least five participants are found, and each wallet signs the joint transaction locally. Two-party CoinJoins needed no coordinator; five-to-eight-party joins still used a centralized coordinator ([POD256 e096](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-26-e096.md)).
- The government claimed Samourai handled $2,000,000,000 in "unlawful" transactions (unlawful only because no KYC/AML was performed) and that 237,000,000 of it was laundered funds — the hosts noted the laundered share is roughly 10% of the total by the government's own numbers. Samourai's OXT chain-analysis platform went offline when servers were seized, so the defense never independently checked the figures; Rodriguez said Samourai's own published research traced Lazarus Group funds through Wasabi (via Sinbad / blender.io), not Whirlpool ([POD256 e096](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-26-e096.md)).

### Why miners are in the blast radius

- Rodriguez argued that treating fees as the marker of money transmission is a dangerous defense line: "if I was a Bitcoin miner, I would be shitting bricks", since miners accept fees — an essential part of the system — and nothing is transmitted in the Bitcoin network until it is included in a block; under the government's theory, miners are the most transmitter-like actors ([POD256 e096](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-26-e096.md)). The prosecutors' language — "to facilitate the transmission of funds by any and all means" — the hosts read as broad enough to sweep in miners, node operators, Lightning node operators, wallet developers and hardware-wallet makers; Rodriguez agreed and warned it is going to be "your node operators, your miners" next ([POD256 e096](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-26-e096.md)).
- The same argument via Ocean's Bob Burnett, cited by Lauren Rodriguez: a transaction isn't complete until mined in a block, so if non-custodial software providers are money transmitters, node operators, miners and Lightning node operators could face the same theory ([POD256 e108](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-03-18-e108.md)). On the Tornado Cash precedent, Rodriguez said the government's position was that the fee is irrelevant — they would have prosecuted a free service anyway ([POD256 e096](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-26-e096.md)).
- Jurisdiction was contested: per Iko, SDNY claimed it because an FBI agent in Manhattan downloaded Samourai Wallet, deposited bitcoin and used Ricochet and Whirlpool, though the developers had no New York operations ([POD256 e108](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-03-18-e108.md)). Lauren Rodriguez noted that Todd Blanche (DOJ) issued a memo saying DOJ would not prosecute software developers for the crimes of their users — prosecutors nevertheless filed a superseding indictment shortly after the defense's motion to dismiss — and that the "willful blindness" doctrine could still threaten developers even if the CLARITY Act passes ([POD256 e108](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-03-18-e108.md)).
- Practical spillover: during the Telehash, Braiins' hashrate marketplace refused a payment from a UTXO recently in Whirlpool; Iko expects merchants and processors to increasingly reject UTXOs not traced to whitelisted exchanges, which he argues raises the importance of open-source home mining as a non-KYC source of coins ([POD256 e108](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-03-18-e108.md)). (See [Network State 2022-2026](network-state-2022-2026.md) for the related template-filtering story: Ocean's launch-time filter rejected Samourai Whirlpool TX0 transactions.)

### Petition, surrender and aftermath

- Rodriguez was ordered to surrender to the Bureau of Prisons by December 19 (a December 19 deadline to report if not designated earlier), designated to a minimum-security facility in Morgantown, West Virginia; Bill reported shortly after the new year ([POD256 e096](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-26-e096.md); [POD256 e098](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-12-13-e098.md); [POD256 e099](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-12-17-e099.md)). Bail conditions barred the developers from directly or indirectly interacting with cryptocurrency, so a GiveSendGo for their families could only enable crypto payments after surrender ([POD256 e097](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-12-03-e097.md)).
- The pardon petition (billandkeonne.org) grew from about 2,000 signatures against a 100,000 goal at the e096 recording, to over 3,000 with about 150,000 views (e097), 4,545 toward a 5,000 milestone (e098), and 7,736 signatures from 368,337 views — a 2.1% conversion rate (e099); change.org kept 100% of donations made after signing ($18,279 so far), which bought 305,933 promoted views yielding 949 signatures — about $200 per signature, versus organic conversion closer to 12% ([POD256 e096](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-26-e096.md); [POD256 e097](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-12-03-e097.md); [POD256 e098](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-12-13-e098.md); [POD256 e099](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-12-17-e099.md)). By March 2026 the petition had stalled just before 15,000 signatures ([POD256 e108](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-03-18-e108.md)). A Decrypt reporter asked President Trump about the campaign at a White House event; Trump said he was aware and would look into it, flagging Pam Bondi, and Ross Ulbricht told Keoni in a Twitter Space that his own change.org petition had been instrumental in securing his pardon ([POD256 e099](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-12-17-e099.md)).
- Policy whiplash noted by Eco: Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche's memo (around May 2025) said DOJ would not pursue developers of mixers for users' crimes, and an executive order routes forfeited bitcoin to the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve — yet SDNY sold the Samourai forfeiture immediately. The government had claimed 280,000,000 of illicit Whirlpool flows ([POD256 e098](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-12-13-e098.md)).
- Counterpoint on the government's theory, from 256F's own newsletter breakdown of episode 108 (2026-03-18): in March 2026 the U.S. Treasury told Congress that "lawful users may leverage mixers for financial privacy" — which the newsletter argued directly undercuts the DOJ's unlicensed-transmitter theory while the developers remain imprisoned ([256F newsletter: e108 breakdown, 2026-03-18](../../raw/history/256f-newsletter/2026-03-18-pod256-episode-108-breakdown-from-mixers-to-miners-why-samou.md)). This is the foundation newsletter's summary of the Treasury report, not the report itself; the underlying Treasury PDF is not yet captured in raw/ (needs Tyler review).
- OPSEC lessons from the raid, per Rodriguez: about 60 or 70 devices were seized (all encrypted); dispose of old devices, use disappearing messages, assume handwritten notes will be read out of context — but never destroy anything once you know of an investigation or subpoena. His own bitcoin could not be seized because it was protected by 12 words in a safe plus a memorized passphrase; for the forfeiture the government had to give him an address to send coins to ([POD256 e096](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-26-e096.md)). The hosts added a chilling-effect angle: the Ashigaru developers operate fully anonymously, and publishing open source early and widely (many contributors, redundant copies) reduces single-point targeting — some Samourai code was not on GitHub when servers were seized ([POD256 e080](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-08-01-e080.md)).

### Related cases and developer advocacy

- Roman Storm (Tornado Cash) raised roughly 7 to $8,000,000 for his defense and, per Rodriguez, is the main precedent path: appealing his unlicensed-money-transmission conviction on the "control is required" argument could support a motion to vacate the Samourai conviction ([POD256 e096](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-26-e096.md)). In the Bitcoin Fog / Roman Sterlingov case, Chainalysis evidence avoided Daubert scrutiny on national-security grounds; Samourai had offered its OXT platform and expert witnesses for that defense, which the government got excluded ([POD256 e096](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-26-e096.md)).
- Advocacy: Senator Lummis co-signed a bipartisan letter supporting the developers after the arrests; the developer safe-harbor language hosts discussed is in the Blockchain Regulatory Certainty Act (introduced, not passed the House per congress.gov) — not the Clarity Act, a correction made in-episode ([POD256 e093](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-05-e093.md)). Rodriguez called for a well-funded community legal-defense organization for open-source Bitcoin builders, crediting BPI with an initial effort ([POD256 e096](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-26-e096.md)). Samourai had also led a February 2024 comment letter to FinCEN — with about twenty-plus Bitcoin companies cosigning — opposing a proposed rule that would require MSBs to file extra reports on users of privacy tech ([POD256 e096](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-26-e096.md)).
- Open-source continuity after the seizure: the Ashigaru fork of Samourai shipped unreleased features and later restored Whirlpool; Dojo continues under Dojo Coder and RoninDojo — hosts tied this to the 256 Foundation's mission of funding open-source developers ([POD256 e096](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-26-e096.md)).
- Adjacent platform-policy precedent: Google announced (then walked back) requiring money-transmitter licenses for noncustodial wallet apps on the Play Store, and moved to require developer ID and restrict sideloading in Android 16; GrapheneOS said the impact on it would be minimal ([POD256 e082](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-08-27-e082.md)).

## US security scrutiny of Chinese mining hardware

- A Bitcoin Magazine article from November 21 by Micah Zimmerman reported a DHS-led inquiry dubbed operation Red Sunset examining whether Bitmain machines could be remotely manipulated for espionage or sabotage of US critical infrastructure; Bitmain said it complies with US law and denied awareness of the operation. The article referenced the May 24 blocking of a mining site near a nuclear missile base in Wyoming, and the Commerce Department blacklisting Bitmain AI affiliate SoftGo Technologies in January '25 over alleged Huawei dealings; the hosts also recalled US port seizures of Bitmain shipments over the summer ([POD256 e097](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-12-03-e097.md)).
- Concentration makes this a systemic risk: hosts put Bitmain at roughly 80% (elsewhere cited at up to 90%) of the miner market, so a US import ban would severely limit miner supply ([POD256 e097](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-12-03-e097.md)). In August 2025 Scott had found a cheap ARM Cortex-A Linux processor from Sophgo effectively embargoed for US import due to Huawei work — Sophgo is believed to be a Bitmain subsidiary ([POD256 e092](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-10-29-e092.md)). By e124 the hosts linked the earlier US government security advisory and customs holds on Bitmain hardware to a Bitmain subsidiary's work with Huawei, noting ASICs have about a thirty month life cycle, so delivery holds are costly ([POD256 e124](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-08-17-e124.md)).
- Eco's technical read: a SHA-256 ASIC can physically only hash block headers, so any malicious capability would sit in other ICs or closed control-board firmware; given Bitmain's history (covert ASICBoost, the "amp bleed" remote shutoff), open-source firmware is the fix because anyone can audit it ([POD256 e097](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-12-03-e097.md)).
- FCC policy points the same direction per the hosts: the FCC said it will not issue new licenses for Chinese-made Wi-Fi chips — essentially all Wi-Fi chips are made in China, with exemptions for vendors pledging to fix that — and the hosts argue similar scrutiny of Chinese-controlled mining hardware and firmware (with documented past backdoors) would favor funding open source ([POD256 e115](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-05-27-e115.md)).

## Trademarks: the Bitaxe USPTO fight

- As of October 2025, two China-based parties, using US-based lawyers, had applied to register the Bitaxe trademark with the USPTO. Scott said Bitaxe had been an unregistered trademark (TM) for over two years and he had never registered it out of distaste for bureaucracy ([POD256 e091](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-10-22-e091.md)).
- Mechanics he described: USPTO applicants must sign a declaration under penalty of perjury that the mark is theirs; Scott, with a trademark law firm, filed a formal opposition and also applied to register the mark himself (an entity, not just an individual, can own a registration). Approval takes roughly eight to ten months, and unopposed applications are generally approved without investigation. Registration requires showing use in commerce, which the real project has ([POD256 e091](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-10-22-e091.md)).
- Context and risk: the USPTO had in August canceled about 25,000 trademark registrations from a Chinese group mass-filing marks; the lawyers' first step was contacting the applicants' lawyers to get the filings withdrawn. Scott's concern is that there is no "public domain" trademark, so someone must hold it — a hostile registrant's leverage is takedowns (Amazon sellers, GitHub repos via trademark claims, where GitHub tends to comply first) ([POD256 e091](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-10-22-e091.md)).
- International: Bitaxe trademarks were reportedly already approved in China to another party, in Germany to a friendly organization, and in the UK to an unknown party; the Madrid system allows streamlined filing abroad from a US registration ([POD256 e091](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-10-22-e091.md)).
- Copyright licenses and trademarks are distinct: MIT does not grant rights to licensor names or logos. Debian was cited as strictly controlling its name and logo while the software stays forkable under another name; Arduino was named as one of few copyleft open-hardware precedents ([POD256 e091](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-10-22-e091.md)).

## Patents and open IP

- ASICBoost — an almost 25% mining efficiency improvement — was patented in 2013 by Timo Hanke and Sergio Damian Lerner, and Bitmain implemented it anyway from China; per Scott, patents are hard to enforce US-to-China and require disclosing the method ([POD256 e127](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-09-28-e127.md) — see the episode transcript for the full discussion).
- Tyler's framing: unlike patents (temporary monopoly, then disclosure), mining ASIC vendors never released chips, datasheets or specifications; historical examples discussed on the show — Oliver Evans' automated flour mill (US patent number three) stunting innovation until it expired, after which 2,000 plus mills appeared, and James Watt's condenser patent (via Eric Hirschman: thirty years of little adoption) — were used to argue patents can slow diffusion of process innovations ([POD256 e127](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-09-28-e127.md)). Block's "Open IP" pledge (content streaming, personalization and artist-compensation patents, starting with Tidal) was read by the hosts as a non-aggression stance toward builders ([POD256 e092](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-10-29-e092.md)).

## Tariffs, customs and import friction

- Eco's box of SMD components from LCSC was destroyed by US customs after the shipper failed to supply a form declaring steel/aluminum/copper content — a harmonized tariff code flagged metal content (illustrative example given: 10% aluminum at a 100% tariff). The refund omitted a $40 reel fee and taxes (about $90 total); Scott suspected a wrong tariff code, since parts cost around 6 tenths of a cent. Lesson drawn: Digi-Key costs more but ships domestically; buying direct from manufacturers needs huge quantities (e.g., a 100,000 resistors) ([POD256 e092](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-10-29-e092.md)).
- Abroad it cuts the other way: Kurt and Gridless released the BitShaka (a Bitaxe Gamma clone) as open source partly because imports into Kenya face tariffs and arbitrarily enforced import taxes, so local assembly with locally obtainable parts is the workaround; Scott noted Bitaxe was designed with US components ([POD256 e118](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-24-e118.md)).

## Certification: UL, FCC, CE, RoHS

- The baseline (Scott): no safety certification such as UL is legally required (unlike FCC ID for RF emissions); UL and competitors are private firms. Certifications are hyper-specific — a certified component does not make a system safe; whole systems must be certified, and modifications void them ([POD256 e083](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-09-03-e083.md)). Tyler's counterpoint: certifications matter for home-insurance claims, and field certification of assembled systems is possible but costly ([POD256 e093](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-05-e093.md)).
- FCC vs UL (Scott): UL is a private safety certification, not legally required anywhere (UL may pursue false claims of certification); FCC is federal and concerns RF interference ([POD256 e093](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-05-e093.md)).
- Heater classification oddities (Tyler): Canaan's Avalon Mini 3 label says "server" though marketing calls it a household baseboard heater, and Heatbit's app blocks remote eco/turbo control in heater mode while allowing it in air-purifier mode — likely because remotely controllable heaters face stricter certification ([POD256 e093](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-05-e093.md)). Products containing miners need extra certification work (e.g., Heatbit's tip-over testing), and an S21 has no gyro sensor ([POD256 e124](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-08-17-e124.md)).
- Bitaxe compliance status (Scott): the ESP32 module has an FCC ID and Bitaxe uses the recommended antenna within spec (component-level certification); Part 15 (unintentional radiator) is self-certified — Bitaxe has passed it, and some manufacturers took complete products through it; open source boards sold as kits put compliance on the end user. Bitaxe has passed CE, and EU sellers carry the CE mark. RoHS mainly restricts lead (notably in solder); many Bitaxe makers use lead-free solder for EU compliance, which needs higher temperature and more process control and produces worse joints ([POD256 e093](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-05-e093.md)).
- The trades work under codes: installers (HVAC, plumbers, electricians) work under codes like the National Electrical Code and prefer UL-listed appliances; an HVAC tech refused to cut a plenum hole for a client's miner install over liability ([POD256 e093](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-05-e093.md)).

## Net metering and utility policy

- Tyler's Xcel (Colorado) bill as read on air: the base rate is 15¢, but with all charges it works out to about 31¢ a kilowatt hour. Solar exports get banked as account credits at that retail value — but at the end of the year leftover credits are paid out at three cents a kilowatt hour, which the hosts call a 90% value reduction; "Net metering is such a rug pull". Their preferred alternative: a miner converting excess generation to sats ([POD256 e115](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-05-27-e115.md)). In a later retelling Tyler put Xcel's year-end payout at about 1¢ ([POD256 e117](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-17-e117.md)).
- Export prices can go negative in parts of Europe — one homeowner's response was running space heaters in a barn to dump excess — and few utilities offer customer APIs ([POD256 e117](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-17-e117.md)). In Switzerland, RY3T built its Nova excess-solar miner because Swiss solar export compensation is shrinking and from the following year exports would face real (sometimes negative) prices ([POD256 e120](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-07-15-e120.md)).
- Interconnection is itself a gate: grid-tied solar needs utility interconnect approval; isolating solar to dedicated outlets or a battery-fed circuit was discussed as a workaround ([POD256 e092](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-10-29-e092.md)). The economics of mining against these tariffs are covered in [Hashrate Heating Economics](../economics/hashrate-heating-economics.md).
