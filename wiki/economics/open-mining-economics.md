## Overview

What open-source mining actually costs, why it can't win on $/TH alone, and where its real economic cases are: alternative form factors, heat reuse, and independence from Bitmain's supply chain. The honest baseline from the community's own manufacturing math: even with free ASICs, small-scale open hardware lands at $8-$12/TH against Bitmain's $.50-$1.50/TH — roughly an order of magnitude — so the mission is 'building something Bitmain doesn't control', not price parity [#2183 · 2025-05-16 · Mike Hamilton] [#2180 · 2025-05-16 · econoalchemist].

## The $/TH debate (May 2025)

- Small-scale manufacturing math (Mike Hamilton): even with free BZM2 ASICs, building 3 hashboards + control board + PSU + Fans + Shell at 3,000-10,000 machine scale costs $8-$12/TH (appx 400 GH/s per chip); Bitmain's economies of scale put them around $.50-$1.50/TH (not including asics) [#2183 · 2025-05-16 · Mike Hamilton]
- Bitmain's market position: 'They have captured over 80% of the market with this BS. Open source mining is currently a worse value. If we do this right it won't be like that forever.' [#2195 · 2025-05-17 · Skot Bitaxe]
- econoalchemist settled the mission question: 'The 256 Foundation is a nonprofit; this isn't about how big the market is, this is about building something Bitmain doesn't control' [#2180 · 2025-05-16 · econoalchemist]
- The foundation's real value is alternative form factors (home water heater, pool heater, brewing boiler); adit 'should be the focus of another dev' [#2188 · 2025-05-16 · Mike Hamilton]
- Elijah Sanders on Bitmain's incentives: 'Nothing bitmain does is to accommodate the customers. The entire design is to support as much Chinese industry as possible. From the shipping packaging to the components packaging' — not profit-maximizing for anyone but Bitmain 'selling shovels' [#2196 · 2025-05-17 · Elijah Sanders] [#2197 · 2025-05-17 · Elijah Sanders]

## Heat-as-service economics

- Heat-reuse-as-a-service could become a vertical inside HVAC: installers maintain miners under service contracts, plus education partnerships with home builders; adoption happens only when customers demand it, and CAPEX must be sold as household opex reduction [#604 · 2024-03-25 · Alex Brammer] [#606 · 2024-03-25 · Alex Brammer] [#607 · 2024-03-25 · Alex Brammer]
- The structural problem: miners obsolesce and the Bitcoin subsidy declines over time, unlike a 15-year water heater [#608 · 2024-03-25 · Alex Brammer]
- Scale reference: DR Horton built 89,000 homes in the prior year — the kind of builder home-mining advocates wanted to reach [#610 · 2024-03-25 · Alex Brammer]
- Company-owns-the-miner lease model keeps homeowner CAPEX at zero but concentrates rewards with the operator — 'a distributed mega miner', weaker for decentralization [#615 · 2024-03-25 · Tyler Stevens]
- Heat-arbitrage datapoint: Dylan Seib's hashprice+solar-aware controller means he is effectively paid +$0.13/kWh to heat his home with mining; the system downshifts when solar drops or the furnace wins [#3130 · 2025-10-15 · Dylan Seib]

## Machine efficiency tiers and unit economics (POD256, 2025-2026)

The podcast's recurring efficiency-and-economics segment, in one place.

- **Where the curve sits (September 2025, Proto launch).** Proto's rig came in "at 14.1, Jules for Terahash" against Bitmain's top of the line at "12 and a half or maybe even lower", with the S23 claimed at "nine and a half" ([POD256 e085](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-09-07-e085.md)). The pod's Dragonmint parallel: efficiency gains once moved so fast that "by the time Dragonmint got to market, it was already unprofitable" ([POD256 e085](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-09-07-e085.md)). A Cholla Markets operator added the demand-side view: cheap dollar-per-terahash machines are "an a sick retirement home" for near-free power, don't pencil at "6 or 7¢" fixed residential rates, and "If I have 80% uptime at $20 a megawatt hour" the effective rate is "still sub 3¢" — after which the heat-reuse market takes them as sunk cost ([POD256 e085](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-09-07-e085.md)). Same episode: wholesale batches of "amp miner" hardware run "10 to 20% of them just, like, won't work or they'll burn out early" ([POD256 e085](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-09-07-e085.md)).
- **Canaan vs the leaders (October 2025).** Canaan's quiet baseboard units already run "18 joules per terra hash", the newly announced A16 is "which is 12 joules per terra hash" (industrial, with chips expected to trickle into home gadgets), and Bitmain's "s 23 is supposed to be nine" — Scott's heat-miner take: "don't care about the difference between nine and twelve jewels per terre hash" ([POD256 e092](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-10-29-e092.md))
- **Whatsminer's December 2025 line-up.** At Bitcoin Mina the M70 series launched: BitDeer's year-old claim of a "5 jewels per terahash Bitcoin miner" never shipped — "their best miner is 12.5 joules per terra hash, so not even close" — while Bitmain was "shooting for" "9.5 jewels per terahash to be released, quarter one twenty twenty six" ([POD256 e098](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-12-13-e098.md)). The M70S itself: "12.5 joules per terahash. That that's 3.6 kilowatts air cooled"; the M76S: "seven kilowatts immersion up to 440 terahash" ([POD256 e098](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-12-13-e098.md))
- **Unit economics on a dashboard (May 2026).** Rafa of Electron published electronics.dev, whose ASIC database includes a unit-economics view: an Antminer S23 Hydro "could earn a daily profit of $11.91" with breakeven "at 15¢ a kilowatt hour", the T19 Hydro on the other end would "lose $2.87 a day, breaking even at 3.8¢" — or "mine 1 Bitcoin today for a $117,000. So negative 57%" ([POD256 e115](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-05-27-e115.md)). The same database forecasts "2 joules per terahash" ahead ("2 joules per terahash. That's insane") and charts immersion releases stopping after 2025; at TEMS, "no one is bullish on immersion anymore" ([POD256 e115](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-05-27-e115.md)). On BitDeer's missed roadmap: they're "issuing press releases like no one's business" ([POD256 e115](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-05-27-e115.md))

> **Status: Disputed** — is there still a single-phase hydro Whatsminer? (December 2025 vs April 2026)
> When Whatsminer announced the M70 series, the heatpunks on the pod were bummed "because there was no announcement of the m 74" — "the four line is what designated that single phase power hydrominer" — leaving the M64/M54 as effectively "the only way to get hot water from a miner in North America where small businesses and homes have a single phase power, not three phase" ([POD256 e098](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-12-13-e098.md)). ## Dev fees, tuning and the open-firmware business model (2026)

- **Braiins persistent tuning (April 2026).** Braiins OS announced persistent tuning — re-tuning across power set points and temperature instead of one run. On the pod, Eco described conventional tuning runs that "can take quite a long time, sometimes upwards of an hour plus", and Scott noted sweet spots drift with chip aging, dust and temperature, so guess-and-check probing costs efficiency. The implementation is closed: "It's definitely closed source" — precisely the surface Mujina's open tuning hooks open up ([POD256 e112](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-04-15-e112.md)); several auto-tuning packages "have shown up" for Bitaxe already ([POD256 e112](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-04-15-e112.md))
- **The Braiins Mini Miner's dev-fee-free license.** Dev-fee free on any pool because its firmware "phones home to brains, and then the license activates in free mode" when attached to "a four chip miner" — encoded to stop people moving the board to bigger miners; Braiins OS is only dev-fee free when mining to Braiins Pool, while Mujina "does not have a dev fee" and can mine anywhere ([POD256 e112](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-04-15-e112.md))
- **The Vnish teardown (August 2026).** The 256 red team's first public target: Vnish "take a portion of your hash rate" as the dev fee, and blocking the fee server puts the miner in a reduced state — "reduce your hash rate by 10%", "which in many mining cases means it's just not profitable" ([POD256 e123](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-08-10-e123.md)). The audit itself cost "$40 and Kimi K3 credits" plus directed prompting — closed-weight model APIs make you "rack up $30 in tokens from, a single prompt" — and the hosts' conclusion is that tuning "special sauce" is now extractable, eroding the case for dev-fee firmware; solutions that "don't require a dev fee" already exist ([POD256 e123](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-08-10-e123.md))
- **The fee-server risk in one line (August 2026).** Vnish's license server has already gone down once — without it the firmware "throttles down like ten, twenty percent"; Scott's summary: "If we can't get our 2% cut, then you can't have 10% of your hash rate" ([POD256 e124](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-08-17-e124.md))
- **"Red Hat" service companies (July 2026).** Schnitzel (funded by RY3T for Nova) predicts custom open-firmware service shops as the business model — "making real money on top of free software" — and not just one of them: "The Red Hat is one of them"; "There is thousands of Red Hats in the world", likely specialized per use case ("Red Hat for solar power or for district heating") ([POD256 e120](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-07-15-e120.md))

- An insurance broker on the pod: home-miner hardware is the same infrastructure as an AI data center — "it's silicon, it's printed circuit boards, it's cooling fans, it's pumps" — but "the premiums are, like, eight times higher on the Bitcoin miners" because insurers "just hate the word Bitcoin"; the education gap runs to questions like "if my mind burns down" ([POD256 e106](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-03-04-e106.md))

## Mining-node economics (August 2026)

- Why miners skip nodes: "it's fairly expensive to set up a full archival node due to the storage requirements, and it doesn't really get you anything in your mining adventure" — "it doesn't get you any more like lottery tickets" — so at "$600" most people pass ([POD256 e124](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-08-17-e124.md)); Eco's phrasing: "it doesn't gain you any more lottery tickets" ([POD256 e123](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-08-10-e123.md))
- The fix being pitched: an "ideally $100 box" running a pruned node — pruned nodes are "first class citizens in every way", and "that is a 100% perfectly good node configuration to mine to. We have confirmation from doctor Konkolovas" (Konkolová; Eric Voskuil of Libbitcoin concurred) ([POD256 e123](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-08-10-e123.md)). The goal: "normalize HomeMiner's mining to your own pruned nodes" with a ready-to-go node image ([POD256 e123](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-08-10-e123.md))
- AssumeUTXO snapshots with checksums baked into Bitcoin Core let you "download these snapshots and run them on your pruned node and get going, much quicker" while full validation catches up in the background ([POD256 e123](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-08-10-e123.md))
- The hardware cut: "A two terabyte NVMe is a non starter" at hobbyist prices, but the LibreBoard's M.2 slot takes "a much more affordable, like, 32 gig or 64 gig drive" — a node inside the miner ([POD256 e123](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-08-10-e123.md)); or as Scott put it, "$600 worth of hardware to spin up a node" when you just want to try the concept ([POD256 e124](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-08-17-e124.md))

## Chip supply and the open-ASIC gap (2025-2026)

- The chicken-and-egg (September 2026): ASIC vendors "would very much like to" land "multimillion dollar orders for chips", but nobody can prototype their way to placing them; getting samples is "pulling teeth sometimes to just get a handful of ASIC samples" before you can order "a million dollars worth of chips" ([POD256 e126](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-09-21-e126.md))
- Heatbit is the standing exception — "at scale large enough to inquire with an ASIC vendor", doing "large scale production of Bitcoin mining systems that use ASIC chips from other vendors"; "a Heatbit is a fantastic example of a company building mining products from scratch" ([POD256 e126](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-09-21-e126.md))
- Supply isn't literally zero (August 2025): Eco showed a reel "Enough bit main chips to make a 100 ember ones", sourced through MegaMiner against the FUD that Bitmain chips are impossible to buy; the normal channel remains desoldering used miners ([POD256 e081](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-08-20-e081.md))
- What home miners actually want (April 2026): "if they just sold chips and other companies could make what they wanted" with their own payment structures and web shops ([POD256 e112](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-04-15-e112.md))
- The commoditization bet (September 2026): aluminum went from "whatever $40 a pound" to "dropped to like $2" once the process was common knowledge, and vendors sell "way more chips" at the lower price ([POD256 e127](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-09-28-e127.md)). ASICBoost is the cautionary version: "a 25% almost improvement in mining efficiency" was "actually patented in 2013", and Bitmain "just implemented it" from China anyway ([POD256 e127](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-09-28-e127.md))
