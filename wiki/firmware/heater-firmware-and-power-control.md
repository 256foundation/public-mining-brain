## Overview

Mining firmware is built for industrial sites that care about cooling and uptime, not for homes that care about holding a room or tank at a set temperature; industrial miners don't want temp-target features, which is why existing firmware lacks them [#2270 · 2024-11-30 · Skot Bitaxe] [#6402 · 2025-06-06 · Dane O] [#6403 · 2025-06-06 · Aadhi M]. Heat miners in the Hashrate Heatpunks group have therefore pieced together control from what exists: Braiins OS+ dynamic performance scaling (DPS), LuxOS ATM, Vnish thermal modes, Whatsminer low/normal/high power modes and power-limit API, aftermarket control boards (ePIC UMC, BCB100, Mara), fan boards like Njord, and external thermostats or Home Assistant. The recurring lessons: power changes are slow (often a full retune or restart), every firmware reports temperature differently, "sleep" still draws real power, a miner with no pool stops making heat unless you configure a fallback, and a true heat-first (temperature-target) firmware is still the open "holy grail" [#2268 · 2024-11-30 · Skot Bitaxe].

## Rules of thumb

- **Duty-cycle at an efficient wattage instead of running very low.** Efficiency tanks exponentially below 700-800W on an S19 even with LuxOS custom profiles; for 600W of heat need, run 1200W half the time if thermal mass allows cycles >20 minutes [#1079 · 2024-09-07 · Zack Bomsta] [#1082 · 2024-09-07 · Zack Bomsta]. Under 1200w an S19 is "not really worth it" [#1080 · 2024-09-07 · Toine Heat Reuse] [#1081 · 2024-09-07 · Zack Bomsta].
- **Efficiency peaks near nominal frequency.** Hash frequency is the main power lever (core voltage moves with it); Antminer nominal is around 500-600MHz depending on model, and efficiency suffers the further you run from it [#2289 · 2024-12-01 · Skot Bitaxe] [#2291 · 2024-12-01 · Skot Bitaxe] [#2309 · 2024-12-01 · Skot Bitaxe]. Underclocking helps only up to a point — "go too low and efficiency drops", and temps matter too [#2314 · 2024-12-01 · Jonathan Y] [#2316 · 2024-12-01 · Karl] [#2318 · 2024-12-01 · Jonathan Y].
- **For low heat demand, drop boards rather than wattage.** Running 2/3 boards gives lower power at better efficiency [#1146 · 2024-09-16 · Bob]; hashboards can be disabled in Braiins OS for tiny spaces — leave the disabled board in place for airflow or disconnect it from the PSU [#5720 · 2025-04-23 · Jake⚡️] [#5726 · 2025-04-23 · ₿ Minion]. Turn a board off in firmware rather than pulling the 18 pin ribbon [#5846 · 2025-04-29 · Toine Heat Reuse] [#5851 · 2025-04-29 · Karl].
- **Expect power changes to cost minutes, not seconds** (see Retune latency below) — design the control loop around it: widen thermostat deadbands, and prefer pause/resume over constant power scaling for room heat [#8077 · 2025-12-02 · Travis Bitkle] [#8079 · 2025-12-02 · Travis Bitkle].
- **Always configure no-internet behavior** (drain:// or equivalent) on any miner that is a primary heat source [#7860 · 2025-11-23 · Dane O] [#7931 · 2025-11-24 · Heatpunk Forum].
- **Know your firmware's temperature offset** before copying anyone else's setpoints (see Temperature reporting).
- **ASICs have a warm-up period before they hash**, which matters for intermittent winter mining [#260 · 2024-08-02 · Toine Heat Reuse].
- Simple can be enough: one system ran months on stock firmware with analog controls, then Braiins + analog, then Braiins + Home Assistant [#1123 · 2024-09-13 · R D]; another builder uses only two modes, low power in Spring/Fall and full power in winter, letting regular heat cover peaks [#940 · 2024-09-04 · Jonathan Y].

## Firmware options compared

### Braiins OS+ (DPS)

- **Thermostat-style DPS recipe:** find chip temp at max power with 25c ambient, then set DPS to downclock just below that, so room temp is approached slowly while uptime is kept; example: downclock by 300w at 70c chip temp, paired with a wifi thermometer/switch for on/off [#250 · 2024-08-02 · Toine Heat Reuse] [#243 · 2024-08-02 · Toine Heat Reuse] [#231 · 2024-08-02 · Toine Heat Reuse]. On S19K Pro, S19 XP and S21, room temps going from 20 to 25 drove chip temps from 60 - 70 fairly reliably [#243 · 2024-08-02 · Toine Heat Reuse].
- **Limits:** DPS won't scale back up after downscaling (Home Assistant can handle the upscale) [#382 · 2024-08-04 · Cody Harris]; it doesn't work when targeting low wattage — step size too big and adjustment too slow, so the miner overheats and restarts [#1371 · 2024-10-08 · Zack Bomsta]. Field tuning with fan fixed at 55% (443 CFM), hot 66C and OFF 24C: the miner ran high ~30 mins then downshifted to 2000w but would not drop to 1500w [#1140 · 2024-09-16 · Toine Heat Reuse] [#1145 · 2024-09-16 · Toine Heat Reuse].
- **Where DPS shines: water loops.** For fixed heat sinks, lower the hot temp from 80°C toward the desired run point (e.g. hot 72°C, danger 78°C) and let DPS manage power [#5159 · 2025-03-29 · Travis Bitkle]. Travis runs a hydro Antminer heating a concrete slab on DPS, powering up/down based on BTU dump ability, with hot set lower and target higher to keep the range tight — "set it and forget it" [#6665 · 2025-06-30 · Travis Bitkle] [#7013 · 2025-08-12 · Travis Bitkle]. Braiins autotune likewise turns itself down when a loop reaches heat saturation [#3561 · 2025-01-16 · Cade] [#3567 · 2025-01-16 · Cade]. Auto tune plus DPS with bigger wattage steps worked well, but it's hard without a heat dump [#5139 · 2025-03-29 · Cade]. A minimal water-heater setup: DPS plus a bang bang 220V timer switch, no Home Assistant [#4911 · 2025-03-27 · Cody Harris].
- DPS boost mode appeared in a newer firmware update (March 2025) [#4757 · 2025-03-23 · Tyler Stevens]. DPS is a firmware directive, not a control-board feature, and is the closest existing thing to automatic wattage-by-chip-temp [#2738 · 2024-12-13 · Toine Heat Reuse] [#2739 · 2024-12-13 · Brett Rowan].
- **Fans:** Braiins' fan management is a chief complaint for air heaters (irrelevant when immersed or waterblocked) [#4916 · 2025-03-28 · Mark | @satstackingpleb]; a fan set to 55 still ramps up every 15 minutes or so [#1348 · 2024-10-08 · Trevor Bello]. Workaround for startup roar: set the fan manually and run Braiins OS+ in "immersion mode", tuning to your highest tolerable cfm [#1686 · 2024-10-23 · Toine Heat Reuse] [#1687 · 2024-10-23 · Toine Heat Reuse]. Fans run at 100% on startup because they are powered before the OS boots [#1683 · 2024-10-23 · Brett Rowan].
- **Other notes:** Braiins worked best for Toine's AC Infinity fan setups, and its power reading matched his wall meter almost exactly [#2221 · 2024-11-29 · Toine Heat Reuse] [#2222 · 2024-11-29 · Toine Heat Reuse]. SSH access means control logic could run on-device [#2249 · 2024-11-29 · Brett Rowan]. It kept an S9 running with a hashboard that had multiple chip failures [#4874 · 2025-03-25 · Dylan Seib] [#4876 · 2025-03-25 · Dylan Seib]. Older BraiinsOS shows the 2.5% fee, with the discount appearing as a "rebate" [#6669 · 2025-06-30 · Dylan Seib] [#6671 · 2025-06-30 · Dane O]. Braiins can now be installed on S21s whose Bitmain firmware dates up to Sept 2025 (as of March 2026) [#9768 · 2026-03-30 · Jason]. On a 3-board S19 jPro on the APW12 120v mod, BraiinsOS shows a minimum of 944 watts [#8044 · 2025-12-01 · Travis Bitkle].

### LuxOS (ATM)

- ATM is LuxOS's analog of DPS (dynamic scaling on chosen variables) and throttles down to 800ish watts; curtailment is a separate feature [#1077 · 2024-09-07 · Tyler Stevens] [#4916 · 2025-03-28 · Mark | @satstackingpleb]. Zack Bomsta rated ATM "much better" than Braiins DPS for space heaters [#1357 · 2024-10-08 · Zack Bomsta].
- **Water-heater example (Josh):** ATM holds 62c and scales down as water gets hotter; lowest achievable power is around 1000w, so a timer sleep was planned for summer [#4912 · 2025-03-27 · Josh] [#4913 · 2025-03-27 · Josh]. With ATM timing set to 1 minute it adjusts quickly and overclocks as cold water enters during long showers; a cron job sleeps the miner at peak-rate times and at night [#5118 · 2025-03-29 · Josh]. At 130 (54c) tank temp the miner held 62c underclocked to 400mhz/2000w [#5120 · 2025-03-29 · Josh] [#5127 · 2025-03-29 · Josh]. Gotcha: Luxor does not allow shutting down individual boards via API while ATM is in use [#5118 · 2025-03-29 · Josh].
- **Standby/sleep** shuts down the PSU and keeps only the control board on — see Sleep, standby and idle draw.
- **Install and platform quirks:** LuxOS won't install unless the miner runs properly with all 3 boards and fans (Braiins has no such limit) [#1376 · 2024-10-08 · Jim ⚡️]; to install over Braiins, first uninstall Braiins via its UI or Braiins manager [#2564 · 2024-12-05 · Jonathan Y] [#2566 · 2024-12-05 · Cade]; LuxOS Commander didn't work on S21 with a firmware version after april 2024 (December 2024), and a newer-firmware Antminer can be downgraded with a thumb drive of earlier firmware via a micro USB adapter [#2594 · 2024-12-06 · Nicolas Drouin-Audet] [#2600 · 2024-12-06 · Cade]. The OS is a very limited Linux build with no crontab [#1908 · 2024-11-08 · Jim ⚡️] [#1912 · 2024-11-08 · Jim ⚡️]. Xilinx and BB control boards reportedly work better with LuxOS [#4734 · 2025-03-22 · Toine Heat Reuse], and it was the only firmware Karl found that keeps a KPro board hashing [#6879 · 2025-07-29 · Karl]. One miner that runs on Braiins showed an unresolved error on LuxOS [#3876 · 2025-02-07 · Toine Heat Reuse] [#3878 · 2025-02-07 · Toine Heat Reuse].
- **Integration:** Lux is "way better with api integration" [#2586 · 2024-12-05 · Cade]; a commercial pool-heater product automates via LuxOS APIs and "It works really well" [#2499 · 2024-12-05 · Nicolas Drouin-Audet], as does the 100acresranch controller [#1344 · 2024-10-08 · Mark | @satstackingpleb]. But Home Assistant never worked with Luxor for at least one builder [#5590 · 2025-04-17 · Josh] [#5118 · 2025-03-29 · Josh], and pyasic lacked set_power_limit for vnish/luxos as of December 2024 [#2413 · 2024-12-02 · Wilfred Allyn].
- **Fees:** running LuxOS waives the Luxor pool fee [#1088 · 2024-09-07 · Nicolas Drouin-Audet]; Luxor has higher fees than Braiins (not counting the original FPP pools) per Gianluca's tests [#8442 · 2025-12-26 · Gianluca L]. Bitcoin Mechanic reportedly said LuxOS creates problems with bolt12 payments (relayed, no detail) [#1856 · 2024-11-06 · Toine Heat Reuse].

### Vnish

- Chosen on StealthMiner for superior low volt operation, better thermal management, constant temp and near-constant fan speed [#1344 · 2024-10-08 · Mark | @satstackingpleb] [#1346 · 2024-10-08 · Mark | @satstackingpleb] [#1347 · 2024-10-08 · Mark | @satstackingpleb] [#1350 · 2024-10-08 · Mark | @satstackingpleb]. Its toolbox is "superuser" and can load any FW file [#1379 · 2024-10-08 · Mark | @satstackingpleb]. Both LuxOS and Vnish have advanced thermal modes that can hold a target chip temp; testing Vnish thermal mode with manual fan at 65-70% needed extra settings so it didn't try to tune the preset [#2274 · 2024-11-30 · Mark | @satstackingpleb] [#2278 · 2024-11-30 · Mark | @satstackingpleb] [#2279 · 2024-11-30 · Mark | @satstackingpleb].
- 67c was found best for jpro; manual tuning after autotune eked out about a percent; autotune didn't match LuxOS numbers for one builder but saves pool fees [#4725 · 2025-03-22 · Karl] [#4727 · 2025-03-22 · Karl] [#4729 · 2025-03-22 · Karl]. An S19 rated 88 TH runs on VNISH at 66TH and 2100 watts for basement heat [#3325 · 2025-01-12 · Zackery Miller].
- **January 2025 hotfix:** added a max fan-speed limit in auto mode, a minimum preset for the autoswitcher, a water inlet/outlet temp widget for S19 Pro Hydro / S19 Pro Plus Hydro, and S21 Hydro submodels HHB68502 and HHB68503 [#3022 · 2025-01-02 · Brett Rowan]. Gotcha: "AUTOTUNING MODE CAN EXCEED PRESET VALUES BY 10-20%" — machines tuned before the update were unaffected, but new tunes could overshoot power [#3022 · 2025-01-02 · Brett Rowan]. A "dev fee offline" status appeared on older S19s at the time; the dev fee was cut to the lowest rate for several days to compensate [#3018 · 2025-01-02 · Karl] [#3022 · 2025-01-02 · Brett Rowan].
- **Caution:** multiple Vnish-branded sites and X accounts exist; one builder hash-checked the vnish.com toolkit against vnish.net [#1444 · 2024-10-09 · Jim ⚡️] [#1446 · 2024-10-09 · Jim ⚡️].

> **Status: Disputed** — best firmware for heaters
> Pro-Vnish: low-volt operation, constant temps and steady fans [#1344 · 2024-10-08 · Mark | @satstackingpleb] [#1350 · 2024-10-08 · Mark | @satstackingpleb]. Pro-Braiins: Lux looked nice but Vnish was "barebones" years ago [#1343 · 2024-10-08 · Jonathan Y] [#1359 · 2024-10-08 · Jonathan Y]; Braiins power readings matched the wall where Lux's looked better than the meter [#2222 · 2024-11-29 · Toine Heat Reuse]. Pro-Lux: Toine later said he "Love luxor os" and switched, while Brett Rowan calls it a tinkering firmware [#2742 · 2024-12-13 · Toine Heat Reuse] [#2744 · 2024-12-13 · Brett Rowan]. Gianluca's tests (December 2025) found Luxor and Braiins similar in profitability at a given frequency, both better than Vnish [#8442 · 2025-12-26 · Gianluca L]. Best assessment: no consensus; pick by use case — Braiins DPS for hydro/water loops and accurate power, LuxOS ATM/standby for air heaters needing low idle draw and API control, Vnish for steady fans and fee savings.

### Stock Whatsminer firmware and power modes

- Whatsminers offer low, normal or high power modes and are typically controlled like a resistive heater: on or off [#3167 · 2025-01-08 · Patrick Patel]. One furnace builder sets the mode at install and prefers more miners on low power to limit power cycles [#3167 · 2025-01-08 · Patrick Patel]. Whatsminers start quickly, so sleep vs off hasn't mattered to him [#3178 · 2025-01-08 · Patrick Patel].
- **Custom power limits exist.** MBT API v3.0.0 has a power set function that adjusts dynamically, usable to track PV production without rebooting [#5257 · 2025-04-02 · Sam] [#5288 · 2025-04-03 · Sam]. The M64 is widely believed to be only High/Med/Low, but the API exposes a custom power-limit variable and the Heat Core team said dynamic adjustment is possible [#6983 · 2025-08-12 · Heatpunk Forum] [#5358 · 2025-04-08 · Tyler Stevens].
- **But re-tuning is slow.** A power limit can be set externally (e.g. Foreman), yet the miner always re-tunes after a change; "Adjust Upfreq Speed" speeds tuning but it still takes minutes and power fluctuates; "Power Fast Boot" ramps faster for curtailment players [#6989 · 2025-08-12 · Jarno] [#6997 · 2025-08-12 · Jarno] [#7000 · 2025-08-12 · Jarno]. For heating, reaching the target fast matters more than optimal tuning — WM tuning time "seems to kill the point" [#6999 · 2025-08-12 · Tyler Stevens] [#6993 · 2025-08-12 · Tyler Stevens]. Full API access requires handling the password setting; pyasic's btminer RPC code is a reference for commands [#6995 · 2025-08-12 · Jarno] [#6996 · 2025-08-12 · Brett Rowan].
- Travis's intuition: air-cooled Whatsminers don't tolerate power scaling the way Antminers do on Braiins DPS, and frequency fiddling may have killed some old units; the M64's upfreq time is shorter than his air-cooled units' [#8076 · 2025-12-02 · Travis Bitkle] [#8080 · 2025-12-02 · Travis Bitkle]. Whatsminers also had trouble with Braiins Manager scheduled curtailment [#8074 · 2025-12-02 · Travis Bitkle].
- **Aftermarket options are thin.** Braiins can run on some Whatsminers and Rosseau gave a little more flexibility than stock [#1917 · 2024-11-10 · Zinjinlao] [#1918 · 2024-11-10 · Cody Harris]; Rousseau is better described as an interface hack over stock [#1684 · 2024-10-23 · Brett Rowan]. By March 2025 Rosseau was defunct and BixBit underwhelmed — it cuts startup from ~90seconds to about 10 seconds but carries a 2% dev fee [#4491 · 2025-03-13 · Cody Harris]. Whatsminer offers a free "Air to Liquid" firmware for aftermarket fans or immersion [#1646 · 2024-10-21 · Toine Heat Reuse]. Whatsminer is significantly harder to reverse engineer than Antminer [#5622 · 2025-04-18 · Skot Bitaxe], and control of M64 boiler units remained "a major hurdle" without unlocked firmware [#6324 · 2025-05-31 · Dane O].

> **Status: Disputed** — Whatsminer on/off responsiveness
> Patrick Patel's bang-bang thermostat control saw fast boots, hashing in under a minute, soft PSU shutdowns and no negative effects [#5204 · 2025-03-31 · Patrick Patel] [#5205 · 2025-03-31 · Patrick Patel]. Pat Kelly described whatsminers as slower to come back for demand response, possibly older units on outdated firmware [#5202 · 2025-03-31 · Pat Kelly | Fog Hashing - Director of Sales, Global] [#5206 · 2025-03-31 · Pat Kelly | Fog Hashing - Director of Sales, Global]. Best assessment: newer units on current firmware boot fast enough for on/off control; on-the-fly power changes remain slow.

## Retune and restart latency

How fast a miner can change power determines what kind of control loop works.

- Skot's summary (December 2024): "Luxor takes _minutes_ to change freq. Braiins does a full restart. Bitmain just doesn’t do it." [#2310 · 2024-12-01 · Skot Bitaxe] — likely to keep the PLL stable and voltage domains balanced on Bitmain chips [#2336 · 2024-12-01 · Skot Bitaxe].
- Braiins OS shuts the miner off and back on when power is adjusted; changing wattage took 2+ minutes for one user; S19J retunes slowly while S19K and S19XP "tune way faster" [#1855 · 2024-11-06 · R D] [#1880 · 2024-11-06 · Cody Harris] [#1881 · 2024-11-06 · Toine Heat Reuse]. Live scaling only works in certain ranges on Braiins OS; outside them a power-target change from HA stops and restarts mining [#1418 · 2024-10-08 · R D] [#1419 · 2024-10-08 · Brett Rowan] [#2372 · 2024-12-02 · R D].
- Brett Rowan says BOS+ shouldn't restart for small enough changes — Braiins told him they added this via gRPC on X19s, "maybe it doesnt work or they changed it" [#2376 · 2024-12-02 · Brett Rowan] [#2381 · 2024-12-02 · Brett Rowan].

> **Status: Outdated** (2024-12-02)
> Cody Harris saw hass-miner-directed scaling events take 10 seconds in winter 2023-24; after updating BOS (and probably HA) they reverted to 2 min restarts between scaling [#2382 · 2024-12-02 · Cody Harris].

- **Why it matters:** with a miner scaling on boiler fluid temp, a long restart lets the fluid cool further and triggers a deeper, unneeded scaling event; shorter startup would allow tighter tuning [#2383 · 2024-12-02 · Cody Harris].
- LuxOS ATM with timing at 1 minute adjusts quickly on a water heater [#5118 · 2025-03-29 · Josh]; Whatsminer power-limit changes force a multi-minute re-tune [#6989 · 2025-08-12 · Jarno] [#6997 · 2025-08-12 · Jarno]; Njord on an S17 while tuning will cycle off and on "for about two days" before it just runs [#6979 · 2025-08-12 · Travis Bitkle]; the ePIC control board was described as tuning faster [#4295 · 2025-02-26 · Dane O].

## Power-target gotchas

- **Single-board Braiins:** Braiins power settings assume 3 hashboards — "if you set it at 3kw then disable two HB it will run at 1kw. If you set it at 1kw, that board will run at 333w" [#2242 · 2024-11-29 · Cade]. This explained a single S19 card on 120v showing only around 13-14th/s when it "should be around 32th" [#2223 · 2024-11-29 · Jonathan Y] [#2240 · 2024-11-29 · Jonathan Y].
- **Firmware power estimates run low:** a single jpro board measured at the wall showed "the firmware overshoots by about 100w" [#8888 · 2026-01-26 · Karl]; Altair BitChimney's wall draw was a bit over the firmware estimate [#8913 · 2026-01-28 · Barnminer Barnmyna]; Lux's numbers looked better than the wall meter for Toine [#2222 · 2024-11-29 · Toine Heat Reuse]. Firmware chip temp and power readings may not be accurate [#5122 · 2025-03-29 · Josh] [#5124 · 2025-03-29 · Cody Harris]. Use a wall meter.
- **Autotune overshoot:** the Vnish January 2025 hotfix warned autotune can exceed preset values by 10-20% [#3022 · 2025-01-02 · Brett Rowan].
- **Sizing:** one hydronic-floor plan set the power limit around 2700 watts based on last year's coldest month and let firmware adjust down [#380 · 2024-08-04 · Tyler Stevens].
- hass-miner with Braiins and DPS had a bug in January 2025 — avoid relying on default values in newer versions [#3468 · 2025-01-15 · Brett Rowan].

## Temperature reporting differences

- LuxOS reports board (sensor) temp as-is; Braiins, Vnish and Bitmain add 15°C to estimate chip temp: "65 in Lux = 80 in Braiins" [#1677 · 2024-10-23 · Jim ⚡️] [#1679 · 2024-10-23 · Jim ⚡️] [#5158 · 2025-03-29 · csh2000]. Toine read the offset as LuxOS under-reporting by 5-10, evident because its 'Dangerous Temperature' max is only 75C [#4723 · 2025-03-22 · Toine Heat Reuse] [#4774 · 2025-03-23 · Toine Heat Reuse]. Karl asked whether 75c critical on Lux equals 90c stock (unanswered) [#6879 · 2025-07-29 · Karl].
- Home Assistant can show "miner temp" and chip temp as significantly different values [#5122 · 2025-03-29 · Josh] [#5124 · 2025-03-29 · Cody Harris].
- **How hot is safe?** Vnish's default critical chip temp is 90c; Karl raised his to critical 90, target 80, hot 85 for more runtime between water draws [#2657 · 2024-12-09 · Karl] [#2666 · 2024-12-09 · Karl] [#2676 · 2024-12-09 · Karl]; Michael Schmid runs "90 target, 95 hot, 100 critical" and gets water "easy to 140-145F" [#2659 · 2024-12-09 · Michael Schmid @Schnitzel] [#2662 · 2024-12-09 · Michael Schmid @Schnitzel]. Later, 90c was called "too hot" on Vnish [#8885 · 2026-01-26 · David Campos].

> **Status: Disputed** — maximum safe chip temperature for water heating
> R D's hottest observed chip temp is 77-78°; 80 is probably safe but "way less efficient J/Th" [#2658 · 2024-12-09 · R D]. Cody Harris stresses "about 70C" [#2680 · 2024-12-09 · Cody Harris]. Schmid and Karl run 80-90c+ [#2986 · 2024-12-29 · Karl]. Mechanism: ASIC chips tolerate heat, but the i2c communication chips glitch at high temps, breaking hashboard-to-controlboard communication, and the control board doesn't recover until mining fully restarts [#2668 · 2024-12-09 · Michael Schmid @Schnitzel]. S19JPRO efficiency drops above ~60° [#1333 · 2024-10-08 · Jim ⚡️]. Best assessment: high setpoints work for some immersion builds but trade efficiency and i2c reliability; remember the Lux/Braiins offset when comparing numbers.

## Sleep, standby and idle draw

- Firmware sleep still consumes power, so a wifi PDU switch is needed to truly cut it at the target temp [#255 · 2024-08-02 · Toine Heat Reuse] [#257 · 2024-08-02 · Toine Heat Reuse]. Antminers draw 150w to 240w in standby; Braiins sleep mode is about 200w [#742 · 2024-08-23 · Toine Heat Reuse] [#257 · 2024-08-02 · Toine Heat Reuse].
- LuxOS standby/curtailment shuts down the PSU and keeps only the control board on — reported as only 25 watts and as 20 Watts [#741 · 2024-08-23 · Tyler Stevens] [#1077 · 2024-09-07 · Tyler Stevens] [#3172 · 2025-01-08 · Tyler Stevens].
- Low-idle alternative: run miners at a very low rate so they never shut off, ramping up when heat is needed (secondhand report of TronMonGone on LuxOS) [#3171 · 2025-01-08 · Trevor Bello].
- Standby via HA/hass-miner avoids cutting physical power and keeps boards ready [#2388 · 2024-12-02 · R D]; a thermostat can put the ASIC into standby/pause instead of cutting power [#1130 · 2024-09-14 · Jonathan Y].
- **Ethernet vs power:** cutting the ethernet link instead of power is probably gentler on hardware, but fans keep running with no heat, which confuses households; smart plugs give a manual button [#7856 · 2025-11-23 · Jarno] [#7857 · 2025-11-23 · Josh].
- Bang-bang vs ramping for longevity: no data yet (March 2025) [#4765 · 2025-03-23 · Tyler Stevens]. Ramping exploits downclocking efficiency and keeps hashboards at steadier temps with longer uptime [#4766 · 2025-03-23 · Toine Heat Reuse] [#4768 · 2025-03-23 · Toine Heat Reuse]; heat-reuse duty adds chip stress through bigger heat swings [#3500 · 2025-01-16 · Brett Rowan] [#3512 · 2025-01-16 · Brett Rowan].

## No-internet behavior and "limp mode"

- **The failure mode:** when the internet went out, miners stopped hashing, blew cold air, and the house got cold [#2897 · 2024-12-23 · Toine Heat Reuse].
- **Braiins `drain://`:** a pool URL that wastes hashrate locally when no pool is reachable, so the miner keeps heating [#3216 · 2025-01-08 · Brett Rowan] [#4024 · 2025-02-13 · Cody Harris]. Any string seems to work after `drain://` (example `drain://mine.ocean.xyz.3334`) [#3235 · 2025-01-10 · Cody Harris] [#3236 · 2025-01-10 · Brett Rowan]. Recommended heater pool order: Pool 1 DATUM, Pool 2 Ocean, Pool 3 drain:// [#4012 · 2025-02-12 · Cody Harris]. This resolved the late-2024 rumor of a "fake" last pool that worked on BOS but "didn't work on Lux" [#2932 · 2024-12-24 · Cade] [#2934 · 2024-12-24 · Jonathan Y] [#2940 · 2024-12-24 · Jonathan Y] [#2939 · 2024-12-24 · Nicolas Drouin-Audet].
- By November 2025, "limp mode" was the forum's name for this: Braiins' drain:// plus similar settings on Epic UMC OS and Luxor [#7931 · 2025-11-24 · Heatpunk Forum]. LuxOS had been reported building a hash-with-no-pool feature in January 2025 [#3207 · 2025-01-08 · Nicolas Drouin-Audet]; a past LuxOS bug that kept the miner "mining" when the network dropped has since been fixed [#7857 · 2025-11-23 · Josh].
- **Whatsminers:** no drain-like option; a Raspberry Pi proxy as a local "drain" proved harder than expected (the slush pool proxy repo is deprecated) [#3217 · 2025-01-08 · Patrick Patel] [#4013 · 2025-02-12 · Patrick Patel] [#4020 · 2025-02-12 · Patrick Patel].
- A local node plus local pool only really helps for 10-20 minute outages [#1052 · 2024-09-04 · Brett Rowan].

> **Status: Disputed** — does a backup pool alone keep heat on?
> In September 2024, setting a last backup pool kept BOS miners working offline [#1041 · 2024-09-04 · Jonathan Y] [#1043 · 2024-09-04 · Brett Rowan]; Vnish did not keep hashing offline in a test, suggesting Braiins-specific behavior, while others argued pool handling should be identical across firmware [#1073 · 2024-09-06 · Karl] [#1074 · 2024-09-06 · Brett Rowan] [#1094 · 2024-09-07 · Toine Heat Reuse]. Best assessment (as of November 2025): use an explicit limp-mode setting (drain:// on Braiins, equivalents on ePIC and Luxor) and test it by unplugging the network.

## The temperature-target ("heat-first") debate

- **The ask:** a home heater wants a temperature target; Braiins has power and hashrate targets but no temp target that adjusts them [#2224 · 2024-11-29 · Jonathan Y]. Its board target-temp setting (e.g. 70c) runs fans at 100% [#2234 · 2024-11-29 · Jonathan Y] [#2237 · 2024-11-29 · Jonathan Y]; manual inline-fan control while the ASIC stays inside its heat target works well [#2238 · 2024-11-29 · Jonathan Y]. Jonathan tried to get Braiins to build heat-primary firmware but "it never came about" [#2227 · 2024-11-29 · Jonathan Y].

> **Status: Disputed** — is a temp-driven tune feasible?
> Brett Rowan: temperature is mostly fan-dependent and no stable chip tuning exists for a given temperature, so temp-driven tuning is "pretty much not feasible" [#2245 · 2024-11-29 · Brett Rowan]. Toine: with fan speed fixed there is a fairly static ambient-to-chip relationship; lock the fan to an allowable dB and downclock to hold chip temp steady — which Skot called "the holy grail of mining heat reuse firmware" [#2269 · 2024-11-30 · Toine Heat Reuse] [#2272 · 2024-11-30 · Toine Heat Reuse] [#2268 · 2024-11-30 · Skot Bitaxe]. Best assessment: partial versions exist (Vnish/LuxOS thermal modes, Braiins DPS, LuxOS ATM holding chip temp on water loops, Superheat's water-temp clock control) [#2274 · 2024-11-30 · Mark | @satstackingpleb] [#4912 · 2025-03-27 · Josh] [#8609 · 2026-01-08 · Tyler Stevens]; a general-purpose heat-first firmware does not.

- **External-sensor alternative:** controllers such as Heatmine and the 100acresranch Themrohashostat adjust mining power from an external temperature rather than internal sensors like DPS/ATM [#1726 · 2024-10-28 · Tyler Stevens] [#1727 · 2024-10-28 · Tyler Stevens]. The pool-heater developer who built his own temp control found it "performing just as good" as the firmware's later version and kept his own [#2515 · 2024-12-05 · Nicolas Drouin-Audet]. Dylan plans PID control to autoscale power limits to hold room temperature [#8075 · 2025-12-02 · Dylan Seib].
- **Asking vendors:** Luxor's David Urlacher offered to relay heat-reuse firmware requests [#528 · 2024-08-09 · Tyler Stevens]; one proposed Luxor feature was linking ATM to curtailment so temp control scales to 0 hashrate with only the control board on [#1077 · 2024-09-07 · Tyler Stevens]. Maintainers can be approached, but some asks are hardware-impossible [#1694 · 2024-10-24 · Jim ⚡️] [#1697 · 2024-10-24 · Jim ⚡️] [#1698 · 2024-10-24 · Brett Rowan]. 256 Foundation's Mujina firmware lead joined in April 2025 seeking heater feature input [#5409 · 2025-04-10 · Tyler Stevens] [#5410 · 2025-04-10 · Ryan].

## Chip and firmware features heaters want

- A chip that draws a set wattage even when not mining (no ramping, keeps heating with the internet down) and runs at 100C+ [#3205 · 2025-01-08 · Michael Schmid @Schnitzel]; a heater chip tolerating 250F/120C would unlock much more reuse [#784 · 2024-08-23 · Jonathan Y]; fatigue testing for thermal cycling [#3206 · 2025-01-08 · Patrick Patel].
- Counterpoint: constant wattage can be done in software without chip changes; 100C+ isn't simple and special casings are expensive [#3209 · 2025-01-08 · Alex HeatBit]. S9 chips could run above 100C but other hashboard components limited the device to 80C [#3212 · 2025-01-08 · Tyler Stevens].
- One startup's ASIC optimizes for a target temperature with direct voltage control instead of clock tuning; its 12nm masks are gone and it was seeking funding for 3nm chips (February 2025) [#3854 · 2025-02-04 · PizzAndy] [#3855 · 2025-02-04 · PizzAndy] [#3856 · 2025-02-04 · PizzAndy].
- Travis's unmet wish list (August 2025): "Single phase, high water temp, BraiinsOS DPS all together" [#7013 · 2025-08-12 · Travis Bitkle].
- A simple heater control interface remains an open problem because most firmware focuses on cooling [#6402 · 2025-06-06 · Dane O] [#6403 · 2025-06-06 · Aadhi M].

## Control boards and fan boards

- **Aftermarket landscape (December 2024):** "The only 3 I know of are the ePIC UMC, BCB100 (braiins), and the Mara board" [#2737 · 2024-12-13 · Brett Rowan].
- **ePIC UMC:** lots of tuning options and API settings, no dev fee (paid upfront with the board), faster tuning, and a vendor open to heater features [#4295 · 2025-02-26 · Dane O] [#4310 · 2025-02-26 · Dane O] [#4314 · 2025-02-26 · Dane O]; the V5 is Loki-compatible with self-tuning or perpetual tuning, price cited as 150$ (October 2024) [#1721 · 2024-10-28 · Trevor Bello] [#1744 · 2024-10-28 · Trevor Bello]; reported working great in immersion [#6670 · 2025-06-30 · Dane O]. Downsides: you can't run Braiins or LuxOS on it [#4308 · 2025-02-26 · Sam Korn], and its web API on port 4028 differs from the CGMiner RPC API most miners expose, causing conflicts in mixed fleets [#2263 · 2024-11-29 · Brett Rowan] [#2265 · 2024-11-29 · Brett Rowan].
- **Braiins BCB100:** saves the dev fee; two ran in S19JPRO immersion for about 4 months with no problems, though the owner would only buy one to replace a broken stock board [#4285 · 2025-02-26 · Cody Harris] [#4290 · 2025-02-26 · Cody Harris]. Braiins control boards going open source could become the de-facto option, but lack of open firmware for that board limits it [#4883 · 2025-03-26 · Brett Rowan] [#4884 · 2025-03-26 · Skot Bitaxe]; in April 2026 Aadhi was hacking a BCB100 that shipped without Braiins OS toward an open-source replacement firmware [#9832 · 2026-04-10 · Aadhi M] [#9839 · 2026-04-10 · Aadhi M].
- **Cvitek (stock S19):** sometimes reboots back into stock firmware — one user's basement went cold [#4284 · 2025-02-26 · Sam Korn].
- **Mara board / MaraFW:** base S19 / S19 Pro is incompatible [#8853 · 2026-01-23 · David Campos]; auto modes use chip tuning and generally give the best efficiency, apw7 results untested [#8855 · 2026-01-23 · David Campos] [#8859 · 2026-01-23 · David Campos]; auto fan control works with only 2 fans [#8851 · 2026-01-23 · Karl]; firmware throttles or shuts off before dangerous temps [#8884 · 2026-01-26 · Karl]. A single jpro board hit 25.5j on 110v with 2 quiet fans, chip temp of 65c [#8886 · 2026-01-26 · Karl] [#8889 · 2026-01-26 · Karl].

> **Status: Disputed** — Mara control boards
> Trevor's Mara board in a modified Loki rig left the boards unable to hash after two weeks ("Dont use it") [#7866 · 2025-11-23 · Karl] [#7867 · 2025-11-23 · Trevor Bello]. Karl later ran an 88-chip S19 and a JPro board successfully on a Mara board [#8825 · 2026-01-22 · Karl] [#8886 · 2026-01-26 · Karl]. Best assessment (as of January 2026): works on compatible boards; check model compatibility first.

- **Njord (fan board):** keeps an inline fan between 300 and 700 cfm depending on chip temp, built around the 8-inch AC Infinity fan [#560 · 2024-08-10 · Toine Heat Reuse] [#617 · 2024-08-10 · Toine Heat Reuse]; requires a fan tach signal so it can't be fooled into running with a stopped fan [#1581 · 2024-10-15 · Zack Bomsta]; handles fan control and fan spoofing "all in one" for S19 fan swaps [#6441 · 2025-06-09 · Travis Bitkle]. An Njord + S17 attempt used Braiins OS version 23.01 manual setting at 70 percent [#6976 · 2025-08-12 · Shawn Flowers].
- **Splitting an S19:** one control board, PSU, enclosure and cooling per hashboard spreads load across house circuits; with a Loki you can use an S9 PSU [#4327 · 2025-02-27 · Josh]. Set up the control board before connecting hashboards [#5545 · 2025-04-16 · Toine Heat Reuse] [#5548 · 2025-04-16 · Karl] [#5551 · 2025-04-16 · Nick]; pivotalpleb.com documents per-firmware Loki configuration [#5554 · 2025-04-16 · Travis Bitkle].
- **Open-source path:** getting Mujina onto miners is "Much harder on any miner that doesn’t already have aftermarket firmware", and Whatsminer is completely different from Bitmain [#10230 · 2026-06-10 · Tyler Stevens] [#10234 · 2026-06-10 · Brett Rowan].

## Where firmware sits in the control stack

"Firmware" like Braiins, Vnish or LuxOS is an application on Linux, and the firmware (not hardware) provides the API [#1776 · 2024-10-30 · Zack Bomsta] [#1778 · 2024-10-30 · Brett Rowan] [#1766 · 2024-10-30 · Brett Rowan]. Above it sit API/SSH/web API, abstraction libraries (pyasic, asic-rs), then apps like hass-miner [#1752 · 2024-10-30 · Brett Rowan] [#1753 · 2024-10-30 · Brett Rowan] [#8421 · 2025-12-24 · Dan Sokil]. Braiins OS has a CGMiner-style API, but settings changes need its gRPC API or the REST API built on it [#8424 · 2025-12-24 · Brett Rowan] [#8431 · 2025-12-24 · Brett Rowan]. See the Home Assistant article for automation patterns.
