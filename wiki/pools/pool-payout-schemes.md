## Overview

How pools actually pay miners, why custody shows up, and what the community considers the honest trade-offs. The recurring 256F position: any pooled pool is a middleman, and there is a cost to that 'in both money and freedom' [#780 · 2024-04-07 · Skot Bitaxe] — which is why direct-from-coinbase payout designs get so much attention here.

## The core mechanics (community explainer)

- FPPS requires a custodian because it pays a constant expected value over time; PPLNS instead pays on share value submitted in a window near when a block is found [#717 · 2024-04-06 · Brett Rowan]
- Pooling math: 1% of hashrate in a 50% pool → the pool finds ~50% of blocks and you get 2% of each; same expected value as solo, just smoothed [#737 · 2024-04-06 · Brett Rowan]
- SPPLNS (proposed for a trustless public pool): pay the highest-n miners directly from the coinbase, with everyone else rotating through remaining coinbase payout slots — slightly more stable than solo, no custodian [#719 · 2024-04-06 · Brett Rowan] [#729 · 2024-04-06 · Brett Rowan] [#730 · 2024-04-06 · Brett Rowan]
- 'Solo pool' = hosted solo/lottery mining: whole block or nothing; the value-add is that they run the node + stratum job generation so you don't have to [#716 · 2024-04-06 · Skot Bitaxe] [#734 · 2024-04-06 · Skot Bitaxe] [#740 · 2024-04-06 · Brett Rowan]
- Large pools' difficulty algorithms aren't built for kh/s-class devices (NerdMiner/Bitaxe), which is why tiny hashers get rejected on big pools [#749 · 2024-04-06 · Jstefanop]

## Share mechanics and luck (book canon)

The community math above is the same math the books teach, with the knobs exposed.

- **Share targets and the proportional split (Mastering Bitcoin, 2nd ed., 2017).** Pool miners hash against a share target "typically more than 1,000 times easier than the bitcoin network's target", and "When someone in the pool successfully mines a block, the reward is earned by the pool and then shared with all miners in proportion to the number of shares they contributed" (§10.11.2) ([Mastering Bitcoin ch. 10 extract](../../raw/books/mastering-bitcoin/ch10-mining-extract.md)). The same section's worked example (2017 numbers): 14 TH/s hardware at ~$2,500 solo-mines a block roughly once every 4 years (98% probability over four years at a 3 EH/s network), while pool participation yields "approximately $50 to $60 per week" — variance smoothing at the same expected value, which is what the pooling-math bullet in the community explainer above describes.
- **The pool operator's fee, as the book describes it:** "The owner of the pool server is called the pool operator, and he charges pool miners a percentage fee" — in exchange the pool server runs a full node so miners avoid "at least 100 to 150 GB of persistent storage (disk) and at least 2 to 4 GB of memory (RAM)", speaking "Stratum (STM) or GetBlockTemplate (GBT)" (§10.11.2). P2Pool (proposed 2011) removed the operator with a share chain mined "one share block every 30 seconds" but is "conceivably vulnerable to 51% attacks against the share chain itself" — the ancestor of every design in [Decentralized Pool Designs](../protocols/decentralized-pool-designs.md).
- **Share difficulty and accounting (Braiins Mining Handbook ch. 47, 2022).** Share difficulty scales per miner — "a miner with 100 PH/s will have a higher share difficulty... than a miner with 15 TH/s" — tuned so miners submit "about once every 2-3 seconds"; accounting is "1 share = 1 proof of work on difficulty 1", and a hash below share target D is worth D shares, so pools validate whale miners without linearly scaling their own verification work ([Braiins Mining Handbook ch. 47](../../raw/books/braiins-mining-handbook/06-mining-pools-shares-luck.md)). Verification is cheap: "the pool simply repeats the same hash computation that you did to produce the shares in the first place" (p. 49). Stale/rejected shares are submissions landing "after the block for those shares has already been found" — the milliseconds after a block find. And the structural point under every custody debate on this page: "without shares, pools would have to redo ALL the hashes that miners do... public mining pools couldn't exist without shares" (p. 49).
- **Luck, defined.** "Pool luck is defined as the expected number of shares to find a block divided by the actual number of shares it took for the pool to find a block" (p. 51). The book's worked example: a pool streaming 100 shares/s with 600,000 expected shares per block should find a block about every 100 minutes; finishing at 300,000 shares is "600k/300k*100% = 200%" luck, needing 1,200,000 is 50%. "luck always trends towards 100% over time—it's just math", luck is indifferent to other pools ("it doesn't matter whether other miners / pools find 20 blocks or 0 blocks in that time"), and at a constant share of pool hashrate "luck translates 1:1 with your actual vs. expected mining rewards" (pp. 51–53) — the book-canon basis for Ocean's "96% luck" first-year claim and the Luxor dispute in the sovereignty panel above.
- **Reported vs estimated hashrate.** Dashboard estimates use a 720-block (~5-day) window — "this is why different dashboards can have different numbers—there's no one 'correct' way to do it" (p. 54); "As long as pool operators are honest in reporting their hashrate, the reported hashrate values will always be more accurate than the estimated hashrate values" (p. 54). The 2022 book states the transparency demand Schnitzel's panel made in 2026.
- **What Stratum V2 changes about pay (Handbook ch. 56+63, 2022).** Under V2's Job Negotiation, "every miner is paid based on the value of the shares they submit, not based on the value of the block that gets mined" — template value, not block luck, sets the payout rate (p. 65). The book's 2022 custody bound: "Pools send payouts frequently (multiple times per day in most cases), so the financial risk for miners of not getting paid for valid work is minimal. Meanwhile, pools who don't pay their miners risk permanent reputational damage" (p. 67) ([Braiins Mining Handbook ch. 56+63](../../raw/books/braiins-mining-handbook/07-stratum-v2.md)) — compare the HPS 2026 share-counting-transparency panel point above.

## Solo & coinbase-direct pools in the wild

- Laurentia Pool (spun up by Dr. Con & MFB) pays directly from the coinbase; been around a few years as of April 2024 [#762 · 2024-04-06 · econoalchemist]
- FutureBit funded a FOSS grant that modernized solo.ckpool for individual use; ships in Apollo OS 2, runs on any hardware, efficient enough for a Raspberry Pi; one user ran a 10 PH / 100-worker farm off a single Apollo [#953 · 2024-05-17 · Jstefanop] [#954 · 2024-05-17 · Jstefanop] [#962 · 2024-05-18 · Jstefanop] [#963 · 2024-05-18 · Jstefanop]
- Public Pool is another self-hosted solo pool option; the classic argument against self-hosted solo is stale-block risk, which a cloud service could mitigate with proper peering [#966 · 2024-05-19 · Skot Bitaxe] [#969 · 2024-05-19 · Skot Bitaxe]
- Wilson pool (Wilson Mining, factory-direct Whatsminer sellers): low pool fees, 130 TH minimum payout; community members pointed hashrate at it to test in mid-2024; they also run a proxy for smaller miners [#1031 · 2024-06-05 · Barnminer Barnmyna] [#1028 · 2024-06-05 · B] [#1048 · 2024-06-08 · Barnminer Barnmyna]
- Block 899826 mined by Solo CK for ₿3.15133486 (June 2025); szarka noted CK's template was just default Core — 'any block not mined by Antpool or Foundry is a good block' [#2317 · 2025-06-04 · ₿usiness Cat] [#2324 · 2025-06-04 · szarka] [#2329 · 2025-06-04 · szarka]
- Who mines solo and why: speculation that big solo-block hash is often rented lottery mining [#2338 · 2025-06-05 · Steve Barbour] [#2331 · 2025-06-04 · Elijah Sanders]

**Coinbase-direct mechanics, per the UnderMine 2025 DATUM tutorial** ([DATUM tutorial](../../raw/history/heatpunks-summit/20251111-undermine-2025-datum-setup-tutorial-with-bitcoin-mechanic.md)):

- "Ocean pays Bitcoin addresses. It doesn't pay people" — "We are not a people pool. We're a Bitcoin pool and no other pool operates this way" — knowing only addresses is also what keeps it outside "annoying regulation" [00:17:20-00:17:51]
- Coinbase-generated coins "can't be spent for a hundred network blocks" — a reorg would erase them entirely, hence the network-enforced cooldown [00:16:18-00:16:49]. Some wallets "just won't show up in your wallet" as a result; "Ironically, the exchange Coinbase was a good example of that. So, but they fixed it now" [00:16:49-00:17:20]
- Pool-side coinbase sizing is per-miner: old Antminer firmware and LuxOS get only "16 outputs" of coinbase payout; new Antminer firmware earns finders coinbases paying "70 or 80 miners" in-block; Bitaxes "can handle enormous coin bases" [00:14:14-00:19:24]

## Payout sovereignty panel (HPS 2026)

The HPS 2026 talk "Your Heat, Hash & Rules: Pool Payout Sovereignty for Heatpunks" staged the scheme landscape with three operators, as named in their on-stage introductions: Evan of VN PRC (hashpool.dev), Bitcoin Mechanic (Ocean) and Michael Schnitzell (256 Foundation, Hydrapool) ([HPS 2026 Talk 14](../../raw/history/heatpunks-summit/2026/20260305-hps-2026-your-heat-hash-rules-pool-payout-sovereignty-talk-14.md) [00:00:01-00:05:29]).

- **The panel's scheme rundown** [00:07:17-00:08:55]: FPPS — "I affectionately refer to this as cucked mining. You get zero say in what goes in the block templates"; Ocean changed that "with DATM and SV2 is coming out", where "you can help build your own block templates" but "you still have to defer typically to the pool above for the Coinbase"; PPLNS; Tides — "kind of ppls with a larger window"; solo/lottery mining; Parasite Pool, which "Got their first block today" — "Today, this morning" — pooled mining where "Whoever finds the block gets one whole Bitcoin out of the Coinbase and then the other two and some change gets split up in a ppl style"; and the "demand pool" (see [Decentralized Pool Designs](../protocols/decentralized-pool-designs.md)) "doing something called slices" — "It's tides but not with any of the real time tracking because that's too difficult for most people to do. So it basically turns the pool back into a bank again. But it has five capital letters like Tides does as well."
- **Hydrapool pitch from the same panel**: a from-scratch Rust rebuild of CKPool ("it's built in C that most people don't know anymore and it's not very good well documented"), runnable "either as solo mining" or as a PPLNS pool — "we think the whole FPS like you need you're basically the bank as a pool then"; running at pool 256foundation.org, "you will not get any sets if you send your hash there, but you will support open source mining" (sets = sats, per caption); Docker containers and Ansible scripts in progress [00:05:29-00:06:47].
- **FPPS as a paid predictability product.** "FPPS is a very specific product. It's for a minor that never wants any ambiguity around what they're going to get paid to make it completely predictable. That is really expensive" — "You're getting, you know, perfect strawberries all year round no matter what the weather is"; the price: "they will charge a lot" [00:26:10-00:26:46]. Ocean "doesn't optimize for variance reduction" — it "optimizes for revenue": "Ocean will have awful periods where it just doesn't find a block for like four or five days. And that's and it should be every 14 hours at current hash rate. If you can handle that, then you earn more money" [00:27:17]; "most miners can't because most miners aren't paying energy bills every 24 hours" — they still get variance reduction "but not the absurdity of FPS" [00:27:49].
- **FPPS as "an obfiscated cost"** (per caption) [00:27:49-00:28:21]: the sticker price is "like 1.5% or whatever it is", but what you receive per block is not "5% of that 3.125 minus the 1% sticker price" — the spread is hidden like "the airport currency conversion 0% commission, but 20% worse than the market somehow", or Bitcoin quoted at "$68,000" while the exchange charges "like $70,000". Wrap-up from the same panel: "don't use FPS unless you actually need it, which you don't" [00:32:52].

> The panel's framing: in every existing pool stack a submitted share becomes "a record in the mining pool database" — "essentially, they custody your hash rate" [00:00:33-00:01:06] — and minimum payout thresholds "lead to a custodial issue as you're waiting to get paid out" [00:08:55]. Unresolved nomenclature — the same speaker concedes there are "nomenclature disagreements in Bitcoin". - **Lightning payouts at scale are painful** [00:15:14-00:16:59]: "when Ocean finds a block, we just break the network" — "about five hours to get through all the thousand miners or something using lightning"; "every single time one of them fails, it locks up half of the network"; "We get 3.125 Bitcoin and we have to push a whole chunk of that into a bunch of lightning channels that we never get back" — "there's nothing worse for lightning than a bunch of fresh bitcoins going in one direction constantly". "We did insist on Bolt 12 and it was literally just core lightning that supported that and now loads of, you know, wallets support Bolt 12"; "L &D" (LND, per caption) "doesn't support bolt 12", so LND users "we still can't pay" — workarounds named were "spin up core lightning or all beehub or uh Phoenix is almost supported" [00:10:41-00:11:13]. Ocean's threshold is "around a million sats, nearest power of two to that", deliberately not user-configurable ("then you are basically turning yourself into a custodian"); a Bitaxe at "like 40 sats a day" will "probably just never" reach it [00:10:08-00:10:41].
- **ARC as the payout fix**: pool payouts are unidirectional, so bidirectional channels are "the wrong tool for the job" — "hammering in a nail with a wrench" [00:12:17-00:12:51]. ARC gives miners VTXOs they hold off-chain and grow payout by payout, broadcasting on-chain only at self-custody size and able to "exit the system without permission from anyone"; "It's an ideal tool for mining pool payouts. So, I'm very excited to uh explore this technology and build it and maybe we'll get a hydropool ocean" [00:12:51-00:14:38].
- **Share counting you can't verify** (Schnitzel, named by the moderator's prompt and the later demo hand-off): "except ocean, there is no other pool that actually can prove you that the shares that you sent them are actually counted" — "they can straight up lie to you" [00:20:49-00:21:55]; pools "don't report stale shares accurately because it scares miners. So Ocean reports that honestly" — which is why new Ocean miners see higher stale-share ratios [00:22:30]. Ocean's first-year numbers ("like 30% above a couple of FPS pools and we had like 96% luck") drew a "mathematical trick to lie to people" accusation from "Nick from Luxor" that "he never did" prove [00:22:30-00:23:01]. "Miners need to demand transparency from their pools. Otherwise, just assume you're getting scammed" [00:23:01].
- **E-hash as a market** [00:29:32-00:31:17]: self-custodied shares can be sold for dollars — e.g. a heater owner "not orange pill yet, they're just in it for the rebate" — opening "a KYC free um on-ramp"; buyers of e-hash tokens act as arbitrageurs who lock in the price and take a slice for assuming the risk — "It's called futures"... "or insurance"; and "Right now today only the largest mining pools get to price hash rate" — e-hash hands that pricing power to anyone, "kind of like Prometheus bringing fire down from the mountain".
- **What big 'pools' actually are** [00:31:49-00:32:19]: "Right now, you have FPS and Bitmain buying all of the world's hash rate, and they are the only two miners out there. They're not pools by any reasonable definition in my opinion. I think a pool is when multiple miners get together, combine their hash rate, and split the rewards" — instead, big pools "buy up all of the world's hash rate and lease their compute, turning miners into just basically hash rate service providers." The same speaker pays "a minor 2% more than they'd get just mining themselves" for rented hashrate [00:32:19]. Marketplace arbitrage against current FPPS: Braiins' "great marketplace called hash power", "Wrigley.io is another great one. I think flexos has that as well" (caption spellings) [00:32:52] — see [On-Demand Hashrate](../hashrate-market/on-demand-hashrate.md).

### Book canon: what pool capture actually costs (2022 book, Jan-2021 vintage)

The Handbook's 51%-attack chapter (ch. 32, published 2022) prices the two vectors behind the April-2024 debate above, starting from the distinction between "hashrate sold on an exchange or via cloud mining contracts as synthetic hashrate, and hashrate used to mine directly as physical hashrate" (p. 33) ([Braiins Mining Handbook ch. 32](../../raw/books/braiins-mining-handbook/04-cost-to-51-percent-attack.md)):

- **Physical hashrate is expensive.** At the January-2021 network size of 150 EH/s, matching it with the then-most-efficient ASIC (Antminer S19 Pro, 110 TH/s at 3250 W) takes "approximately 1.364M ASICs" — "~4.4 GW of power" — and "at a conservative unit price of $4000 each including power supply units, the cost of hardware would exceed $5.46 billion", with chip supply (Samsung, TSMC) the bottleneck (p. 35). The book's summary figure: a physical attack costs "~$5.5 billion as a conservative estimate at the time of writing", or coordinating seizure of farms is "an extremely difficult coordination problem likely involving 100+ operations" (p. 39).
- **Synthetic hashrate is nearly free — and that is the pool-trust problem.** "attacking with physical hashrate would cost at least $5 billion, whereas attacking with synthetic hashrate by taking over several pools would be essentially FREE", summarized as "simultaneously take over 3-5 mining pool operations" (pp. 37, 39) — Brammer's hidden-51%-behind-proxy-front-ends scenario, eight months before the Telegram debate. The book's mitigation is the community's: "miners can switch pools in a matter of seconds", so "it can be mitigated by ensuring that some miners would detect it within minutes and switch pools" (p. 37).
- **Bounds on the damage, per Mastering Bitcoin (2017):** "A consensus attack cannot steal bitcoin, spend bitcoin without signatures, redirect bitcoin, or otherwise change past transactions or ownership records"; a double-spend "can only be done on the attacker's own transactions"; and statistical modeling means an attack is viable "with as little as 30% of the hashing power" — the name overstates the bar (§10.12) ([Mastering Bitcoin ch. 10 extract](../../raw/books/mastering-bitcoin/ch10-mining-extract.md)). The same section names the managed-pool operator risk behind this page's template-censorship vector: "The pool operator in a managed pool controls the construction of candidate blocks and also controls which transactions are included".
- **Vintage stamps.** Pool concentration in the book is January 2021 — "~97% of Bitcoin's total network hashrate goes through Chinese pools" (p. 36) — and its NiceHash check found SHA-256 volume on hashrate markets "is not nearly large enough" to rent an attack (pp. 32–33).
