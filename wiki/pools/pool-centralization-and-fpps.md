## Overview

A pool decides two things that matter for Bitcoin beyond its own customers: **which transactions go into the block template** its miners hash on, and **who holds the reward** until it is paid out. Most hashrate today sits in a handful of pools paying Full Pay Per Share (FPPS), a scheme that pays a steady amount per share and therefore needs a large bitcoin reserve to ride out bad luck. Pools that cannot carry that reserve end up as proxies bankrolled by a larger pool — in 2025 reporting, mostly Antpool — so template control and custody concentrate further than the pool names on a block explorer suggest. This page collects the dated evidence for that picture (concentration statistics, proxy-pool evidence, invalid-job incidents), the 256 Foundation's editorial position, the POD256 hosts' running commentary (2025–2026) on FPPS, OCEAN and the Bitmain coinbase-output cap, and the tools that let anyone check what pools are doing. Every figure is a snapshot; read the dates.

For how FPPS, PPLNS and TIDES pay out — and why FPPS can still be the right call for an on/off heater — see [Pool Payout Schemes](pool-payout-schemes.md) and [Pool Choice for Heat Miners](pool-choice-for-heat-miners.md).

## Proxy pools: the Antpool network

- **Mechanism.** FPPS pays a steady amount per share while block finds are variable, so an FPPS pool needs a large balance sheet to absorb bad luck (the newsletter points to OrangeSurf's Pool Survival Analysis for the reserve math). Operators who cannot afford the reserves ask Antpool to bankroll them as a proxy ([newsletter, Jun 2025](../../raw/history/256f-newsletter/2025-06-19-you-know-i-m-something-of-a-decentralized-pool-myself.md)).
- **Pools named as absorbed** (newsletter, June 2025): Poolin, Braiins, Ultimus Pool, Binance Pool, SecPool, SigmaPool, Rawpool, Luxor, CloverPool (formerly BTC.com) and Mining Squared (same source). This is the newsletter's characterization, not a disclosure by those pools.
- **Template evidence (June 5, 2025).** boerst showed Luxor's template coinbase tag change from "Powered by Luxor Tech" to "Mined by AntPool"; the AntPool and Luxor templates were an exact 1:1 match, and his Bitaxe kept getting shares accepted by Luxor on the mangled template for several minutes. The newsletter called this confirmation that Luxor is an AntPool proxy, and asked how such tags can be trusted at all ([newsletter, Jul 2025](../../raw/history/256f-newsletter/2025-07-15-the-bigger-they-are-the-harder-they-fall.md)).
- **Why it matters (April 2024 Telegram debate).** Proxy front-ends can hide how much hashrate one entity really controls; the community consensus was that template censorship is the nearer-term risk than a 51% attack. See the Disputed block in [Pool Payout Schemes](pool-payout-schemes.md).
- **Pool exits feed the proxies (July 2026).** SBI Crypto (parent SBI Holdings, Japan; traditional FPPS) announced its pool would shut down on July 31 without a stated reason; Wilson Mining had proxied through it. SBI recommended Luxor, Braiins and Neopool to its miners — the POD256 hosts noted that, per stratum.work data, Braiins and others on that list are themselves AntPool proxies, so the shutdown likely moves hashrate further toward the same template ([POD256 e119](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-07-08-e119.md)).
- **Pool contracts lock in big miners (disputed).** Scott said he has heard that large public miners negotiate custom FPPS pool contracts rather than paying retail fees, which makes switching pools institutional; MARA runs its own pool. Bob Burnett has said large miners cannot switch pools on the fly, while an executive told Eco they would have a fiduciary duty to switch if bad things happened; Scott doubted such clauses cover censorship or pool size ([POD256 e092](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-10-29-e092.md)).

## The FPPS critique: custody and templates

- **Two problems driven by FPPS** (newsletter, June 2025): custody — a custodian can influence pool operators — and templates — the operator dictates which transactions miners hash on. Both raise censorship risk, and concentration raises 51% risk ([newsletter, Jun 2025](../../raw/history/256f-newsletter/2025-06-19-you-know-i-m-something-of-a-decentralized-pool-myself.md)).
- **Miner-built templates are not enough.** After Antpool announced (May 27, 2025) that miners could build their own templates while it kept FPPS payouts, the newsletter argued that a centralized pool still counting shares and coordinating payouts can be coerced into excluding shares built on non-conforming templates, or excluding a user's payout address; DATUM and SV2 are "good steps" but over-marketed, and "centralized pools will never decentralize Bitcoin mining" (same source).
- **SV2's limit.** Stratum V2 job negotiation lets miners propose templates the pool validates but cannot censor at the transaction level, yet the pool still controls the coinbase payout and share accounting ([newsletter, Apr 2026](../../raw/history/256f-newsletter/2026-04-15-assembling-freedom-24-bitcoin-mining-renaissance-stratum-v2.md)); the July 2025 issue called the SV2/DATUM decentralization emphasis overblown while centralized pools still do accounting and payouts ([newsletter, Jul 2025](../../raw/history/256f-newsletter/2025-07-15-the-bigger-they-are-the-harder-they-fall.md)).
- POD256 #112 (2026-04-15) described today's pool landscape as centralized around FPPS ([POD256 #112](../../raw/history/pod256/2026-04-15-pod256-112-stratum-v2-nonce-space-and-the-diy-miner-s-comeba.md)).
- **Share accounting is the choke point (hosts' view, 2025).** Scott argued that even with unlimited coinbase space, share accounting stays centralized on the pool's server, so authorities could compel a pool to stop paying a given miner, and changing addresses does not fix that ([POD256 e086](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-09-10-e086.md)). Eco made the matching point that DATUM and SV2 let miners select transactions but the pool still controls the coinbase and thus payouts; he sees a P2Pool v2 share chain as the next step ([POD256 e099](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-12-17-e099.md)). In April 2026 the hosts summed up: physically distributed home miners and template-selection pools help, but any pooled mining with a central share-collecting server is centralized, and there is no complete solution yet ([POD256 e112](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-04-15-e112.md)).
- **Eco's position on FPPS (opinion).** Eco said in September 2025 that FPPS is bad for Bitcoin and that he had moved off FPPS pools ([POD256 e083](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-09-03-e083.md)); in February 2026 he called FPPS a mess because it centralizes, and said the Foundation had moved its own donation hashrate to solo mining on its own Hydrapool instance to eat its own dog food ([POD256 e103](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-02-04-e103.md)). Earlier Foundation pool accounts had been mostly FPPS and paid out only a few hundred dollars over two years (same source).
- **Lottery vs variance (disputed).** Tyler argued all miners are lottery miners; Eco pushed back that FPPS pool miners are lowering variance, not lotto mining ([POD256 e088](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-09-24-e088.md)).
- **Large miners should solo.** Scott's view: EH/s-scale miners that solve blocks monthly should solo mine their own templates; MARA is likely the biggest that does ([POD256 e099](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-12-17-e099.md)). MARA solo mines with its own pool, which also lets it sell services such as its Slipstream private mempool that only large pools can offer ([POD256 e122](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-08-03-e122.md)).
- **Regulatory choke point.** Scott (September 2026): stopping Bitcoin mining would only require calling a short list of pools and an even shorter list of hardware manufacturers ([POD256 e127](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-09-28-e127.md)). Scott also argued that while there cannot be a million pools (there are not enough blocks), new pool designs are needed, and the hosts suggested pools be redefined as protocols rather than company offerings ([POD256 e117](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-17-e117.md)).
- **Bootstrapping problem.** Even shared PPLNS pools need very large hashrate pointed at them or users go without payouts; P2Pool v2 or novel payout schemes (vnprc working on futures markets for shares) could reduce variance ([POD256 e112](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-04-15-e112.md)). Miners fleeing a coerced pool to small self-hosted instances could go years without a block, which is the motivation for decentralized payout-split designs such as GridPool ([POD256 e116](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-10-e116.md)).
- **KYC pressure on payouts.** Iko expects merchants and processors to start rejecting UTXOs not traced to whitelisted exchanges; during the Telehash, Braiins' hashrate marketplace refused a payment from a UTXO that had recently been in Whirlpool — which he argues raises the importance of open-source home mining as a non-KYC source of coins ([POD256 e108](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-03-18-e108.md)).

> **Status: Disputed** — can a pool still censor miner-built templates?
> On the ESP-Miner SV2 issue, benjamin-wilson (Public Pool) argued that a pool can still censor proposed templates; TheBlueMatt countered that under the SV2 reference implementation a miner whose template is rejected falls back to another pool or solo mining, which turns censorship into a passive act, and miners doing custom job declaration broadcast the blocks they find themselves ([ESP-Miner #168](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2024-04-23-github-esp-miner-issue-168-adding-support-for-stratumv2-prot.md)). Best assessment: SV2 job declaration removes the pool's power to silently alter a template, but not its power to refuse shares or payouts — which is the 256 Foundation's point about accounting and custody. Agent P made the same critique of OCEAN in June 2026: decentralizing template construction is good, but payouts managed by a central company remain a choke point a government could pressure, for example by forbidding credit for shares that include censored transactions ([POD256 e116](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-10-e116.md)).

## Who steers Stratum V2 (2024-2026)

- The Working Group founded by Braiins and Spiral in 2022 formally expanded on 2026-05-07: "ANTPOOL, Block, DMND, F2Pool, Foundry, MARA Foundation and SpiderPool" joined ([SV2 WG new members, 2026-05-07](../../raw/protocols/sv2/2026-10-10-stratumprotocol-org-blog-new-members.md)). The blog's framing: keeping Stratum V2 "as a public, vendor-neutral specification" removes compatibility barriers, and the newcomers' "real-world experience will materially advance the protocol" (same source).
- Read as a decentralization signal, the people who run most production hashrate are now steering a miner-autonomy standard; read as a co-option question, the pools this page documents as FPPS operators and proxy principals (AntPool, Foundry, F2Pool — 23.7%, 20.3% and 16% of blocks in September 2026 above) now help steer the standard meant to check them. Both readings fit the same facts; the corpus does not settle it (see Contradictions & Open Questions).
- OCEAN is the standing dissent inside the pool world: it says it considered Sv2 but concluded "Sv2 wouldn't be a viable solution in the near term" and built DATUM from scratch — "the first fully decentralized mining protocol since 2017 when the last decentralized pool shut down", with Sv2 dismissed as "bolted onto the original centralized design" ([OCEAN — Origins of DATUM, 2024-09-29](../../raw/pools/ocean/2026-10-10-ocean-xyz-docs-datum.md)). That dissent carries weight because OCEAN ships non-custodial payouts at scale (21.49 Eh/s self-reported, 2026-10-10) without Sv2.
- Production signal: the reference implementation cut four releases between May and September 2026 (v1.9.0 → v1.12.0, the latter on 2026-09-17 with a hardening pass over share validation, bounded job storage and BIP323 version-rolling) ([SRI v1.12.0](../../raw/protocols/sv2/2026-10-10-github-sv2-stratum-release-v1.12.0.md)), and its maintainers report the rebuilt Job Declarator Client "tested them in production with real ASICs and firmware", naming Braiins and DMND as collaborators ([SRI roadmap 2026](../../raw/protocols/sv2/2026-10-10-stratumprotocol-org-blog-sri-roadmap-2026.md)). The stratumprotocol.org adoption list (2026-10-10) counts Blitzpool, CKPool, Public Pool, NexusPool and PyBlock as production SV2 solo pools, Braiins Pool and DMND as production pools — DMND "enabling miner selected templates" — with the SRI Community Pool still testing-only ([stratumprotocol.org home](../../raw/protocols/sv2/2026-10-10-stratumprotocol-org-home.md)).

## OCEAN: custody, TIDES and DATUM (contested)

OCEAN is the most-discussed non-FPPS pool on POD256 and the hosts disagree about it among themselves. Positions below are opinions as stated on air, dated by episode.

- **Custody.** Eco said in September 2025 that his OCEAN payouts did not come from a coinbase reward, implying OCEAN custodies and disburses like other pools despite its non-custodial marketing ([POD256 e083](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-09-03-e083.md)); he added that OCEAN never solved the Antminer coinbase-output limit, so smaller miners are paid from addresses controlled by OCEAN or its custodian, while OCEAN supporters on Twitter claimed payouts are non-custodial ([POD256 e086](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-09-10-e086.md); repeated on [POD256 e093](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-05-e093.md)). OCEAN's explanation, as relayed to Eco: block rewards go to a wallet OCEAN controls and payouts are issued automatically once the 100-block maturity is reached; Average Gary countered that whoever holds the keys holds the coins ([POD256 e090](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-10-15-e090.md)).
- **"Decentralized" marketing.** In December 2025 Scott and Eco called OCEAN's decentralization marketing wrong (closed-source share accounting controlled by a select group) and noted Luke reportedly considers OCEAN at 90% of network hashrate acceptable; Tyler countered that any new non-incumbent pool is decentralizing and that OCEAN's beta API let him verify his shares. Eco had still used OCEAN as the only non-FPPS PPLNS option before Parasite Pool existed ([POD256 e099](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-12-17-e099.md)). Eco also objected to OCEAN celebrating DATUM-miner block finds as if they were solo finds, since a pool finder receives only its share-based sats rather than the full reward a solo miner keeps ([POD256 e080](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-08-01-e080.md)); a NerdQaxe user who found a block on OCEAN received only his normal share payout of about 42 sats ([POD256 e090](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-10-15-e090.md)).
- **TIDES window and storage.** Per OCEAN documentation as read by Eco, the TIDES window is current network difficulty multiplied by eight, so a difficulty rise can reach back to older shares and OCEAN stores every share it has ever validated — a large hardware burden that raises the bar for pool operators ([POD256 e090](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-10-15-e090.md); [POD256 e087](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-09-17-e087.md)). For a temporary hashrate event, payment depends on OCEAN finding blocks while the event's shares are still in the window — similar to old Slush Pool PPLNS variance ([POD256 e094](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-12-e094.md)). Average Gary's understanding (at his last review) was that TIDES does not account for block-template fees, whereas DEMAND's SliceJD PPLNS variant weights fee revenue by each miner's template fees ([POD256 e090](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-10-15-e090.md)).
- **TIDES vs FPPS field test.** Tyler split an S9 fifty-fifty between OCEAN (TIDES) and an FPPS pool for over two months; OCEAN was behind by about 3% in sats paid, having started during a block-luck lull ([POD256 e083](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-09-03-e083.md)).
- **Share verification.** OCEAN does offer share-verification data via API, but not publicly — you have to ask; the hosts verified their shares that way ([POD256 e090](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-10-15-e090.md); [POD256 e094](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-12-e094.md)).
- **Server source.** DATUM Prime (OCEAN's server side) is not open source, so you cannot run your own DATUM-based pool; someone reverse-engineered the DATUM protocol into a beta open server ([POD256 e087](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-09-17-e087.md)), and Agent P's GridPool now acts as a DATUM-compatible server any DATUM miner can switch to, though it does no share accounting ([POD256 e116](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-10-e116.md)). Eco argued OCEAN must open-source its server because it descends from the AGPL Eligius pool; Scott noted Luke as copyright holder may not be bound (disputed, [POD256 e086](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-09-10-e086.md)).
- **Operations.** OCEAN has a high minimum share difficulty, so low-hash Bitaxes submit shares rarely and can show as offline while hashing ([POD256 e082](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-08-27-e082.md)); OCEAN's terms prohibit reselling, yet people point at others' DATUM gateways because the fee drops from 2% to 1% ([POD256 e086](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-09-10-e086.md)); OCEAN's terms of service are revocable despite there being no accounts ([POD256 e105](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-02-18-e105.md)); at launch OCEAN's template filter rejected Samourai Whirlpool TX0 transactions ([POD256 e119](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-07-08-e119.md)); and a large miner arriving via NiceHash exposed bugs at OCEAN and payouts were temporarily suspended, while OCEAN said no work was lost ([POD256 e083](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-09-03-e083.md)).
- **BIP 110 default endpoint (August 2026).** OCEAN made its default non-DATUM stratum endpoint a BIP 110 node and launched a separate non-BIP 110 endpoint users had to switch to manually; the hosts called this a scummy default ([POD256 e122](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-08-03-e122.md)). OCEAN reportedly offered a refund for eighteen hours of redirected hashrate (the hosts doubted the math); the change applied only to Stratum v1 endpoints, not DATUM ([POD256 e123](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-08-10-e123.md)). Luke Dashjr then stepped down as OCEAN's CTO ([POD256 e124](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-08-17-e124.md)). OCEAN's dashboard claims 12,935 blocks found, which the hosts say counts blocks from its predecessor pool (disputed framing, [POD256 e122](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-08-03-e122.md)).
- **OCEAN's own docs (verbatim, captured 2026-10-10).** The DATUM origins page (2024-09-29) states the anti-Sv2 stance outright: OCEAN considered Sv2 but concluded "Sv2 wouldn't be a viable solution in the near term", built DATUM "from the ground up", and bills it as "the first fully decentralized mining protocol since 2017 when the last decentralized pool shut down" ([OCEAN — Origins of DATUM](../../raw/pools/ocean/2026-10-10-ocean-xyz-docs-datum.md)). The non-custodial claim, verbatim: "coinbase payouts go directly to miners, instantaneously and without custodial oversight", and the adoption carrot is that "DATUM miners receive a 50% discount on the OCEAN pool fee" (same source) — the pool's own confirmation of the fee mechanism the hosts describe above. Template centralization inside OCEAN ended on a dated event: "We decommissioned our alternate template options on December 21st, 2025" (2025-12-21), so miners wanting other policies must run DATUM ([OCEAN — alternate templates](../../raw/pools/ocean/2026-10-10-ocean-xyz-docs-alternate-templates.md)). Pool status at capture: 21.49 Eh/s, last block 970835 ([OCEAN docs index](../../raw/pools/ocean/2026-10-10-ocean-xyz-docs-index.md)).
- **TIDES accounting, from the pool's own doc.** The TIDES technical page (2024-02-29) confirms the mechanics Eco read on air: the share-log window is eight blocks' worth of work and shares "are never removed from the share log" (kept "indefinitely"), a windowed share has a 99.9665% chance of being rewarded at least once, and TIDES "is intended to be implemented in a non-custodial fashion" ([OCEAN — TIDES doc](../../raw/pools/ocean/2026-10-10-ocean-xyz-docs-tides.md)). It also concedes the hosts' centralization critique in the pool's own words: "TIDES, by nature of how the share log works, cannot itself be decentralized, sadly" (same source). On Average Gary's fee point: the doc says the full block reward including fees is split proportionally to work in the window — "Miners get their fair split of the transaction fees as well as the block’s subsidy" — but nothing weights fees by whose template produced them, which matches his claim that per-template fee weighting is the DEMAND SliceJD differentiator (same source; his reading was "at his last review"). Small earnings may be accrued "until the sum exceeds a minimum threshold" — the docs' own gap between non-custodial rewards and custodial-looking payouts (same source).

Best assessment: both sides agree OCEAN's share accounting and payout custody are centralized; the disagreement is about weighting that against FPPS concentration.
> Sub-dispute, docs vs podcast, on the non-custodial claim: OCEAN's DATUM page promises "coinbase payouts go directly to miners, instantaneously and without custodial oversight" ([OCEAN — Origins of DATUM](../../raw/pools/ocean/2026-10-10-ocean-xyz-docs-datum.md)), while Eco's on-air observations (above, e083/e086/e090) were payouts arriving from OCEAN-controlled addresses after the 100-block maturity. Unreconciled as of 2026-10-10; what is verbatim on both sides is that the share log "cannot itself be decentralized, sadly" (OCEAN's doc) and that OCEAN's accounting server is closed (hosts' claim, disputed by Luke's copyright position).

## The Bitmain coinbase-output cap

Paying many miners directly from the coinbase is the obvious non-custodial alternative to FPPS, and stock Antminer firmware is the practical obstacle the POD256 hosts keep returning to.

- **What the limit is.** Scott: stock Antminer firmware's Stratum parsing cannot handle long coinbase transactions — this is not a Bitcoin rule. FPPS pools like AntPool use one or two coinbase outputs, so Bitmain has no incentive to fix it ([POD256 e093](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-05-e093.md)). The number is quoted loosely: roughly fifteen or sixteen addresses ([POD256 e090](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-10-15-e090.md)); about 16 coinbase outputs ([POD256 e086](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-09-10-e086.md)); somewhere around 16 to 20 ([POD256 e094](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-12-e094.md)); about 15 or 16 at other pools ([POD256 e101](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-01-14-e101.md)); roughly 15 ([POD256 e112](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-04-15-e112.md)). No transcript gives a tested figure.
- **History.** Eco recounted that Laurentia Pool (Con Kolivas with the MineFarmBuy operator) tried paying directly from the coinbase but stalled on this limit ([POD256 e086](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-09-10-e086.md)), and the hosts believed OCEAN's precursor also tried all-coinbase payouts ([POD256 e093](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-05-e093.md)).
- **Consequences.** Coinbase size is bounded in theory only by block size, but more outputs displace fee-paying transactions ([POD256 e094](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-11-12-e094.md)); GridPool's 300 payout slots were a trade-off between finer payouts and block space, and DATUM carries complex code to fingerprint miners and size coinbases to what their firmware can handle ([POD256 e116](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-06-10-e116.md)). Small daily payouts for a home heater cannot come directly from the coinbase at all; they need Lightning or other custodial or novel schemes (same source). The hosts questioned how Blitz Pool, a zero-fee SV2 solo pool planning DATUM-style coinbase PPLNS, will handle the Antminer limit ([POD256 e113](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-04-22-e113.md)).
- **Hydrapool's answer.** Hydrapool defaults to 100 coinbase payouts and deliberately does not cater to Bitmain's limit; the hosts expect a stock Antminer receiving such work to fail or produce invalid coinbases ([POD256 e112](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-04-15-e112.md)). See [Hydrapool](hydrapool.md).
- **Published budgets from live non-custodial pools (As-Of 2026-10-10).** Blitz Pool — the pool the hosts questioned above — now publishes its coinbase math on its API: payouts capped at 198 miner outputs max inside a 35,000 WU coinbase weight budget (5,000 sats min payout), with "Stratum V2 is auto-negotiated on the same port" and a worker base of 769 connections that is Bitaxe/NerdQAxe-class — only 8 Antminer connections (~164 TH/s) — so the budget sidesteps rather than solves the stock-firmware limit ([Blitz Pool site + API](../../raw/protocols/p2p-pools/2026-10-10-blitzpool-site-home.md)). P2Poolv2 takes the same fight to node policy: its README tells operators to raise `blockmaxweight` to 3930000 to make room for 500 coinbase outputs ("can start running P2Poolv2 with direct payments from coinbase") ([P2Poolv2 README](../../raw/protocols/p2p-pools/2026-10-10-github-pool2win-p2pool-v2-readme.md)). Both confirm the hosts' framing that the cap is software policy, not consensus — and that pools serious about coinbase payouts simply publish around it.

## Transparency tools

- **stratum.work** (boerst) connects to pools and visualizes their live jobs ([POD256 #048](../../raw/history/pod256/2024-06-12-pod256-048-decentralizing-bitcoin-mining-insights-from-boers.md)). April 26 (2025): a per-pool block-template detail page, including OP_RETURN data and merge mining such as RSK ([newsletter, May 2025](../../raw/history/256f-newsletter/2025-05-05-bitcoin-mining-will-not-be-decentralized-until-it-is-open-so.md)). July 11 (2025): a Sankey diagram tracing how pools assemble templates through shared Merkle branches ([newsletter, Aug 2025](../../raw/history/256f-newsletter/2025-08-18-is-open-source-communism.md)). August 26 (2025): an events panel for forks and invalid templates ([newsletter, Sep 2025](../../raw/history/256f-newsletter/2025-09-25-rig-bitcoin-mining-re-imagined.md)).
- **Coinbase tags on your own miner.** By 2025-08-20 ESP-Miner logged the block height and miner tag from each job (e.g. `ckpooleu/solo.ckpool.org/`), so a Bitaxe owner can see whose template they are hashing ([ESP-Miner #1011](../../raw/firmware/esp-miner/2025-06-06-github-esp-miner-issue-1011-show-block-header-information-in.md)). Tags are self-declared — the Luxor incident shows they can be wrong.
- **Pool checker.** Scott vibe-coded a pool checker that decodes the coinbase in the work a pool sends and shows each payout address and amount; the feature was being added to AxeOS so Bitaxe users solo mining can verify their coinbase on the dashboard ([POD256 e099](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-12-17-e099.md)). With pooled mining your address is not in the coinbase, so you still have to trust the pool (same source).
- **HashScope.** A man-in-the-middle Stratum proxy from the 256 Foundation that logs every miner–pool message, runnable in Docker on a laptop with a Bitaxe pointed at it ([POD256 e107](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-03-11-e107.md)); see [HashScope](../mining-software/hashscope.md).
- **What pools see.** Pools receive each miner's user agent (hardware and firmware version) on connect; OCEAN says it does not save it ([POD256 e092](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-10-29-e092.md)).
- **Auditable share accounting.** Hydrapool exposes its PPLNS shares through an API so miners can check payouts instead of trusting the operator ([hydrapool.org](../../raw/pools/hydrapool/hydrapool-org-home.md)); see [Hydrapool](hydrapool.md) for the pool-signature and mempool.space attribution debate. The hosts named the core trust problem: miners cannot easily tell whether a pool inflates the share supply, and many small local pools reduce lock-in so miners can leave a cheating pool ([POD256 e090](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-10-15-e090.md)); Scott noted no existing firmware lets users audit the shares they sent and their difficulty ([POD256 e087](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-09-17-e087.md)).
- **On-chain checks.** How to decode a block's coinbase tag yourself: [Solo Blocks Found by Open-Source & Home Miners](../history/open-source-solo-blocks.md). Hosted pools like CKPool and Public Pool show worker names and hashrate, which is how small-miner solo blocks get attributed; self-hosted finders reveal only what they choose ([POD256 e105](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2026/2026-02-18-e105.md)).

## What a home miner can do

1. **Solo with your own template:** self-hosted [Public Pool](public-pool.md) or Solo CKPool-style software on your own node.
2. **Non-custodial pooled payouts:** run or join a [Hydrapool](hydrapool.md) instance (coinbase payouts, auditable shares), or use designs from [Decentralized Pool Designs](../protocols/decentralized-pool-designs.md). Mind the Antminer coinbase-output cap above.
3. **Check what you hash:** read the miner tag in your firmware logs, decode the coinbase with a pool checker, and spot-check pools on stratum.work.
4. **Encrypt the connection:** prefer SV2 or TLS stratum where offered to avoid hashrate hijacking — see [Stratum in Practice](../protocols/stratum-in-practice.md).
5. **Consider coinbase privacy:** Average Gary floated rolling a fresh payout address per block so blocks become unattributable on mempool.space — a future idea, not yet implemented ([POD256 e090](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-10-15-e090.md)).

## Contradictions & Open Questions

- **Exact Antminer output limit.** Quoted on air as anywhere from about fifteen to 20 outputs; no tested figure (see the Bitmain coinbase-output cap section).
- **OCEAN custody and value.** See the OCEAN section and its Disputed block.
- **Pool contracts.** Whether large miners' pool contracts would let them leave a censoring pool is unknown ([POD256 e092](../../raw/history/pod256/transcripts/2026-10-07/transcripts/2025/2025-10-29-e092.md)).
- **SV2 governance: adoption signal or co-option?** The Working Group that began as Braiins + Spiral now includes AntPool, Foundry, F2Pool, SpiderPool, Block, MARA Foundation and DMND (2026-05-07) — the same large pools this page documents as FPPS operators and proxy principals. Big-pool stewardship could accelerate the standard's deployment or dilute the miner-autonomy parts (job declaration, miner-selected templates); the corpus has both readings and no test. OCEAN's exit from the process entirely — rejecting Sv2 and building DATUM — is the counterfactual datapoint ([SV2 WG new members](../../raw/protocols/sv2/2026-10-10-stratumprotocol-org-blog-new-members.md); [OCEAN — Origins of DATUM](../../raw/pools/ocean/2026-10-10-ocean-xyz-docs-datum.md)).
- **Non-custodial alternatives remain rounding errors.** The live non-custodial datapoints as of 2026-10-10 are small against the FPPS incumbents: OCEAN self-reports 21.49 Eh/s (~2% of network), Blitz Pool runs ~2.74 PH/s on 769 hobbyist workers, and the decentralized designs (Braidpool pre-testnet, P2Poolv2 on testnet4) have zero production hashrate ([OCEAN docs index](../../raw/pools/ocean/2026-10-10-ocean-xyz-docs-index.md); [Blitz Pool site + API](../../raw/protocols/p2p-pools/2026-10-10-blitzpool-site-home.md); [p2p-pools capture report](../../raw/protocols/p2p-pools/_REPORT.md)). Whether any of them can bootstrap to exashash scale without the custody trade-offs they reject is the open question behind the whole page.
